### About My Research

I am a Ph.D. candidate supervised by Prof. Simone Paolo Ponzetto and Prof. Goran Glavaš, focusing on neural methods for natural language understanding. My research centers on two main areas: **Argument Mining** and **Language Representations**.

**Argument Mining:**
The goal here is to enable machines to understand human argumentation. This involves complex tasks such as identifying argumentative units (e.g., claims, premises) and the relationships between them (e.g., attack, support), as well as predicting the overall argumentative quality of texts.

**Scientific Publication Mining (Scitorics):**
Since scientific publications can be viewed as arguments themselves, I also work on solving problems in scientific publication mining (in collaboration with Prof. Eckert at Stuttgart Media University). My aim is to gain a deeper insight into the argumentative nature of scientific writing by developing state-of-the-art methods for analyzing and exploring the rhetorical aspects of scientific publications—a field we call 'scitorics'.

**Language Representations & Ethics:**
A key component of computationally analyzing argumentation is utilizing high-quality language representations. Therefore, I specialize in this area, for instance, by injecting linguistic or common-sense knowledge into text encoder models. Furthermore, I investigate the ethical dimensions of text representations, focusing on detecting unfair biases such as sexism and racism in models and developing corresponding debiasing algorithms.
