# Advanced Methods in Text Analytics (IE 696)

This Master's level course serves as an advanced follow-up to [IE 661 Text Analytics](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/en/information-systems/chairs/prof-dr-strohmaier/teaching/master-level-courses/ie-661-is-661-text-analytics/), which introduces basic concepts and methods for Natural Language Processing (NLP).

The course dives deep into the latest state-of-the-art methods for NLP, focusing heavily on language models implemented using deep learning architectures. Key topics include:

- The transformer architecture and its applications.
- The design, training, and applications of Large Language Models (LLMs), including the technology behind products like ChatGPT.

**Important Note:** This course focuses on *how LLMs work internally*, rather than how to use them in applied settings. Hands-on exercises are designed for the training and evaluation of LLMs, not for practical application. For learning applied use cases, students are advised to consider [Large Language Models and Agents](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/dws/teaching/course-details/courses-for-master-candidates/ie-685-large-language-models-and-agents/).

## Prerequisites

Students should possess:

- BSc-level knowledge in linear algebra and probability theory, along with basic calculus.
- It is strongly recommended to have completed one of the following courses: [Machine Learning](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/dws/teaching/course-details/courses-for-master-candidates/ie-675b-machine-learning/) or [Deep Learning](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/dws/teaching/course-details/courses-for-master-candidates/course-archive/fss-2025/ie-678-deep-learning/).

## References

The advanced material draws from recent research papers referenced in the course materials. The foundational concepts are based on the following textbooks:

- *Speech and Language Processing: An Introduction to Natural Language Processing, Computational Linguistics and Speech Recognition*, 3rd Edition, 2026 by Dan Jurafsky and James H. Martin (author draft available [here](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/)).
- *Natural Language Processing* by Yue Zhang and Zhiyang Teng, 2021.
- *Natural Language Processing* by Jacob Eisenstein, 2018.
