# SM 459: Seminar on Explainable AI Methods (HWS 2026)

This seminar provides an in-depth exploration of classical attribution methods within Explainable AI (XAI). The course focuses on techniques such as saliency maps, feature importance, and gradient-based explanations, analyzing how these methods help interpret and analyze the decision-making processes of machine learning models.

## Seminar Details

- **Organization:** Prof. Dr.-Ing. Margret Keuper
- **Target Audience:** Available for Bachelor Students
- **Prerequisites:** Basic understanding in Machine Learning
- **Capacity:** Maximum of 6 participants

## Learning Goals

Upon completion of this seminar, students will be able to:

- Read, understand, and discuss a basic topic relevant within computer vision.
- Summarize the chosen topic in a concise report (10 single-column pages + references).
- Deliver two presentations on the topic: a 3-minute flash presentation and a 15-minute presentation.
- Moderate a scientific discussion regarding a fellow student's topic.
- Review a draft report submitted by a fellow student.

## Seminar Topics and Research Focus

Each student will work on a specific topic within the seminar's scope, accompanied by a reference paper. The presentation and report must explore the topic using the reference paper as a foundation, but must not be limited solely to the paper's content.

**Example Reference Papers:**

1. **Grad-CAM:** [Visual Explanations from Deep Networks via Gradient-based Localization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02391)
1. **Visualizing and Understanding Convolutional Networks:** [Visualizing and Understanding Convolutional Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2901)
1. **This Looks Like That:** [Deep Learning for Interpretable Image Recognition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10574)
1. **LRP:** [On Pixel-Wise Explanations for Non-Linear Classifier Decisions by Layer-Wise Relevance Propagation](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0130140)
1. **Score-CAM:** [Score-Weighted Visual Explanations for Convolutional Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01279)
1. **Cos-Networks:** [Alignment is All We Need for Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10268)
