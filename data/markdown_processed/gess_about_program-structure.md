# Program Structure GESS

The teaching and training concept of the GESS is designed to guide doctoral students from active learners toward becoming independent researchers.

## Course Phase

During the initial course phase, graduate students complete up to two years of coursework. This phase is complemented by small group exercises, tutorials, and dedicated research training, culminating in examinations.

### Core Course Program

The mandatory core courses cover essential topics across all three doctoral centers, including research design, statistical modeling, and methods of causal inference.

### Field Course Program

Specialized field courses introduce students to the practical application of methodology within their chosen research area. The interdisciplinary nature of the Graduate School is emphasized throughout both stages of the course phase.

*Progression:* Advancement to the thesis writing phase is determined by the Admissions and Assessments Committee of the respective doctoral center, based on performance in examinations and the quality of the first- or second-year paper.

## Writing Phase

In the thesis writing phase, doctoral students work on their research projects under the close supervision of dedicated faculty members. This stage focuses on transitioning from guided research execution to the independent development of research ideas. Students are expected to write research papers, present findings in seminars and colloquia, and submit their work to international conferences and journals.

Students regularly present their research progress to supervisors and peers in area-specific and interdisciplinary doctoral seminars. Additional opportunities are provided to encourage cross-field work, participation in international conferences and summer schools, and integration into the international research network.

## Placement Phase

The final placement phase provides graduate students with comprehensive information and training for the academic (and non-academic) job market. Each doctoral center appoints 'placement officers' to assist students in preparing their job market packages. Practical support includes mock interviews with faculty members and practice job talks, offering valuable feedback for a successful job market experience. Recent placements of graduates include roles as assistant professors and post-doctoral fellows in top departments, as well as positions in international organizations, public institutions, and the private sector.
