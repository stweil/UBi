# Information Systems (IS) Research Overview

Information Systems support organizations by providing the necessary information for better or faster decision-making. They are crucial enablers of new business processes and business models, fundamentally altering the competitive landscape of any company. The discipline utilizes Information and Communication Technologies (ICT) across various user roles. The nature of Information Systems is inherently both theoretical and technological.

- **Theoretical-Empirical Contributions:** Focus on the management and utilization of Information Systems and Information Infrastructures within organizations.
- **Technological-Constructivist Contributions:** Aim at the design of Information Systems specifically to facilitate new business processes and models.

## Core Research Areas

The research approach is complementary, combining both theoretic-empirical and technological-constructivist perspectives. The major fields of research are grouped as follows:

- Development of Information Systems and Infrastructures
- Innovative Information Systems
- Management of Information Systems and Infrastructures
- Electronic Business and Government

### 1. System Development and Engineering

This area covers the practical aspects of building and improving IS. Key themes include:

- **Software Product Management** and **Usability Engineering:** Investigating development processes with a specific focus on customer and end-user integration.
- **Component-based Software Development** and **Object Data Management:** Providing instruments to design and develop specific business functions at the application layer, alongside managing underlying data.
- **Development Processes:** Researching the productivity and impact of IS and Infrastructure development processes to improve collaboration activities.

### 2. Innovative and Advanced Systems

This domain focuses on designing and evaluating new system concepts and solutions for complex organizational needs.

- **Distributed Systems** and **Context-Aware Computing:** Laying the foundation for inter-organizational Information Systems and Infrastructures that facilitate new value chain forms.
- **Middleware Layer:** Enabling a modular and flexible design for contemporary application functions.
- **Enterprise Information Systems (EIS):** These systems, which rely on enterprise software packages (e.g., Enterprise Resource Planning, Customer Relationship Management, Business Intelligence), form the backbone—and sometimes the differentiator—for high-performing corporations. Research here adopts an end-user perspective, focusing on social augmentation, information security, and flexibility.

### 3. Management and Governance

This area explores the full potential of IS objects by focusing on strategic alignment and governance.

- **Information Management** and **Business Process Management (BPM):** Investigations are conducted to determine the "fit" between tasks, processes, technologies, and people.
- **IS Governance and IS Sourcing:** Scrutinizing complex arrangements at the infrastructure level.
- **Business Value of Information Systems:** Investigating how IS can be effectively deployed to support a corporation's value creation.

### 4. Application Domains

Information Systems and infrastructures are key enablers for modern operational models:

- **Electronic Business (E-Business)** and **Electronic Government (E-Government):** Facilitating new forms of digital commerce and public service delivery.
- **Behavioral Research:** Investigating the key drivers for efficient utilization of these systems, including the adoption of system standards, electronic business models, cloud computing, process virtualization, online pricing, and smart metering solutions.
