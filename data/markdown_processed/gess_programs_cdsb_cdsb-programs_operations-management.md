# Operations Management Research Areas

The Operations Management Area encompasses research across four renowned chairs: [Bode](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/en/bode/), [Fleischmann](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/en/fleischmann/), [Schön](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/en/schoen/), and [Stolletz](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/en/stolletz/). These chairs cover a broad spectrum of topics, ranging from procurement and production to supply chain and service operations management.

While each chair maintains its individual specialization, the overall curriculum is integrated. A core methodological focus is **Operations Research (OR)**, which utilizes analytical models and tools to systematically address complex decision-making scenarios involving trade-offs across costs, profits, and performance measures. The faculty is dedicated to producing high-quality, practically relevant research, regularly publishing in top journals such as *Journal of Operations Management* and *Management Science*.

## Core Research Disciplines

The research activities are structured across several key, interconnected fields:

### Procurement

Research in this area focuses on understanding, describing, explaining, and predicting phenomena critical to procurement. Current research thrusts include:

- Risk and disruptions
- Interfirm relationships
- Innovation and entrepreneurship
- Sustainability
- Strategies and performance

A key focus is on research questions situated at the intersection of procurement and related disciplines (e.g., logistics, marketing, management), requiring the integration of multiple theoretical perspectives.

### Logistics and Supply Chain Management

This domain covers managerial decisions related to the design and operation of the supply chain in a broad sense. The goal is to maximize overall performance through:

- Optimal resource deployment
- Process optimization
- Coordination of individual processes
- Key processes include transportation and inventory management.

The research approach combines scientific rigor with managerial relevance, relying heavily on quantitative modeling and operations research techniques.

### Service Operations

Research in service operations is concerned with developing quantitative OR models and methods to provide decision support for practical problems in service management. Current areas of focus include:

- Revenue management
- Service design
- Sustainable (Service) Operations Management and Green Product Design
- Strategic pricing and tariff design

### Production Management

This chair's research encompasses various managerial decisions within production and operations management. Current projects emphasize quantitative decision support for designing and managing production systems, such as:

- Operations scheduling
- Management of dynamic systems
- Design of lean operations systems

Application areas are diverse, including assemble-to-order (ATO) systems, automotive flow lines, airport operations, and call center operations. Techniques applied include deterministic optimization, performance analysis of queueing systems, and optimization of stochastic systems.
