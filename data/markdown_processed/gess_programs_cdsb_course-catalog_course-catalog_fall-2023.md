## Fall 2023 Course Offerings

### Accounting & Taxation Research Seminar

This seminar focuses on current research topics in accounting and taxation. Visiting researchers present their latest working papers, and the discussions are structured like leading scientific conferences. The course deepens students’ insights into various research methods popular in empirical and theoretical research.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Students will learn to follow up with and discuss current research topics in accounting and taxation.
- Interaction with leading researchers helps develop own research ideas and provides insight into research design, execution, and presentation.

**Assessment:**

- Participation (20%)
- Paper presentations and discussions (80%)

**Schedule:**

- **Lecture:** 04.09.23 – 04.12.23 (Monday, 17:15 – 18:45 | O 129)

**Seminar Dates:** Seminar dates are announced [here](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/en/area-accounting-taxation/research/research-seminar/).

### Econometrics: Applied Methodology

This course provides an applied introduction to the methodology used in empirical research literature. Main topics include Ordinary Least Squares, instrumental variables estimation, and panel data econometrics.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Students can apply econometric methods commonly used in economic research.
- Special attention is given to interpreting empirical results and understanding potential caveats of different approaches.

**Assessment:**

- Oral exam (10 minutes): 50%
- Class Participation: 50%

**Schedule:**

- **Lecture:** 04.09.23 – 04.12.23 (Monday, 08:30 – 10:00 | O226–28)

### Microeconomic Theory (Graduate Level)

This course introduces microeconomic theory at a graduate level, focusing on aspects relevant to business research.

**Main Topics:**

- Theory of consumer choice (certainty and uncertainty)
- Theory of the firm, production cost, and supply
- Markets, equilibrium, welfare
- Strategic behavior (complete and incomplete information)
- Incentives and asymmetric information

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Equips doctoral students to read and discuss economic theory.
- Provides tools to formulate original research based on theoretically grounded hypotheses.

**Assessment:**

- Midterm and final exams: 80–100%
- Exercises: Up to 20%

### Mathematics for Economists (Advanced)

This course covers fundamental mathematical concepts necessary for advanced economic analysis.

**Topics Covered:**

- Chapter 1: Sets, functions, and relations.
- Chapter 2: Metric and normed spaces, convergence, continuity, compact sets.
- Chapter 3: Vector spaces, matrix algebra, linear transformations, eigenvalues.
- Chapter 4: Multivariate differentiability and constrained/unconstrained optimization.

**Competences Acquired:**

- Students master basic concepts of analysis and linear algebra.
- They can develop rigorous mathematical proofs and solve nonlinear optimization problems.

**Assessment:**

- Exam (120 min)

**Schedule:**

- **Lecture:** 04.09.23 – 25.09.23 (Monday, 10:15 – 11:45 | L7, 3–5 Room 001)
