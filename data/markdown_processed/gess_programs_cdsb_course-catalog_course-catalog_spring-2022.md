## 📊 Forschungsmethodik und Quantitative Analyse

### Systematic Review (PRISMA)

**Course Content:**
This course introduces the method of systematic review according to the PRISMA statement (Liberati et al. 2009). Students apply the PRISMA statement to a research review question from their PhD thesis, culminating in a systematic review paper. Concepts and heuristics of qualitative and quantitative meta-analysis are covered, allowing students to critically reflect on the methodologies used in existing research reviews.
**Learning Outcomes:**

- Students will be able to conduct a systematic review.
- Students will understand meta-analysis concepts.
- Students will critically evaluate research methodologies.
  **Form of Assessment:**
- Class Participation: 10 %
- Presentation: 10 %
- Discussion: 10 %
- Assignment: 70 %
  **Schedule:**
- Lecture: 15.02.22 – 31.05.22 | Tuesday | 13:45 – 15:15 | L 4, 1, room 004

### Textual Analysis (Python/NLP)

**Course Content:**
Students learn how textual analysis methods work and how to implement them using Python. The course covers:

1. **Textual Analysis Methods:** Discussion of prominent papers, including methods like bag-of-words and basic machine learning (e.g., Naïve Bayes).
1. **Text Databases:** Detailed introduction to frequently used databases (e.g., EDGAR, LexisNexis).
1. **Implementation in Python:** Using Python to automatically retrieve data from text databases and the internet. Techniques include text editing, information extraction, dictionary-based textual analyses, and analyzing text characteristics (e.g., language complexity, document similarity).
   *Note: No prior Python knowledge is required.*
   **Learning Outcomes:**

- Implement state-of-the-art research methods for analyzing verbal information in accounting, finance, and economics.
- Incorporate research methods from computer linguistics.
- Obtain solid programming knowledge in Python.
  **Form of Assessment:**
- Assignment

### Ökonometrie und Kausale Inferenz

**Course Content:**
This course provides a refresher on econometric concepts such as endogeneity, multicollinearity, and selection bias, with a strong focus on causal inference. Methods discussed include discrete choice modeling, instrumental variable regressions, regression discontinuity, difference-in-difference, matching, and panel econometrics. Empirical applications from finance are critically discussed.
**Learning Outcomes:**

- Knowledge of several econometric concepts and their applications in finance.
- Ability to plan and carry out independent empirical research.

### Behavioral Finance

**Course Content:**
Behavioral Finance examines how individual attitudes and behavior affect financial decisions, challenging the standard rational investor paradigm. The course reviews recent research on mispricing due to psychological biases, covering topics like overconfidence, prospect theory, and heuristic-driven biases.
**Learning Outcomes:**

- Understand how behavioral findings integrate with neo-classical theory.
- Better understand economic decisions and their effect on market prices and returns.
  **Form of Assessment:**
- Written exam (60 min.)

## 📈 Management und Marketing

### Marketing Research (Theorieentwicklung)

**Course Content:**
This course teaches students how to develop and test theories in an applied manner. It discusses structural equation modeling (SEM) and methods for analyzing unstructured data, with an emphasis on marketing strategy. Students develop and test theoretical frameworks for their own research.
**Learning Goals:**

- Learn to generate ideas, define concepts, and clarify relationships between concepts.
- Explore theory construction and testing using SEM.
- Identify substantive theoretical contributions to the marketing strategy literature.

## 💡 Interdisziplinäre Forschungsthemen (IS/OM/Sustainability)

**Course Content:**
This seminar discusses the latest advances and challenges associated with the transition towards a decarbonized energy economy. Topics cover the economics and management of sustainability activities and clean energy technologies across all sectors (energy, transport, manufacturing). Students present papers and act as discussants.
**Learning Outcomes:**
