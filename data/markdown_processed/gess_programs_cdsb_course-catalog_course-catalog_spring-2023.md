# CDSB Course Catalog Overview

This catalog summarizes the core research seminars and advanced courses offered within the CDSB program, consolidating repeated content across different semesters and departmental listings.

## 📚 Core Research Seminars (Consolidated)

### 1. Interdisciplinary Research Seminar

This course is designed to foster interdisciplinary thinking by having participants engage with research from multiple academic centers.

**Course Content:**
The seminar is structured around four core building blocks:

1. **Kick-Off & Introductory Session:** Participants present the core idea of an interdisciplinary paper from a top journal in their field.
1. **Mini-Research-Day:** Students present their current working paper and discuss a paper from a fellow student in another field.
1. **Science Speed Dating:** Short bilateral talks to match research interests, leading to collaborative proposal development.
1. **Project Presentations & Writeups:** Groups present their final interdisciplinary research proposal and write a detailed write-up.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Ability to develop and communicate an interdisciplinary research proposal (written and oral).
- Critical evaluation of research methods from diverse fields.
- Experience presenting work to a critical, interdisciplinary audience.

**Assessment:** Pass/Fail. Requires active participation in all four building blocks, including presentations and proposal submission.

**Prerequisites:** Exclusively for doctoral students at GESS.

**Schedule:** (Dates vary by year; check specific catalog links for details.)

### 2. Climate/Sustainable Finance Seminar

This seminar focuses on the latest advances and challenges associated with the transition towards a decarbonized energy economy.

**Course Content:**
Topics cover the economics and management of sustainability activities and clean energy technologies across all sectors, with a focus on the energy sector, transportation services, and carbon-free manufacturing processes. Participants present papers and take the role of a discussant.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Introduction to current research paradigms in climate/sustainable finance.
- Training in presenting and evaluating academic papers in seminar and conference settings.

**Assessment:** Participation (20%), Paper presentations and discussions (80%).

**Prerequisites:** Assumes a background in core graduate-level finance.

**Schedule:** (Dates vary by year; check specific catalog links for details.)

### 3. Empirical Methods in Accounting & Taxation

This seminar provides deep dives into advanced empirical methods used in accounting and taxation research.

**Course Content:**
Covers core empirical methods for causal identification, including Potential Outcome Framework, surveys, Difference-in-Difference estimation, Regression Discontinuity (RDD), and Instrumental Variables (IV). Sessions involve summarizing core papers and discussing state-of-the-art applications.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Acquaintance with key topics and methods for causal identification in empirical tax research.
- Ability to critically discuss the strengths and weaknesses of recent research papers.
- Development of own research ideas and execution through a project write-up.

**Assessment:** Varies by specific course iteration (e.g., Presentation/Essay/Participation).

**Prerequisites:** Advanced Econometrics I or Applied Econometrics I.

**Schedule:** (Dates vary by year; check specific catalog links for details.)

### 4. Data Science & NLP Lab

A practical lab focusing on applying data science techniques to textual data.

**Course Content:**
Guides students through the entire scientific data-science project lifecycle: problem formulation, data acquisition, method selection, analysis, and documentation. The focus is on analyzing textual data (e.g., news or social media) using Natural Language Processing (NLP).

**Learning Goals:**

- Practical experience in conducting scientific data-science projects.
- Training in presentation and research communication.

**Assessment:** Written elaboration (90%) and presentation (10%).

**Prerequisites:** Recommended: Knowledge of basic statistics and prior experience with R or Stata.

**Schedule:** (Dates vary by year; check specific catalog links for details.)

### 5. Core Econometrics & Causal Inference

A comprehensive reading course on advanced econometric methods for causal inference.

**Course Content:**
Examines the interplay between sampling and experimental methods, statistical inference, and estimation of causal effects. Uses the German Business Panel as a case study to implement cutting-edge methods. Students present papers and write technical reports.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Skills in designing data collection and analysis (questionnaires, survey experiments).
- Ability to critically evaluate empirical evidence and design research.

**Assessment:** Paper (technical report) (optional), Presentation (50%), Class Participation (50%).

**Prerequisites:** Aimed at Ph.D. students in or beyond their second year.

**Schedule:** (Dates vary by year; check specific catalog links for details.)

## 💼 Departmental Focus Areas

### Accounting & Taxation Research Seminar

Focuses on current research topics in accounting and taxation.

**Course Content:**

- **Seminar Style:** Students present their own research ideas at various stages (early ideas to working papers).
- **Discussion:** Interactive discussion on potential contribution, literature, and research design.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Ability to present and discuss original research results scientifically.
- Insight into assessing research contribution and designing studies.

**Assessment:** Oral Participation.

**Prerequisites:** Advanced Econometrics I or Applied Econometrics I.

### Finance Research Seminar

Focuses on current research topics in finance.

**Course Content:**
Visiting researchers present working papers, deepening student insights into empirical and theoretical research methods.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Ability to follow and discuss current research topics in finance.
- Developing research ideas and understanding project design.

**Assessment:** Oral Participation.

**Prerequisites:** Assumes background in core graduate-level finance.

### Management & Operations Research

These courses focus on empirical research design in management and operations.

**Course Content (General):**

- **Operations Management:** Focuses on moving beyond the "OR approach" to empirical research design, emphasizing the link between research questions and appropriate methodologies (quantitative vs. qualitative).
- **Management/Marketing:** Focuses on developing and testing theories in an applied manner, covering structural equation modeling (SEM) and developing research hypotheses.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Ability to initiate, design, implement, and evaluate empirical research in the social sciences.
- Developing theoretical frameworks and hypotheses for research projects.

**Assessment:** Varies (e.g., Oral Exam, Essay, Presentation).

**Prerequisites:** Varies (e.g., Fundamentals of Statistics, specific finance modules).

## 🌐 Specialized Topics

### Behavioral Finance

Reviews recent research on how psychological biases affect financial decisions.

**Course Content:**
Examines behavioral finance models that explain market anomalies when rational models fail, covering topics like overconfidence and prospect theory.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Understanding how human and social cognitive/emotional biases affect market prices.
- Integrating behavioral findings with neo-classical theory.

**Assessment:** Exam.

**Prerequisites:** FIN 901 participants.

### Economics & Politics (Big Questions)

A broad seminar tackling major questions at the intersection of economics and politics.

**Course Content:**
Discussion of key texts (e.g., *Capitalism and Freedom*, *Moral Tribes*) to explore fundamental questions about society, morality, and the role of values in economic organization.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Developing critical perspectives on the relationship between economics and politics.

**Assessment:** Essay and participation.

**Prerequisites:** Doctoral students at GESS.

## 🛠️ Skills & Methodology Workshops

### Research Skills Seminar Series

A general seminar covering Open Science, reproducibility, and data management.

**Course Content:**
Covers conceptual issues like the replication crisis and practical skills like pre-registration and data wrangling in R, supporting an optimized and transparent research workflow.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Knowledge of Open Science principles.
- Practical skills in research data management.

**Assessment:** Participation.
