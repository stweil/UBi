# Dissertation Proposal Guidelines

A written dissertation proposal must be submitted to the admission and examination committee at the beginning of the third semester.

**Key Deadline:** 31 August

The process involves the mentor and prospective supervisor commenting on the proposal before the committee. Successful acceptance of the proposal by the committee confirms the continuation of the doctoral program and recognizes the successful coursework completed during the first year of study.

## Required Proposal Components

The proposal must be structured according to the following sections:

### 1. Title Page

This section must include:

- (Working) Title
- Name
- Short Abstract

### 2. Introduction

This section should provide a comprehensive overview of the research:

- Overview of the research question.
- Explanation of the academic and/or practical importance/relevance of the topic.
- Outline of the main objectives, the anticipated contribution, the expected findings, the planned interpretation of those findings, and the broader implications of the research.
- Provide detailed coverage of two or three key aspects of the research.

### 3. Relevant Literature and Theoretical Background

Demonstrate a deep understanding of the existing academic discourse:

- Show awareness of the debates and issues raised in relevant bodies of literature.
- Identify your specific niche within the literature and explain how your research question will contribute usefully to that area.
- This section may include the development of hypotheses supported by theoretical reasoning.
- State the main research techniques planned (e.g., econometric techniques, interviews, case studies, modeling).

### 4. Methodology

This section details the research execution plan:

- Demonstrate awareness of available methodological tools and justify which ones are suitable for the research.
- State the main research techniques to be used (e.g., econometric techniques, interviews, case studies, modeling).
- Indicate suggested data collection procedures, specifying sources and addressing potential difficulties.
- Name any analytical techniques intended for use.
- If applicable, describe any artifacts that will be created as part of the work, including the methods planned for their creation and evaluation.

### 5. Expected Research Results and Future Plan

This section looks forward, detailing outcomes and next steps:

- Present expected findings and how they relate to the established hypotheses (if applicable). Initial findings may also be presented.
- Discuss how the results fit into the larger academic context.
- Include a proposed timetable of activities.
- Provide a short outlook on potential future research topics.

### 6. Bibliography

List all references cited within the proposal.

## Formatting and Scope Guidelines

- **Length:** 20 pages $\\pm$ 10% (excluding the appendix).
- **Font:** Arial
- **Font Size:** 11 pt
- **Line Spacing:** 1.5
