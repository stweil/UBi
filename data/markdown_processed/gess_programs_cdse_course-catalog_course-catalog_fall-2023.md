### Course: [Economics Course 1] (Mathematics/Analysis Focus)

**Prerequisites:** Basic mathematical knowledge
**Course Content:**
The course consists of four chapters:

1. **Chapter 1:** Introduces and discusses basic mathematical concepts like sets, functions, and relations. Strict mathematical reasoning is explained and applied.
1. **Chapter 2:** Covers the concept of metric and normed spaces, discussing the convergence of sequences in these spaces, the continuity of functions, and the concept of compact sets.
1. **Chapter 3:** Deals with vector spaces, matrix algebra, linear transformation, and eigenvalues of matrices.
1. **Chapter 4:** Covers a multivariate concept of differentiability and its application in solving unconstrained and constrained optimization problems.

**Competences Acquired:**
Students know basic mathematical concepts of analysis and linear algebra. They can interpret mathematical formulas written in condensed mathematical syntax, understand the concept of a proof, and develop rigorous mathematical proofs at an elementary level. They understand abstract mathematical concepts like metric spaces and linear spaces and are able to comprehend argumentation based on these concepts. They are able to apply their knowledge, especially regarding the calculation of limits and derivatives, linear algebra methods, and solving nonlinear optimization problems. Students can communicate their mathematical knowledge in English.

**Assessment:**

- Exam (120 min)

**Teaching Assistants:**
So Jin Lee and Chang Liu

### Course: [Microeconomics Theory]

**Prerequisites:** E700
**Course Content:**
The course provides a foundation for studies in microeconomics at the Ph.D. level.

- **Part 1: Decision Theory:**
  1. Choice, preferences and utility
  1. Choice under uncertainty: Expected utility
  1. Utility for money
  1. Behavioral models of choice under uncertainty
- **Part 2: Game Theory:**
  5\. Static games of complete information: Rationalizability and iterated strict dominance
  6\. Static games of complete information: Nash equilibrium
  7\. Static games of incomplete information
  8\. Dynamic games: The extensive form
  9\. Dynamic games: Equilibrium concepts

**Competences Acquired:**
Students will acquire the basic tools for graduate-level microeconomic analysis, learning to model decision-making and strategic interactions. They will gain important mathematical skills in decision theory and game theory, enabling them to apply theory to their own research and read recent developments. The concepts serve as building blocks for advanced topics (Advanced Microeconomics II and III, macroeconomics, empirical economics). Students also learn to write rigorous formal proofs for microeconomic questions.

**Textbook References:**

- Fudenberg, D & Tirole, J. (1991). *Game Theory*. MIT Press.
- Kreps, D. (2012). *Microeconomic Foundation 1: Choice and Competitive Markets*. Princeton University Press.
- Mas-Colell, A. Whinston, M.D. & Green, J. (1995). *Microeconomic Theory*. Oxford University Press.
- Osborne M. and Rubinstein, A. (1994). *A Course in Game Theory*. MIT Press.

### Course: [Macroeconomic Theory]

**Prerequisites:** E700
**Course Content:**
This course provides an introduction to the foundations of modern macroeconomic analysis. The focus is on structural dynamic models where household preferences, firm technology, and market structure are explicitly specified. Agent behaviors are derived from microeconomic foundations, and macroeconomic aggregates are determined by aggregating these micro-founded decisions. Applications will also be considered.

**Literature:**

- Stokey, Nancy, and Robert Lucas with Edward Prescott (1989): *Recursive Methods in Economic Dynamics*. Harvard University Press.
- Ljungqvist, Lars, and Thomas J. Sargent. (2012) *Recursive macroeconomic theory*. MIT press.
- Acemoglu, Daron (2009): *Introduction to Modern Economic Growth*. Princeton University Press.

**Competences Acquired:**
Students will be familiar with basic methodology such as recursive methods and dynamic programming, as well as basic macroeconomic models.

**Assessment:**

- Problem sets (15%)
- Midterm (90 min, 35%)
- Final exam (120 min, 50%)

### Course: [Econometrics Theory]

**Prerequisites:** E700
**Course Content:**
This course offers an advanced treatment of econometric theory, serving as a gateway to further advanced theoretical and applied econometric modules.
**Topics Covered:** Asymptotic theory (basic probability concepts, modes of convergence, limit theorems), point estimation (general principles, maximum likelihood, extremum estimation, generalized method of moments), hypothesis testing (general principles, local power analysis, confidence sets), linear regression (general principles, causality, standard errors, regularization), instrumental variables (IV estimation, weak instruments, heterogenous effects), and the bootstrap. The module also trains in using mathematical arguments in econometrics.

**Textbook References:**

- Hansen (2022). *Econometrics*.
- Hansen (2022). *Probability and Statistics for Economists*.

**Competences Acquired:**
Students attain advanced theoretical knowledge in econometrics, are familiar with current theories, and gain a higher level of analytical capability. They are prepared for follow-up advanced theoretical and applied econometrics modules and can undertake independent research.

**Assessment:**

- Written exam (120 min)
