## 🎓 Elective Courses

### Electives from CDSS (Human Rights Focus)

This module aims to provide systematic knowledge and understanding regarding key debates on human rights violations and state repression. Students will learn to:

- Critically assess and evaluate academic research (theoretical and empirical contributions).
- Familiarize themselves with key datasets in human rights research.
- Engage in analytical discussions about published research.
- Improve writing and presentation skills.

**Recommended Reading/Resources:**

- Dunn, P. K., & Smyth, G. K. (2018). *Generalized linear models with examples in R*. Springer.
- Hoffman, L. (2015). *Longitudinal analysis: Modeling within-person fluctuation and change*. Routledge.
- James, G., Witten, D., Hastie, T., & Tibshirani, R. (2013). *An introduction to statistical learning with applications in R*. Springer.
- Kline, R. B. (2023). *Principles and practice of structural equation modeling*. Guilford.
- Snijders, T. A. B., & Bosker, R. J. (2012). *Multilevel analysis: An introduction to basic and advanced multilevel modeling*. Sage.

### Electives from CDSB (European Union Law)

This course examines the increasing impact of European Union Law on the taxation of private individuals and companies operating in Europe. Topics covered include:

- Introduction to European Union Tax Law, aims, and measures.
- Positive harmonization of indirect taxes (European directives).
- Secondary law harmonizing direct taxes (e.g., the Parent-Subsidiary Directive).
- The importance of fundamental freedoms for taxation in Europe.
- Special focus on the case law of the European Court of Justice.

**Key Learning Areas:**

- Basic principles of European Law.
- Harmonization of indirect and direct taxes by primary and secondary law.
- Fundamental freedoms referring to taxation in Europe.
- Important case law analysis.

## 🔬 Quantitative Methods Tutorial (Cross-Disciplinary)

This tutorial accompanies the "Quantitative Methods" course in the M.A. program in Political Science. The lab sessions focus on practical quantitative issues, including:

- Obtaining and preparing datasets.
- Using statistical software and selecting appropriate tests.
- Graphing data effectively for presentation and analysis.
- Interpreting results.
- The seminar serves as a software tutorial; no prior statistical programming knowledge is expected.

**Practical Skills Focus:**

- **Causal Inference:** Students learn to implement causal inference methods in R. Participants must bring their own laptop and ensure R and R Studio are installed before the first session.

## 🌟 Specialized & Advanced Modules

### Women in Leadership (Interdisciplinary Focus)

This course examines the gender gap in leadership positions from psychological and economic perspectives, with a special emphasis on the academic environment.

**Structure (Four Core Building Blocks):**

1. **Women in Leadership: The Economic Perspective:** Analyzes economic reasons for gender gaps, examining time trends, gender quotas, and the impact on labor market choices and the gender pay gap.
1. **Women in Leadership: The Psychological Perspective:** Addresses gender stereotypes (descriptive vs. prescriptive) and their impact on leadership aspirations. Topics include the "Think Manager – Think Male" phenomenon, Glass metaphors, and backlash effects.
1. **“Raise Your Voice” – Rhetoric Training:** A hands-on session where students practice presenting research findings in a conference-like scenario and receiving critical feedback. *Preparation required: A short presentation (5–7 minutes) on "What excites me about my research topic?" and a video-capable device.*
1. **“Raise Your Pay” – Negotiation Training:** Teaches negotiation skills, conflict management, and responding to verbal attacks through a negotiation game. *Prerequisite: Students must submit a personal negotiation topic in advance.*

### Bridge Courses (General Skills)

These courses provide foundational skills in specific areas:

- **Bridge Course from CDSB/CDSS (Human Rights):** Focuses on systematic knowledge of human rights debates, critical evaluation of research, and data handling.
- **Bridge Course from CDSS (Psychology):** Focuses on advanced statistical methods, including:
  - Generalized linear models (Dunn & Smyth, 2018).
  - Longitudinal analysis (Hoffman, 2015).
  - Statistical learning (James et al., 2013).
  - Structural equation modeling (Kline, 2023).
  - Multilevel analysis (Snijders & Bosker, 2012).
- **Bridge Course from CDSS (Sociology):** Focuses on implementing causal inference methods in R.
