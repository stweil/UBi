## 1. General Equilibrium Theory (Spring 2022)

**Prerequisites:** E700, E701, E702, E703
**Grading:** Final examination (120 min). Prerequisite for examination: regular class participation, submission of problem sets, and good performance in exercise sessions.

**Course Content:**
This course provides an advanced introduction to the theory of general equilibrium. It builds on introductory microeconomics (E702) and uses mathematical tools from E700 and E701. It covers topics such as:

- Aggregate Demand
- Production Theory
- Exchange Economies
- Production Economies
- Uncertainty and Arrow-Debreu Equilibrium
- Basic Asset Pricing

The course establishes the fundamentals of economic equilibrium theory, forming a basis for macroeconomics, international trade, and finance. Learning is complemented by intensive problem-solving work.
**Teaching Method:** Lecture (4 SWS) + Exercise (2 SWS)

**Competences Acquired:**
Students learn core concepts like the Law of Demand, Walrasian equilibrium, arbitrage-free allocations, and market incompleteness. They gain the ability to analyze problems independently across various economic fields and assess the appropriateness of equilibrium-theoretic approaches.

## 2. Dynamic Macroeconomic Modeling (Spring 2022)

**Prerequisites:** E700, E701, E702, E703
**Grading:** Final examination, solutions to problem sets, and participation in exercise sessions.

**Course Content:**
This module covers basic methods for dynamic economic modeling under rational expectations. Topics include:

- Linear Rational Expectations (RE) Models: determinacy, indeterminacy, and 'sunspot' equilibria.
- Linear RE models and Vector Auto-Regressions (VARs): state space representation, identification of economic shocks.
- Linear Quadratic (LQ) Dynamic Programming: Ricatti equation, stochastic problems, and certainty equivalence.
- Introduction to the New Keynesian Model and its Linearized Form.

**Competences Acquired:**
Students can apply and understand basic tools for business cycle analysis and follow recent literature on dynamic stochastic general equilibrium models.

## 5. Heterogeneity in Macroeconomics (Spring 2022)

**Prerequisites:** E700, E701, E702, E703, E801, E802, E803
**Grading:** Written exam and assignments.

**Course Content:**
Focuses on heterogeneity in macroeconomics, introducing basic tools of modern macroeconomics. Topics include:

- **Consumption/Savings:** Complete and incomplete markets, aggregate uncertainty.
- **Investment:** Neoclassical theory, adjustment costs, financial frictions.
- **Labor Markets:** Partial equilibrium search, general equilibrium search, and matching.

**Competences Acquired:**
Students gain a deep understanding of mathematical methods in modern macroeconomics, preparing them for independent Ph.D. research.

## 6. Econometrics and Time Series Analysis (Spring 2022)

**Prerequisites:** E700, E701, E702, E703, E801, E802, E803
**Grading:** Written exam and assignments.

**Course Content:**
Covers advanced econometric techniques, including:

- Extremum estimators (M-estimation).
- Three main testing principles (LR, LM, WALD).
- Bootstrap theory related to testing.
- Basic analysis of panel data models.
- Time series analysis (focusing on stationary setups).

**Competences Acquired:**
Students develop mathematical argumentation skills and the ability to read and apply advanced econometric literature.

**Recommended Textbooks:**

- Cameron, A.C. and Trivedi, P.K. (2005), *Microeconometrics: Methods and Applications*.
- Hamilton, J.D. (1994), *Time Series Analysis*.
- Wooldridge, J.M. (2010), *Econometric Analysis of Cross Section and Panel Data*.

## 7. Private Information and Behavioral Economics (Spring 2022)

**Prerequisites:** E700, E701, E702, E703, E801, E802, E803
**Grading:** Problem sets and presentations.

**Course Content:**
This course examines how household heterogeneity affects the economy and how macroeconomic policy and market frictions influence inequality. It introduces the standard incomplete-markets model, extending it to:

1. Models with overlapping generations and a life-cycle component.
1. Two-asset models.
   The course also covers advanced numerical methods for solving dynamic programming problems.

**Competences Acquired:**
Students learn to use numerical methods to solve dynamic programming problems and understand the implications of household financial decision-making.

## 8. Industrial Organization (IO) (Spring 2022)

**Prerequisites:** Formal: 2nd+ year Ph.D. students (CDSE); 2nd year Master of Economic Research.
**Grading:** Presentation (80%) and Discussion (20%).

**Course Content:**
Studies the strategic interactions of firms in markets. Key concepts include market power and market structure. The goal is to develop an econometric understanding of market power and competition, with applications to competition policy, price competition, investment, and mergers.

**Competences Acquired:**
Students can develop and apply econometric analysis of market power and competition, combining data, economic models, and appropriate econometric techniques.

## 14. Behavioral Economics and Ambiguity (Spring 2022)

**Prerequisites:** First and second year PhD courses.
**Grading:** Presenting of Research Projects.

**Course Content:**
Examines theoretical solutions to decision-making under ambiguity, moving beyond Subjective Expected Utility (SEU). Topics include:

- Ellsberg’s experiments
- MEU and CEU frameworks
- Smooth Ambiguity model

**Competences Acquired:**
Ability to understand and use non-SEU preference relations, test them experimentally, and write discussion papers on current research topics.
