## Elective and Bridge Courses

### 1. International Security and Peacekeeping

**UN Peacekeeping and the Protection of Civilians**
This course examines the problems and possibilities of United Nations (UN) peace operations. It covers the evolution of peacekeeping mandates from monitoring ceasefires to complex tasks like protecting civilians (POC), disarming ex-combatants, and promoting the rule of law. The subject is relevant for those focusing on conflict studies, international organizations, or global governance.

- **Skills Developed:** Critical evaluation of theoretical and empirical literature; effective communication of arguments based on empirical evidence; group discussion of scientific positions.

### 2. Comparative Political Behavior

**Comparative Political Behavior**
This lecture provides an introduction to theoretical approaches, key concepts, and substantive issues in comparative political behavior. It adopts a multi-level perspective, analyzing micro-level political processes and their relationship to macro-level societal and political institutions.

- **Key Readings:**
  - Cameron, A. Colin and Trivedi, Pravin K. (2005). *Microeconometrics: Methods and Applications*. Cambridge University Press.
  - Green, William H. (2008). *Econometric Analysis* (sixth edition). Prentice Hall.
  - Wooldridge, Jeffrey M. (2001). *Econometric Analysis of Cross Section and Panel Data*. The MIT Press.

### 3. Methodological & Computational Skills

These modules focus on advanced quantitative and textual data analysis techniques.

**Generative AI in Social Science Research**
Upon completion, students will be able to:

- Present basic knowledge of Generative AI applied to social science research.
- Name the latest Generative AI developments in the field.
- Describe in-depth knowledge of empirical approaches to Generative AI.
- Critically evaluate the empirical literature and applications of Generative AI.

**Textual Data Analysis (NLP)**
This lab guides students through scientific data-science projects, focusing on analyzing textual data (e.g., news or social media datasets) using Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques.

- **Learning Outcomes:**
  - Implement state-of-the-art textual methods for analyzing text data in business administration and economics.
  - Incorporate textual analysis methods to expand current knowledge and find new findings.
  - Acquire solid programming knowledge in Python (no prior knowledge required, but experience is helpful).
- **Assessment:** Written elaboration (90%) and presentation (10%).

**Causal Inference and Experimental Design**
This course provides a hands-on approach to understanding data analysis by focusing on the interplay between sampling and experimental methods. Using the German Business Panel as a case study, students learn to implement cutting-edge methods to gain insights into causal mechanisms.

- **Deliverables:** Participants present research papers and write a short technical report summarizing methods and implications for peers.

These courses provide specialized methodological training, often requiring a research paper presentation and a technical report.

- **Bridge Course from CDSB (Accounting):** Focuses on advanced analytical accounting, tax, or organizational research, emphasizing academic rigor for top-tier journal publication.
- **Bridge Course from CDSB (Finance):** (Content overlaps with Textual Data Analysis, focusing on quantitative methods.)
- **Bridge Course from CDSB (Information Systems):** Focuses on scientific data-science projects, training presentation skills, and analyzing textual data using NLP.
- **Bridge Course from CDSB (Marketing):** (Specific content not detailed, listed as a module.)
- **Bridge Course from CDSB (Taxation):** Focuses on causal inference and experimental design using the German Business Panel.
- **Bridge Course from CDSS (Political Science):** Focuses on UN Peacekeeping and the Protection of Civilians.
- **Bridge Course from CDSS (Psychology):** Focuses on critical evaluation of theoretical and empirical literature, communication of arguments, and group discussion.
- **Bridge Course from CDSS (Sociology):** Focuses on econometrics, referencing key texts (Cameron & Trivedi, Green, Wooldridge).
