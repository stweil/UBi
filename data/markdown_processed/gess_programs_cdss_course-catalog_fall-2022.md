## Advanced Quantitative Methods

### 1. Mathematical Logic and Rigour (Math for Social Science)

- **Course Content:** Provides an introduction to mathematical logic and rigor, covering set and function theory, probability, and linear algebra—the foundation for data analysis.
- **Assessment:** Exam scheduled for Monday, December 12, 2022 (10–12 in room C 012 in A5, 6).
- **Basic Readings:** Sydsaeter & Hammond (2008); Chiang & Wainwright (2005); Gill (2006); Pemberton & Rau (2007); Simon & Blume (1994).
- **Schedule:**
  - Lecture: September 8 – December 8, 2022 (Thursday, 12:00 – 13:30) [211 in B6, 30–32]

### 2. Statistical Modeling and Estimation (Beyond OLS)

- **Course Content:** Focuses on statistical models and estimators beyond linear regression. Covers violations of asymptotic properties (heteroscedasticity, endogeneity, etc.), Maximum Likelihood Estimation, and Generalized Linear Models (GLS) for binary/ordinal/count data.
- **Assessment:** Written exam (graded, 90 min).
- **Schedule:**
  - Tutorial: September 6 – December 6, 2022 (Tuesday, 15:30 – 17:00) [B 243 in A5]
  - Tutorial: September 8 – December 8, 2022 (Thursday, 12:00 – 13:30) [B 244 in A5, 6]
  - Lecture: September 6 – December 6, 2022 (Tuesday, 13:45 – 15:15) [B 244 in A5]

### 3. Data Science Approaches for Big Data (Survey Data)

- **Course Content:** Overview of data used in political science, moving from traditional statistics to data science approaches for big data. Topics include Total Survey Error (TSE) framework, operationalizing research questions, sampling, coverage, and nonresponse.
- **Assessment:** 3 mock exams (pass/fail), active participation, term paper (graded).
- **Taught by:** Prof. Joseph Sakshaug
- **Schedule:**
  - Tutorial: September 6 – December 6, 2022 (Tuesday, 10:15 – 11:45) [Online]
  - Lecture: September 6 – December 6, 2022 (Tuesday, 08:30 – 10:00) [Online]

### 4. Multivariate Analysis in Political Science

- **Course Content:** Focuses on quantitative methods, starting with linear regression models, model assumptions, and inference. Second half covers likelihood theory for binary and count data.
- **Assessment:** Take-home exam (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Tutorial: September 7 – December 7, 2022 (Wednesday, 12:00 – 13:30) [A 103 in B6, 23–25]
  - Tutorial: September 8 – December 8, 2022 (Thursday, 10:15 – 11:45) [C 217 in A5, 6]
  - Tutorial: September 9 – December 2, 2022 (Friday, 10:15 – 11:45) [A 102 in B6, 23–25]
  - Lecture: September 7 – December 7, 2022 (Wednesday, 08:30 – 10:00) [B 244 in A5, 6]

### 5. Advanced Statistical Modeling (Multivariate)

- **Course Content:** Advanced overview of standard multivariate methods, including GLMs, Structural Equation Models, Multilevel Modeling, and time-related effects. Focuses on formal foundations and empirical applications.
- **Assessment:** Active participation, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Block 1: October 13 & 14, 2022 (Mannheim: Room D 002; B6, 27–29; Bauteil B)
  - Block 2: November 10 & 11, 2022 (Mannheim: Room D 002; B6, 27–29; Bauteil B)
  - Block 3: January 12 & 13, 2023 (Mannheim: Room O 142[Schloss, Ostflügel])

### 6. Causal Inference Methods

- **Course Content:** Teaches fundamental concepts for estimating causal effects, addressing potential obstacles to causal inference. Students apply techniques in both randomized and observational settings using R.
- **Assessment:** Lecture: Participation, written exam (graded); Tutorial: Homework, oral participation, presentation.
- **Schedule:**
  - Tutorial: September 7 – December 7, 2022 (Wednesday, 13:45 – 15:15) [A102 in B6, 23–25]
  - Tutorial: September 8 – December 8, 2022 (Thursday, 15:30 – 17:00) [A 103 in B6, 23–25]
  - Lecture: September 8 – December 8, 2022 (Thursday, 13:45 – 15:15) [A 103 in B6, 23–25]

### 7. Game Theory

- **Course Content:** Provides a graduate-level introduction to formal modeling in political science. Covers normal form games, Nash equilibria, extensive form games, repeated games, and signaling games.
- **Assessment:** Working in small groups on assignments, final exam (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Tutorial: September 9 – December 9, 2022 (Friday, 08:30 – 10:00) [C 217 in A5, 6]
  - Lecture: September 5 – December 5, 2022 (Monday, 10:15 – 11:45) [B 244 in A5, 6]

## Specialized Topics

### 1. Comparative Methods in Sociology

- **Course Content:** Introduces comparative strategies for cross-national comparison of welfare states and market economies. Discusses QCA, process tracing, and within-case analysis.
- **Assessment:** Active participation, oral presentation, written term paper (graded, max. 5,000 words).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: September 6 – December 6, 2022 (Tuesday, 10:15 – 11:45) [B 317 in A5, 6]

### 2. Organizational Behavior and Development

- **Course Content:** Advanced seminar exploring research explaining variation in organizational behavior and development, covering institutional requirements and organizational development.
- **Assessment:** Presentation of required readings, active participation, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: September 8 – December 8, 2022 (Thursday, 10:15 – 11:45) [D007 in B6, 27–29]

### 3. Poverty and Social Exclusion

- **Course Content:** Introduction to concepts, dimensions, and measures of poverty and social exclusion in Europe. Discusses theories, policies, and consequences.
- **Assessment:** Active participation, regular small assignments, term paper (graded, max. 5000 words).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: Biweekly (September 7 – September 21, 2022; October 12, 2022; October 26 – December 7, 2022) (Wednesday, 08:30 – 11:45) [222 in B6, 4–5]

### 4. Gender Gap in Leadership (CDSB)

- **Course Content:** Interdisciplinary analysis of the gender gap in leadership, covering psychological and economic reasons, structural hurdles, and effectiveness of equality measures. Includes rhetoric and negotiation training.
- **Assessment:** Graded course based on participation, response papers, and learning portfolio.
- **Schedule:**
  - Lecture: September 7, 2022 (Wednesday, 10:15 – 11:45) [O 048]
  - Lecture: September 21 – October 19, 2022 (Wednesday, 10:15 – 11:45) [O 048 then in O 145]
  - Lecture: October 26 – November 16, 2022 (Wednesday, 10:15 – 13:30) [O 048]

### 5. Human Rights Violations

- **Course Content:** Examines human rights violations as a theoretical concept and empirical phenomenon, analyzing dynamics of repression in conflict and peace.
- **Assessment:** Regular participation, group presentation, research proposal, final empirical research paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: September 8 – December 8, 2022 (Thursday, 10:15 – 11:45) [C112 in A5, 6]

### 6. Political Bias in Science

- **Course Content:** Discusses political biases in science, their origins, consequences, and mitigation strategies.
- **Assessment:** Active class participation, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: September 6 – December 6, 2022 (Tuesday, 13:45 – 15:15) [C 217 in A5, 6]

### 7. Elections and Comparative Politics

- **Course Content:** Focuses on comparative research on elections, analyzing contextual effects like institutional configurations, campaign strategies, and political parties.
- **Assessment:** Mandatory readings, case study, agenda-setting presentation, short proposal, term paper (ca. 5000 words, graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: September 5 – December 5, 2022 (Monday, 13:45 – 15:15) [B 317 in A5, 6]

### 8. Work and Organizational Psychology

- **Course Content:** Overview of core topics, focusing on recent theoretical approaches and empirical findings (meta-analyses) in motivation, stress, leadership, and teams.
- **Assessment:** Graded homework assignment.
- **Schedule:**
  - Lecture: September 8 – December 8, 2022 (Thursday, 17:15 – 18:45) [Online]

### 9. Cognitive Psychology (Advanced)

- **Course Content:** Advanced treatment of research methods and topics in cognitive psychology, including stochastic modeling, information search, and memory.
- **Assessment:** Active participation, final written exam (90 mins, graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Lecture: September 8 – December 8, 2022 (Thursday, 15:30 – 17:00) [B 244 in A5, 6]

### 10. Social Psychology (Workshop)

- **Course Content:** Addresses a variety of current research topics in social psychology, allowing active discussion of methodological and theoretical implications.
- **Assessment:** Participation.
- **Schedule:**
  - Workshop: September 5 – December 5, 2022 (Monday, 10:15 – 11:45) [B317 in A5, 6]
