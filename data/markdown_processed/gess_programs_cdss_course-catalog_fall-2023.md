## 🎓 Core Methodological & Research Skills Courses

These courses are foundational and are offered across multiple departments (Sociology, Political Science, Psychology).

### 1. Current Research Perspectives (Cross-Disciplinary)

**Description:** This course introduces first-year CDSS doctoral students to the theoretically informed research approaches and substantive research fields underpinning social science research in Mannheim. Faculty members present an overview of their research areas, current findings, and potential future topics. Students gain exposure to diverse faculty expertise.
**Assignment:** Students must develop a research project idea, informed theoretically or methodologically by insights from faculty presentations (outside their primary field). This requires a short 3-page paper due October 31st.
**Schedule (Fall 2023):**

- **Lecture:** 08.09.23 (Fri) 12:00 – 15:15 (Online) | 11.09.23 (Mon) 12:00 – 15:15 (Online) | 18.09.23 (Mon) 12:00 – 15:15 (Online)
- *(Note: Schedules are repeated across Sociology, Political Science, and Psychology sections.)*

### 2. Introduction to Mathematical Logic and Rigour (Quantitative Methods)

**Description:** Essential for modern social scientists, this course provides an introduction to mathematical logic and rigor. It covers introductory set and function theory, analysis of functions, probability, and linear algebra—the foundation for data analysis.
**Assessment:** The exam is scheduled for Tuesday, December 12, 2023, from 10:00 to 12:00 in room 211 in B6, 30–32.
**Readings:**

- **Basic:** Knut Sydsaeter and Peter Hammond. 2008. *Essential Mathematics for Economic Analysis*. 3rd edition.
- **Additional:** Includes texts by Chiang & Wainwright (2005), Gill (2006), Pemberton & Rau (2007), and Simon & Blume (1994).
  **Schedule (Fall 2023):**
- **Workshop:** Biweekly, 13.09.23 – 06.12.23 | Wednesday | 13:45 – 17:00 | 211 in B6, 30–32

### 3. Research Design and Methodology

**Description:** This course addresses core challenges in research design—the plan for executing a project and using evidence to answer a question. It helps students understand the trade-offs involved in choosing a design. Students are expected to develop original research questions and actively participate in seminar-style meetings.
**Assessment:** Mandatory readings, active participation, homework assignments, research proposal presentation, and a final term paper (approx. 10 pages).
**Schedule (Fall 2023):**

- **Workshop:** 05.09.23 – 05.12.23 | Tuesday | 10:15 – 11:45 | 211 in B6, 30–32

### 4. Advanced Quantitative Methods (Statistics)

**Description:** Focuses on statistical models and estimators beyond linear regression. Prerequisites include a good understanding of classical linear regression. Topics cover violations of asymptotic properties (heteroscedasticity, endogeneity, IV-estimator), Maximum Likelihood Estimation, and Generalized Linear Models (GLS) for binary/ordinal/count data (Logit, Probit, Poisson, Negative Binomial).
**Assessment:** Regular participation in lab sessions, weekly exercise presentation (including slides, Stata syntax, and interpretation), and a written exam (graded, 90 min).
**Credits:** 9 ECTS (lecture & tutorial).
**Schedule (Fall 2023):**

- **Lecture:** 05.09.23 – 05.12.23 | Tuesday | 13:45 – 15:15 | A 103 in B6, 23–25
- **Tutorial:** 05.09.23 – 05.12.23 | Tuesday | 15:30 – 17:00 | D002 in B6, 27–29

### 5. Causal Inference

**Description:** Teaches fundamental concepts for estimating causal effects, including potential obstacles to causal inference. Students learn to apply techniques using real datasets in R, focusing on both randomized and observational settings.
**Assessment:** Lecture: Participation, term paper (graded). Tutorial: Homework, oral participation, presentation.
**Schedule (Fall 2023):**

- **Lecture:** 07.09.23 – 07.12.23 | Thursday | 10:15 – 11:45 | B143, in A5, 6 entrance B
- **Tutorial:** 06.09.23 – 06.12.23 | Wednesday | 12:00 – 13:30 | A103 in B6, 23–25

### 6. Bayesian Statistics in the Social Sciences

**Description:** Introduces selected topics relating to Bayesian statistics. Activities provide insight into the current status of research in this field, helping to identify open questions.
**Learning Outcomes:** Students can present basic knowledge, name methodological developments, describe empirical approaches, and critically evaluate the literature.
**Assessment:** Term paper (5,000 words max.) involving either an empirical study or a critical literature review. Evaluated as 'pass/fail'.
**Schedule (Fall 2023):**

- **Seminar:** 08.09.23 – 08.12.23 | Friday | 08:30 – 10:00 | Online

### 7. Multivariate Modeling (Advanced Statistics)

**Description:** Advanced overview of standard multivariate methods, including GLMs, structural equation models, multilevel modeling, and time-related effects. The goal is to bring PhD candidates to a common level of statistical knowledge and data analytic skills.
**Schedule (Fall 2023):**

- **Block 1:** Oct 19 & 20 (10:00 a.m. - 6:00 p.m.) | Location: Mannheim (Room: 108 CIP Pool, B6 30-32)
- **Block 2:** Nov 09 & 10 (10:00 a.m. - 6:00 p.m.) | Location: Mannheim (Room: 108 CIP Pool, B6 30-32)
- **Block 3:** Jan 11 & 12 (10:00 a.m. - 6:00 p.m.) | Location: Mannheim (Room: 108 CIP Pool, B6 30-32)
- *(Note: Registration required via [online](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/smip/program/qualification-program/fall-winter-2023/))*

### 8. Game Theory (Introductory & Advanced)

**Description:**

- **Introductory:** Provides a graduate-level overview of game theory, covering normal/extensive form games, Nash equilibria, repeated games, and signaling games. Focuses on reading and writing formal models.
- **Advanced:** A continuation, emphasizing applications in political science (e.g., electoral competition, war bargaining).
  **Assessment:** Includes assignments, presentations, and a final exam.
  **Schedule (Fall 2023):**
- **Lecture:** 04.09.23 – 04.12.23 | Monday | 10:15 – 11:45 | C217 in A5, 6 entrance C
- **Tutorial:** 08.09.23 – 08.12.23 | Friday | 12:00 – 13:30 | C217 in A5, 6 entrance C

### 9. Data Science in Action (Lecture Series)

**Description:** A lecture series offered by the Mannheim Center for Data Science. GESS doctoral students can attend as a bridge course.
**Assessment:** Requires participation in at least 80% of lectures and writing a 15-page essay (pass/fail).
**Registration:** Visit [https://www.uni-mannheim.de/en/datascience/details/vortragsreihe-data-science-in-action/](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/en/datascience/details/vortragsreihe-data-science-in-action/)

## 🏛️ Departmental Focus Areas

### Sociology

- **Doctoral Theses:** Discussions are supervised by [professors in the department of Sociology](https://www.sowi.uni-mannheim.de/en/about/professors/sociology/). Check with individual chairs for dates and times.

### Political Science

- **Doctoral Theses:** Discussions are supervised by [professors in the department of Political Science](https://www.sowi.uni-mannheim.de/en/about/professors/political-science/). Check with individual chairs for dates and times.

### Psychology

- **Doctoral Theses:** Check with individual chairs in the Psychology Department for research colloquia dates and times.
- **Research Colloquia:**
  - **Clinical Psychology:** Focuses on research methods and open questions in the field.
  - **Cognitive Psychology:** Covers basic methodology, stochastic modeling, and cognitive processes.
  - **Social Psychology:** Addresses varied research topics, focusing on methodological and theoretical implications.
  - **Work & Organizational Psychology:** Overview of core topics (motivation, stress, leadership) with a focus on meta-analyses.

## 🌍 Specialized Seminar Topics

### 1. Educational Inequalities in Europe

**Description:** Discusses classic and recent theories of educational inequalities, trends over time, and cross-national variation in Europe, focusing on social origin, ethnic background, and gender.
**Assessment:** Regular small assignments, compulsory attendance, active discussion, and a term paper (max. 5000 words).
**Schedule (Fall 2023):**

- **Seminar:** 13.09.23 (Wed) 08:30 – 11:45 | 27.09.23 (Wed) 08:30 – 11:45 | 04.10.23 (Wed) 08:30 – 11:45 | 18.10.23 (Wed) 08:30 – 11:45 | 08.11.23 (Wed) 08:30 – 11:45 | 22.11.23 (Wed) 08:30 – 11:45 | 29.11.23 (Wed) 08:30 – 11:45
- **Location:** B6, 4–5 room 237

### 2. Immigration and Integration in Western Europe

**Description:** Examines reactions to Muslim immigration in Western Europe at various levels. Discusses state policies for mass immigration and integration, and methods for explaining Islamophobia.
**Assessment:** Participation, weekly reading, empirical study presentation, and a term paper.
**Schedule (Fall 2023):**

- **Seminar:** 06.09.23 – 06.12.23 | Wednesday | 10:15 – 11:45 | A102, in B6 23-25

### 3. Social Stratification and Inequality

**Description:** Covers core aspects of social stratification and inequality in modern societies, including dimensions, processes of reproduction, and transformation.
**Assessment:** Active participation, presentation, and a term paper (max. 5000 words, due Jan 31, 2024).
**Schedule (Fall 2023):**

- **Seminar:** 15.09.23 (Fri) 10:15 – 13:30 | 20.10.23 (Fri) 10:15 – 13:30 | 27.10.23 (Fri) 10:15 – 13:30 | 03.11.23 (Fri) 10:15 – 13:30 | 10.11.23 (Fri) 10:15 – 13:30 | 24.11.23 (Fri) 10:15 – 13:30 | 01.12.23 (Fri) 10:15 – 13:30
- **Location:** B317 in A5, 6 entrance B

### 4. Political Science Colloquia (MZES/MaRCS)

**Description:** Students can choose between two weekly colloquia: MZES A (“European Societies and their Integration”) or MZES Colloquium B (“European Political Systems and their Integration”). Alternatively, they can attend the Mannheim Research Colloquium on Survey Methods (MaRCS).
**Requirement:** 2nd and 3rd year students must submit a list of attended colloquia for 2 ECTS. Minimum 80% attendance required.
**Prerequisites:** Knowledge of statistics and empirical social research methods.

### 5. Political Science: Coalition Politics

**Description:** Addresses coalition politics throughout its life cycle, including bargaining, formation, role of agreements, and termination.
**Assessment:** Active participation, oral presentation, and a term paper (15–20 pages).
**Schedule (Fall 2023):**

- **Seminar:** 05.09.23 – 05.12.23 | Tuesday | 13:45 – 15:15 | B317 in A5, 6 entrance B

### 6. Political Science: Free Speech and Censorship

**Description:** Discusses the importance of free expression, how states regulate it, and the effects of regulation. Also considers the impact of cultural diversity and digital communication.
**Assessment:** Participation and a term paper.
**Schedule (Fall 2023):**

- **Seminar:** 07.09.23 – 07.12.23 | Thursday | 08:30 – 10:00 | B318 in A5, 6 entrance B

### 7. Political Science: Contextual Effects on Behavior

**Description:** Examines how context (social, political, institutional) influences individual-level political behavior. Addresses conceptual, theoretical, and methodological issues in analyzing contextual effects.
**Assessment:** Oral presentation of a literature review, active participation, and a term paper (ca. 8,000 words).
**Schedule (Fall 2023):**

- **Seminar:** 07.09.23 – 07.12.23 | Thursday | 13:45 – 15:15 | B318 in A5, 6 entrance B

### 8. Political Science: Populism vs. Technocracism

**Description:** Compares the relationship between populism and democracy versus technocracism and democracy, and explores the relationship between populism and technocracism.
**Assessment:** Active participation, presentation of two studies, and a term paper.
**Schedule (Fall 2023):**

- **Seminar:** 05.09.23 – 05.12.23 | Tuesday | 12:00 – 13:30 | C 112 in A5, 6 entrance C
