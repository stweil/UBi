## Spring 2023 Course Offerings

### Sociology

#### Dissertation Proposal Workshop

- **Course Content:** Support and crucial feedback on writing the dissertation proposal. This proposal outlines the doctoral thesis project, covering research motivation, theoretical/empirical literature review, theoretical framework development, methodology specification, and planned empirical analysis. Students must be prepared to address questions regarding the question's interest, importance, and scholarly contribution.
- **Prerequisites:** Crafting Social Science Research, Literature Review.
- **Assessment:** N/A (Information on submission (8 ECTS) is available in the CDSS regulations section).
- **Schedule:**
  - Workshop: Further dates tbd | 14.02.23 | Tuesday | 10:15 – 11:45 | Zoom | [Link]

#### Causal Inference Methods

- **Course Content:** Addresses the core challenge of establishing credible causal effects in empirical analyses. The course introduces key conceptual and methodological approaches: potential outcomes model, experimental designs, matching and regression, instrumental variables, regression discontinuity designs, difference-in-differences, and fixed effects.
- **Prerequisites:** None specified.
- **Assessment:** Active participation, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Workshop: biweekly | 17.02.23 – 31.03.23 | Friday | 13:45 – 17:00 | 211 in B6, 30–32
  - Workshop: biweekly | 21.04.23 – 02.06.23 | Friday | 13:45 – 17:00 | 211 in B6, 30–32

#### CDSS Doctoral Workshop (Sociology)

- **Course Content:** Provides support and crucial feedback for ongoing dissertation projects for CDSS doctoral candidates in sociology. Students are expected to play two roles: providing feedback to peers and presenting their own work.
- **Prerequisites:** Mandatory for first to third year CDSS Sociology students; recommended for later candidates (no credit).
- **Schedule:**
  - Workshop: irregular, see course description | 15.03.23 – 24.05.23 | Wednesday | 15:30 – 16:30 | 209 in B6, 30–32

#### Colloquium Attendance

- **Course Content:** CDSS doctoral students in political science and sociology can choose freely which weekly colloquium to attend. Attendance is mandatory in year two and three of doctoral studies. Options include:
  - MZES A Colloquium “European Societies and their Integration”
  - MZES Colloquium B “European Political Systems and their Integration”
  - Alternatively: Mannheim Research Colloquium on Survey Methods (MaRCS).
- **Prerequisites:** CSSR, Literature Review.
- **Details:** Refer to [the MZES webpages](http://www.mzes.uni-mannheim.de/d7/en/news/events/colloquium/1) for details.

#### Academic Paper Writing Workshop

- **Course Content:** Guidance and constructive feedback on writing academic papers in English. Students learn writing/revision techniques, applying them to their own texts, and improving papers to a publishable standard.
- **Prerequisites:** CSSR, Literature Review.
- **Assessment:** N/A (Focus on improvement through practice).

### General Methodology & Data Science Courses

#### Survey Design Fundamentals

- **Course Content:** Teaches the fundamentals of survey design, covering implementation steps, design decisions, and sources of error. Uses large-scale surveys like ALLBUS, ESS, EVS, and SOEP for illustration.
- **Prerequisites:** Some basic knowledge of statistical inference and R is required.
- **Assessment:** Active participation, homework assignments/oral presentations, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: | 16.02.23 – 01.06.23 | Thursday | 13:45 – 15:15 | tbc

#### Longitudinal Data Analysis (Panel Data)

- **Course Content:** Broad overview of methods for longitudinal data analysis, focusing on panel data (advantages, structure, models: pooled OLS, fixed/random effects, first-differences, GLMs, event history analysis).
- **Tutorial:** Applying methods using R to real survey data.
- **Prerequisites:** Some basic knowledge of statistical inference and R is required.
- **Assessment:** Lecture (3 quizzes, written exam); Tutorial (12 homework assignments, 9 must pass).
- **Schedule:**
  - Lecture: | 13.02.23 – 22.05.23 | Monday | 10:15 – 11:45 | B317 in A5, 6 entrance B | [Link]
  - Tutorial: | 14.02.23 – 30.05.23 | Tuesday | 12:00 – 13:30 | B 318 in A5, 6 entrance B | [Link]
  - Tutorial: | 14.02.23 – 30.05.23 | Tuesday | 15:30 – 17:00 | A 102 in B6, 23–25 | [Link]

#### Data Science & Machine Learning with Python

- **Course Content:** Covers major steps in statistical analysis: 1) Python basics and libraries (programming paradigms, big data); 2) Data exploration, visualization, and statistical modeling; 3) Machine learning basics (supervised, unsupervised, semi-supervised) and neural networks (computer vision, linguistics, AI). Applies concepts to real-world projects.
- **Prerequisites:** Some programming skills (Python, R, JAVA, C, HTML, BASH, etc.) OR motivation to learn Python. *Students must bring their own laptop.*
- **Assessment:** Coding homework, Data analysis project (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: biweekly | 24.02.23 – 24.03.23 | Friday | 08:30 – 13:30 | C 112 in A5, 6 entrance C
  - Seminar: biweekly | 21.04.23 – 02.06.23 | Friday | 08:30 – 13:30 | tbc

#### Causal Inference via Field Experiments

- **Course Content:** Practical introduction to field experiments, covering the logic behind experimentation and the potential outcomes framework. Focuses on analyzing real experimental designs, addressing issues in conducting/interpreting field experiments.
- **Lecturer:** Nan Zhang, PhD.
- **Assessment:** Oral participation, homework, presentations, compulsory attendance, term paper (graded, 5000 words).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: biweekly | 02.03.23 – 30.03.23 | Thursday | 10:15 – 13:30 | tbc | [Link]
  - Seminar: biweekly | 20.04.23 – 01.06.23 | Thursday | 10:15 – 13:30 | tbc

#### Survey Methods and Replication

- **Course Content:** Focuses on the interplay between sampling and experimental methods, statistical inference, and causal effects. Uses the German Business Panel as a case study. Participants present and discuss research papers, writing a technical report.
- **Prerequisites:** Aimed at Ph.D. students (2nd year+); basic statistics/R/Stata helpful but not necessary.
- **Assessment:** Paper (technical report) (optional), Presentation (50 %), Class Participation (50 %).
- **Schedule:**
  - Lecture: | 14.02.23 – 30.05.23 | Tuesday | 10:15 – 11:45 | B 144, A5, 6 – B
  - Lecture: | 14.02.23 – 30.05.23 | Tuesday | 10:15 – 11:45 | B 144, A5, 6 – B

#### Interdisciplinary Research Methods

- **Course Content:** A comprehensive course designed to foster interdisciplinary research. It involves four building blocks: 1) Kick-Off (presenting an interdisciplinary paper idea); 2) Mini-Research-Day (presenting own work and discussing a peer's work); 3) Science Speed Dating (developing collaborative proposals); 4) Project Presentations & Writeups.
- **Prerequisites:** None specified (Elective, counts as 'Bridge Course').
- **Assessment:** Pass/fail based on full participation in all four blocks, including a final written proposal.
- **Schedule:** (Detailed dates provided in the original document, covering Jan-June 2023).
