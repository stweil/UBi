## Sociology Courses

### Dissertation Proposal Workshop

- **Course Content:** Provides support and crucial feedback on writing a dissertation proposal. This proposal serves as a research outline detailing the doctoral thesis project, including research motivation, theoretical/empirical literature review, theoretical framework development, methodology specification, and planned empirical analysis. Students must be prepared to address questions regarding the question's interest, importance, and scholarly contribution.
- **Prerequisites:** Crafting Social Science Research, Literature Review.
- **Assessment:** Information on submission (8 ECTS) is available in the CDSS regulations section.
- **Schedule:**
  - Workshop: Further dates tbd | 13.02.24 | Tuesday | 10:15 – 11:45 | Zoom | [Link]

### Causal Inference in Social Science

- **Course Content:** Addresses the challenge of establishing credible causal effects in empirical analyses. The course introduces key conceptual and methodological approaches, including the potential outcomes model, experimental designs, matching and regression, instrumental variables, regression discontinuity designs, and difference-in-differences and fixed effects.
- **Lecturer:** Prof. Marc Ratkovic, PhD.
- **Prerequisites:** None specified.
- **Assessment:** Active participation, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Workshop: 13.02.24 – 28.05.24 | Tuesday | 08:30 – 10:00 | 211 in B6, 30–32

### CDSS Sociology Workshop

- **Course Content:** A support workshop for CDSS doctoral candidates in sociology on their ongoing dissertation project. Students are expected to play two roles: providing feedback to peers and presenting their own work to receive feedback.
- **Prerequisites:** Mandatory for first to third-year CDSS Sociology students; recommended (no credit) for later CDSS doctoral candidates.
- **Schedule:**
  - Workshop: 13.02.24 – 28.05.24 | Tuesday | 17:15 – 18:45 | 209 in B6, 30–32

### Weekly Colloquium Attendance

- **Course Content:** CDSS doctoral students in political science and sociology can choose freely which weekly colloquium to attend. Attendance is required in years two and three of doctoral studies. Options include:
  - MZES Colloquium A “European Societies and their Integration”
  - MZES Colloquium B “European Political Systems and their Integration”
  - Alternatively: Mannheim Research Colloquium on Survey Methods (MaRCS) or the MZES Social Science Data Lab.
- **Prerequisites:** CSSR, Literature Review.
- **Schedule:** (Details to be checked via the [MZES web page](http://www.mzes.uni-mannheim.de/d7/en/news/events/colloquium))

### Advanced Quantitative Methods (Longitudinal Data Analysis)

- **Course Content:** Provides a broad overview of methods for longitudinal data analysis, focusing on panel data. It covers the advantages of panel data, main models (pooled OLS, fixed effects, random effects, first-differences), panel generalized linear models, and event history analysis.
  - **Lecture:** Overview of methods.
  - **Tutorial:** Applying longitudinal data analysis methods using R to real survey data.
- **Lecturer:** Dr. Danielle Martin.
- **Prerequisites:** Some basic knowledge of statistical inference and R is required.
- **Assessment:** Lecture – Three quizzes (two must pass), regular attendance, written examination (graded, closed-book). Tutorial – Homework (pass at least six of eight), Assignments.
- **Schedule:**
  - Lecture: 12.02.24 – 27.05.24 | Monday | 10:15 – 11:45 | B 143 in A5, 6 entrance B | [Link]
  - Tutorial: 13.02.24 – 28.05.24 | Tuesday | 15:30 – 17:00 | B 143 in A5, 6 entrance B | [Link]

### Comparative Survey Research

- **Course Content:** Teaches fundamentals of comparative survey research, enabling cross-national/cross-cultural analysis. The course covers implementing and conducting comparative surveys, focusing on sources of error from design decisions. Examples include ESS, EVS, GGS, PIAAC, and WVS.
- **Prerequisites:** Some basic knowledge of statistical inference and R is required.
- **Assessment:** Active participation, homework assignments/oral presentations, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: 16.02.24 – 31.05.24 | Friday | 10:15 – 11:45 | A 102 in B6, 23–25 | [Link]

### Generative AI in the Social Sciences

- **Course Content:** Introduces selected topics relating to Generative AI in the social sciences. Readings and activities provide insight into the current research status, helping to determine open questions and future perspectives.
- **Lecturer:** Prof. Joe Sakshaug.
- **Prerequisites:** None specified.
- **Assessment:** Term paper (5,000 words max.) requiring either an empirical study using Generative AI or a critical literature review of the topic.
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: 16.02.24 – 31.05.24 | Friday | 08:30 – 10:00 | Online

### Survey Methods (GESIS Summer School)

- **Course Content:** (Note: This is a reference to the GESIS Summer School, not a specific course module.) The GESIS summer school takes place in Cologne from 24 July to 16 August. Detailed information is available on the [GESIS website](https://www.gesis.org/en/gesis-training/what-we-offer/summer-school-in-survey-methodology).
- **Prerequisites:** CDSS doctoral students have privileged access to GESIS workshops, exempt from course fees\*.
- **Note:** Contact the Center Manager before registering and mention CDSS doctoral student status.

### Natural Experiments in Political Science

- **Course Content:** Focuses on how natural experiments allow drawing causal inferences from observational data by leveraging "as-if" random assignment to real-world interventions. The course aims to equip students with the skills to recognize and evaluate potential natural experimental designs.
- **Lecturer:** Nan Zhang, PhD.
- **Prerequisites:** None specified.
- **Assessment:** Term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**

### Comparative Political Behavior

- **Course Content:** Addresses conceptual and methodological issues in measurement and comparison in comparative political research, including defining what is 'similar' or 'different'.
- **Prerequisites:** None specified.

### Political Economy of International Organizations (IOs)

- **Course Content:** Studies the political economy of major IOs (UN, World Bank, IMF, EU) using quantitative methods for causal inference. Students examine how these organizations operate, make decisions, and affect political/economic outcomes.
- **Prerequisites:** None specified.
- **Assessment:** Active participation, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: 13.02.24 – 28.05.24 | Tuesday | 13:45 – 15:15 | B 317 in A5, 6 entrance B | [Link]

### Comparative Government and Institutions

- **Course Content:** Overview of theoretical concepts and research findings in Comparative Government, focusing on the role of political institutions and their impact on decision-making. Themes include electoral institutions, party competition, and coalition governance.
