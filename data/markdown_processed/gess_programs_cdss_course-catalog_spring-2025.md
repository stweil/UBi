## Course Offerings (Spring 2025)

### Sociology Department

#### 1. Dissertation Proposal Workshop

- **Course Content:** Provides support and crucial feedback on writing a dissertation proposal. The proposal must outline the research project, including motivation, theoretical/empirical survey, theoretical framework, methodology, and planned empirical analysis. Students must be prepared to defend the question's interest, importance, and scholarly contribution.
- **Prerequisites:** Crafting Social Science Research, Literature Review.
- **Assessment:** N/A (Workshop format).
- **Schedule:**
  - Workshop: Further dates TBD | 11.02.25 | Tuesday | 10:15 – 11:45 | Zoom | [Link]
- **Note:** Further meeting dates will be determined during the first session. Only the first meeting is online; the remainder is in person. Submission guidelines (8 ECTS) are available in the [CDSS regulations section](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/gess/programs/cdss/student-information/program-regulations/).

#### 2. Causal Inference Methods

- **Course Content:** Addresses cause and effect in social science. Introduces key conceptual and methodological approaches to causal inference: potential outcomes model, experimental designs, matching and regression, instrumental variables, regression discontinuity designs, difference-in-differences, and fixed effects.
- **Lecturer:** Prof. Marc Ratkovic, PhD.
- **Assessment:** Active participation, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Workshop: 11.02.25 – 27.05.25 | Tuesday | 08:30 – 10:00 | 211 in B6, 30–32
- **Note:** Prof. Ratkovic offers open consulting hours via Calendly (access via Portal).

#### 3. CDSS Doctoral Workshop (Sociology)

- **Course Content:** Supports CDSS doctoral candidates in sociology on their ongoing dissertation project. Students are expected to play two roles: providing feedback to peers and presenting their own work.
- **Prerequisites:** None specified.
- **Assessment:** N/A.
- **Schedule:**
  - Workshop: 11.02.25 – 27.05.25 | Tuesday | 17:15 – 18:45 | 209 in B6, 30–32 | [Link]

#### 4. Weekly Colloquium Attendance

- **Course Content:** CDSS doctoral students in Political Science and Sociology can choose one weekly colloquium to attend. Attendance is mandatory in years two and three.
  - Options include: MZES Colloquium A (“European Societies and their Integration”), MZES Colloquium B (“European Political Systems and their Integration”), Mannheim Research Colloquium on Survey Methods (MaRCS), or the MZES Social Science Data Lab.
  - Details are available on the [MZES web page](http://www.mzes.uni-mannheim.de/d7/en/news/events/colloquium).
- **Prerequisites:** CSSR, Literature Review.
- **Schedule:** Varies by chosen colloquium.

#### 5. Academic Paper Writing Workshop

- **Course Content:** Provides guidance and constructive feedback on writing academic papers in English. Students learn writing/revision techniques and apply them to their own texts, aiming to improve at least one paper to a publishable standard.
- **Assessment:** Term paper.
- **Schedule:**
  - Workshop: 13.02.25 – 22.05.25 | Thursday | 12:00 – 13:30 | B 317 in A5, 6 entrance B | [Link]

### Cross-Disciplinary & Methods Courses

#### 1. Computational Social Science (CSS)

- **Course Content:** An introduction to CSS, which intersects social science, data science, and computer science. Students learn foundational theories, methods (data management, ML, visualization, statistical analysis), and ethical/legal challenges. The course is application-oriented, requiring implementation in R.
- **Lecturer:** Prof. Sebastian Stier.
- **Assessment:** Regular small assignments (programming homework, project); compulsory attendance; written term paper (max. 5000 words), due July 31, 2024.
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: Biweekly | 12.02.25 – 09.04.25 | Wednesday | 09:15 – 12:30 | tbc | [Link]

#### 2. Experimental Research Designs

- **Course Content:** Overview of experimental methods (survey, field, lab-in-the-field, lab experiments) for causal identification. Discusses best practices like open science and ethics. Students learn to design their own experiments.
- **Lecturer:** Dr. Sandra Morgenstern.
- **Assessment:** Weekly preparation of discussion questions, one presentation, active participation; Graded: Presentation of Exposé and research design seminar paper.
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: 12.02.25 – 28.05.25 | Wednesday | 13:45 – 15:15 | A103 in B6, 23–25 | [Link]

#### 3. Survey Design Fundamentals

- **Course Content:** Teaches fundamentals of survey design, covering implementation steps, design decisions, and sources of error. Uses large-scale surveys (ALLBUS, ESS, EVS, SOEP) for illustration.
- **Assessment:** Active participation, homework/oral presentations, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: 13.02.25 – 22.05.25 | Thursday | 13:45 – 15:15 | A103 in B6, 23–25 | [Link]

#### 4. Generative AI in the Social Sciences

- **Course Content:** Introduces selected topics relating to Generative AI. Students gain insight into the current research status, determining open questions.
- **Lecturer:** Prof. Joe Sakshaug.
- **Assessment:** Term paper (5,000 words max.) requiring either an empirical study using GenAI or a critical literature review.
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: 14.02.25 – 30.05.25 | Friday | 08:30 – 10:00 | Online | [Link]
- **Prerequisites:** Basic knowledge of statistical inference and R.

#### 5. Longitudinal Data Analysis (Panel Data)

- **Course Content:** Broad overview of methods for panel data analysis (pooled OLS, fixed/random effects, first-differences, panel GLMs, event history analysis).
- **Lecturer:** Dr. Danielle Martin.
- **Assessment:** Lecture: Three quizzes (two must pass), regular attendance, written exam (graded, closed-book). Tutorial: Must pass 6 out of 8 homework assignments.
- **Schedule:**
  - Lecture: 10.02.25 – 26.05.25 | Monday | 10:15 – 11:45 | B 143 in A5, 6 entrance B
  - Tutorial: 11.02.25 – 27.05.25 | Tuesday | 15:30 – 17:00 | B 143 in A5, 6 entrance B

#### 6. Advanced Statistical Modeling (GLMs & Classification)

- **Course Content:** Overview of standard/advanced linear models (multiple regression, regularization), generalized linear models (logistic, Poisson), and supervised/unsupervised classification methods (discriminant analysis, clustering). Models are introduced with mathematical foundations and applied using R.
- **Assessment:** Participation and written exam (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: 10.02.25 – 26.05.25 | Monday | 10:15 – 11:45 | 108 CIP Pool in B6, 30–32

#### 7. Item Response Theory (IRT)

- **Course Content:** Introduction to IRT with basic and advanced models for dichotomous and polytomous items (Rasch model, partial credit model, etc.). Covers formal equations, estimation techniques, and practical exercises using R.
- **Prerequisites:** Basic knowledge and experience in R (including data management).
- **Assessment:** Presentation of IRT analysis.
- **Schedule:**
  - Workshop: Block Seminar (Specific dates listed in original document).

#### 8. Digital Transformations of Work

- **Course Content:** Examines digital transformations in work and society, addressing conceptualization, characteristics, measurement, drivers, and consequences for individuals and families.
- **Assessment:** Regular small assignments; compulsory attendance; written term paper (graded, max. 5000 words), due July 15, 2025.
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: Varies (e.g., 12.02.25 | Wednesday | 08:30 – 11:45 | tbc)

#### 9. Housing and Social Inequalities

- **Course Content:** Examines how housing reflects and amplifies social inequalities across the life course. Supplements the British perspective with materials on housing issues in other European countries, particularly Germany.
- **Lecturer:** Malgorzata Mikucka, PhD.
- **Assessment:** N/A.
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: 13.02.25 – 22.05.25 | Thursday | 15:30 – 17:00 | B318 in A5, 6 entrance A

#### 10. Social Network Analysis (SNA)

- **Course Content:** General introduction to SNA, focusing heavily on political sociology examples. Treats networks both as a theoretical approach and a methodological toolkit. Covers concepts like weak ties, brokerage, and diffusion.
- **Lecturer:** Benjamin Rohr.
- **Assessment:** Regular & active participation, formulation of questions/comments to literature, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: 10.02.25 – 26.05.25 | Monday | 13:45 – 15:15 | tbc | [Link]

#### 11. Comparative Political Behavior

- **Course Content:** Advanced introduction to theoretical approaches and key concepts in comparative political behavior, building on a multi-level perspective to analyze micro-level processes feeding into macro-level political processes.
- **Assessment:** Regular participation recommended, mandatory reading, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Lecture: 10.02.25 – 26.05.25 | Monday | 10:15 – 11:45 | B 244 in A5, 6 entrance B | [Link]

#### 12. International Political Economy & Causal Inference

- **Course Content:** Applies statistical methods for causal inference to international political economy topics. Covers experiments, natural experiments, DiD, RD, and IVs.
- **Assessment:** Active participation, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: 13.02.25 – 22.05.25 | Thursday | 12:00 – 13:30 | A301 in B6, 23–25

#### 13. Economic Inequality

- **Course Content:** Studies economic inequality from a political economy perspective. Covers concepts, global trends, determinants (political/economic factors), and implications for political/economic outcomes, with a focus on quantitative methods for causal inference.
- **Assessment:** Active participation, term paper (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: 13.02.25 – 22.05.25 | Thursday | 10:15 – 11:45 | B 318 in A5, 6 entrance B

#### 14. UN Peace Operations

- **Course Content:** Examines the problems and possibilities of UN peace operations. Covers the evolution of mandates from ceasefires to civilian protection (POC), and the role of peacekeepers in international security.
- **Lecturer:** Prof. Melanie Sauter.
- **Assessment:** Term paper (max. 8000 words, graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Seminar: 12.02.25 – 28.05.25 | Wednesday | 10:15 – 11:45 | C012 in A5, 6 entrance C

#### 15. Global Governance & International Politics

- **Course Content:** Surveys academic debates on international politics, including sources of war/peace/terrorism, international organizations, and transnational civil society.
- **Assessment:** Written exam (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Lecture: 10.02.25 – 26.05.25 | Monday | 13:45 – 15:15 | B 143 in A5, 6 entrance B

#### 16. Comparative Government & Institutions

- **Course Content:** Overview of theoretical concepts and research findings in Comparative Government, focusing on the role of political institutions (electoral systems, party competition, coalition governance) and their impact on decision-making.
- **Lecturer:** Or Tuttnauer, PhD.
- **Assessment:** Lecture of recommended texts, written exam (graded).
- **Schedule:**
  - Lecture: 11.02.25 – 27.05.25 | Tuesday | 10:15 – 11:45 | C217 in A5, 6 entrance C
