# Keynote Program Highlights

This document summarizes the key presentations given at the event, covering topics from Internet of Things (IoT) data quality to advanced deep learning models and digital product design.

## Daniela Nicklas (Otto-Friedrich-Universität Bamberg)

### Who watches the watchmen? Online data quality in future IOT applications

**Abstract:** In many "Internet of Things" applications, sensor data plays a vital role for monitoring real-world phenomena. However, sensor data can have multiple data quality issues; to make things worse, these issues are context-dependent, i.e., they change over time. This keynote highlights some data management challenges that need to be addressed for future IoT infrastructures, with a focus on data quality in sensor-based applications. It illustrates these challenges with applications and research topics from the joint research programme „FutureIOT“, funded by the Bavarian Research Foundation and introduces the „Living Lab Bamberg“, an open platform for sensor-based research and long-running experiments.

**Video:** [Keynote 1](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/lwda-2018/videos/keynotes/#c90594)

## Kerstin Bach (NTNU Trondheim)

### Personalized Self-Management: A Case-Based Reasoning Application for Low Back Pain Patients

**Abstract:** This talk presents the selfBACK decision support system (DSS) project, which uses Case-Based Reasoning to support patients with low back pain. The DSS, developed over the last two years, will now be tested in a pilot study and a multisite randomized controlled trial. The selfBACK DSS empowers the patient to facilitate, improve, and reinforce self-management of LBP. Specifically, it assists the patient in deciding and reinforcing appropriate actions after consulting a healthcare professional. The decision support is delivered via a smartphone app, providing advice tailored to the patient's symptom state, symptom progression, goal-setting, and characteristics, including data from a physical activity-detecting wristband.

**Video:** [Keynote 2](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/lwda-2018/videos/keynotes/#c90598)

## Frank Giesler (Vice President, Capgemini Consulting)

### Digital Product Design: Practical Experiences from Developing Data-Driven Digital Products

**Abstract:** Machine Learning enables the development of innovative and novel digital products. The foundation for this lies in integrating different data sources and intelligently linking them with self-learning mechanisms. However, user acceptance is absolutely critical. Acceptance research shows that new and "voluntary" software must offer a 10-fold higher benefit than existing solutions to be adopted into daily use. Frank Giesler presents a proven approach that ensures successful data-driven digital product development through the close involvement of future users.

**Video:** [Keynote 3](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/lwda-2018/videos/keynotes/#c90599)

## Stephan Mandt (Disney Research)

### Finding Hidden Structure in Data with Deep Probabilistic Models

**Abstract:** This overview covers exciting recent developments in deep probabilistic modeling, which combines deep neural networks with probabilistic models for unsupervised learning. These models are capable of synthesizing artificial data that highly resembles real training data, fooling both machine learning classifiers and humans. They have numerous applications in creative tasks, such as voice, image, or video synthesis and manipulation. Furthermore, combining neural networks with strong priors results in flexible yet highly interpretable models for finding hidden structure in large datasets. The presentation summarizes the group’s activities in this space, including measuring semantic shifts of individual words over hundreds of years, summarizing audience reactions to movies, and generating artificial video sequences with partial control over content and dynamics.
