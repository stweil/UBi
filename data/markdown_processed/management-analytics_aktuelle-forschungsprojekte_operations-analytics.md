# Operations Analytics Research Projects

This section details current research projects focusing on the application of advanced analytical methods—including process mining, stochastic modeling, and labor economics—to optimize complex operational processes across various industries.

## 1. Employee Responses to Error in Monitored Environments

**Focus:** Analyzing how production staff react to errors when monitored by emergent technologies.
**Authors:** [Hartmut Höhle](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/hoehle/), Florian Pethig, [Fareed Zandkarimi](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/hoehle/team/wissenschaftliche-mitarbeiter-innen/alumni/fareed-zandkarimi/)
**Cooperation Partner:** Aalberts Material Technology GmbH
**Description:**
This project uses process mining to measure production-level behavior in response to reclamations. By analyzing core-production data from a large company over three years, the research employs a Difference-in-Difference experiment. The study contributes significantly by applying statistical methods to production-level data and using process mining to investigate employee responses to errors. The expected outcomes offer practitioners new insights for making effective decisions before or after an error is reported. Data collection involved processing 2.5 million production cases via the company’s ERP system, requiring the development of over 500 lines of SQL code and a dedicated Java artifact for the Dif-in-Dif analysis, with final analysis conducted using Stata.

## 2. Mergers and Acquisitions and Labor Reallocation

**Focus:** Studying the impact of mergers and acquisitions (M&A) on the labor force structure in Germany.
**Authors:** Britta Gehrke, [Ernst Maug](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/maug/), Stefan Obernberger, Christoph Schneider, [Peter Severin](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/maug/team/alumni/peter-severin/)
**Cooperation Partner:** Institut für Arbeit und Beruf (IAB)
**Description:**
The research investigates labor reallocation following M&A activities. Findings indicate substantial restructuring, with target companies losing over half their workforce within two years post-acquisition. The study argues that merged firms build "knowledge-based hierarchies," which efficiently utilize scarce skills among managers and highly-skilled employees, correlating with improved productivity. The research finds limited support for alternative theories suggesting that acquisitions primarily address skill shortages or create internal labor markets.

## 3. Performance Evaluation in Time-Dependent Queues with Retrials

**Focus:** Developing models for multi-server queueing systems that account for customer retrials.
**Authors:** [Ömer Özümerzifon](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/stolletz/), Benjamin Legros, [Raik Stolletz](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/stolletz/)
**Cooperation Partner:** EM Normandie
**Description:**
Queueing systems are vital in service sectors like healthcare, repair facilities, and call centers. This project addresses the complexity where customers may leave the queue due to impatience but later re-enter as retrials. The research focuses on the stationary and time-dependent performance evaluation of such multi-server systems. The team develops and applies a Stationary Backlog-Carryover (SBC) approach and a modified Stationary Independent Period-by-Period (SIPP) approach to approximate key performance measures, demonstrating the measurable impact of the retrial time distribution.

## 4. Process Mining in Procurement Processes (P2P)

**Focus:** Applying process mining methods to identify and quantify inefficiencies within procurement processes (e.g., Purchase-to-Pay, P2P).
**Authors:** [Prof. Dr. Christoph Bode](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/bode/), [Prof. Dr. Hartmut Höhle](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/hoehle/), [Jun.-Prof. Dr. Jana-Rebecca Rehse](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/rehse/), [Jonas Ronellenfitsch](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/bode/team/alumni/dr-ronellenfitsch-jonas/), [Fareed Zandkarimi](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/hoehle/)
**Cooperation Partners:** Schott AG, Hilti AG, HeidelbergCement AG, and Heidelberger Druckmaschinen AG
**Description:**
Procurement processes are critical, as inefficiencies can cause significant delays and costs. This interdisciplinary team applies process mining—the analysis of process execution data—to identify, measure, and explain operational inefficiencies in P2P processes. By analyzing data from three large industrial companies, the research develops new Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) that precisely quantify process inefficiency. These KPIs can be used to track temporal patterns or assess the impact of external events, such as the COVID-19 pandemic, on procurement workflows.

## 5. Analytical Approaches for Digital Production Optimization

**Focus:** Developing advanced analytical models for performance evaluation and optimization in dynamic semiconductor manufacturing processes.
**Authors:** [Johannes Diefenbach](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/stolletz/team/promotionen-und-postdocs/dr-johannes-diefenbach/), [Fikri Karaesmen](https://mysite.ku.edu.tr/fkaraesmen/), [Justus Arne Schwarz](https://www.uni-regensburg.de/wirtschaftswissenschaften/fakultaet/org/produktionsmanagement/team/prof-dr-justus-arne-schwarz), [Raik Stolletz](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/en/stolletz/team/)
**Cooperation Partners:** Koc University (Istanbul) and Robert Bosch GmbH
**Description:**
This project contributes to the "Productive 4.0" initiative by developing new analytical models and fast approximation methods for optimizing dynamic semiconductor manufacturing processes. Using prescriptive analytics, the research generates insights on product introduction into the production system and market, especially when yield is stochastic. This work is recognized as an ECSEL Lighthouse Initiative, investigating how ICT can enhance production efficiency across the entire semiconductor value chain and product life cycle.
