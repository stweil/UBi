# People Analytics Research Projects

This section details current research projects focusing on the intersection of data analytics, human capital, and organizational change.

## Private Equity Buyouts and Employee Health

**Authors:**

- Pilar Garcia-Gomez
- [Ernst Maug](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/en/maug/)
- Stefan Obernberger

**Cooperation Partners:**
Chair of Corporate Finance

**Description:**
This research examines the role of employee health in Private Equity buyouts. Using employee-level data covering employment, wages, and medical prescriptions, the study conducts matched-sample difference-in-differences estimations involving over 55,000 buyout employees. The findings indicate that employees with lower health status prior to the buyout face the most substantial losses in income and employment during and after the buyout. While buyouts do not measurably impact health outcomes or expenditures, they significantly influence career paths. Specifically, the health of unemployed employees deteriorates, whereas those who secure new jobs show improvement. The study concludes that buyout-related restructuring has a stronger negative impact on the human capital of employees with pre-existing health problems.

## Rethinking the Gold Standard With Multi-armed Bandits: Machine Learning Allocation Algorithms for Experiments

**Authors:**

- [Prof. Torsten Biemann](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/biemann/team/lehrstuhl/prof-dr-torsten-biemann/)
- Chris Kaibel

**Cooperation Partners:**
Chair of Business Administration, Human Resource Management and Leadership

**Description:**
Traditional experimental design often allocates subjects randomly and equally to different treatment conditions, failing to utilize new information gathered during the experiment. Drawing on methodologies from computer science and medicine, this project proposes a machine learning algorithm for subject allocation in randomized experiments. The research specifically discusses a Bayesian multi-armed bandit algorithm for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and compares its efficiency against fixed, balanced subject allocation using Monte Carlo simulations. The findings suggest that allocation based on Bayesian multi-armed bandits is more efficient and ethical in most settings. Recommendations for researchers are provided, along with a discussion of limitations.

- **Publication:** Results were accepted for publication by the journal *Organisational Research Methods*: [https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1094428119854153](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1094428119854153)
- **Tool:** A related tool for deploying Multi-armed bandits is available here: [https://randomization-with-bandits.shinyapps.io/bayesian-bandit/](https://randomization-with-bandits.shinyapps.io/bayesian-bandit/)

## Who Should Judge Me When I’m Different? Hiring Algorithms and Applicant Perceptions

**Authors:**

- [Prof. Torsten Biemann](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/biemann/team/lehrstuhl/prof-dr-torsten-biemann/)
- [Dr. Chris Kaibel](https://lsz.at/speaker/chris-kaibel)
- [Dr. Irmela Koch-Bayran](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/biemann/team/wissenschaftliche-mitarbeiter/dr-irmela-koch-bayram/)
- [Max Mühlenbock](https://www.goinger-kreis.de/dr-max-muehlenbock.html)

**Description:**
While algorithm-based decisions appear promising for data-driven hiring, the key question remains whether these systems help organizations attract and recruit a diverse workforce with unique talents. Building on procedural justice theory, two experimental studies were conducted to examine how applicants perceive selection processes managed by algorithms versus human decision-makers. The research found that applicant perceptions are not automatically negative or positive; rather, they vary depending on the outcome variables and the applicants' personal experiences and beliefs. While algorithms may help attract applicants who have faced discrimination, they appear to discourage applicants with unique characteristics. Implications and future research directions are discussed.

- **Publication:** Outcomes were published in the "Best Paper Proceedings" of the Academy of Management 2019.
