# Research Portfolio: Data Science, Marketing, and Digital Transformation Projects

This document summarizes various research projects focusing on advanced data analytics, market behavior, digital transformation, and educational technology.

## Learning Analytics and Educational Technology

### LeAP – Learning Analytics Profile

**Authors:** [Dirk Ifenthaler](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/ifenthaler/)
**Cooperation Partners:** Chair of Learning, Design and Technology
**Description:** The field of learning analytics is generating interest in data and computer science as well as educational science, becoming an important aspect of modern e-learning environments. Implementing learning analytics into existing legacy systems in learning environments with high data privacy concerns is quite a challenge. This research shows how a learning analytics application has been implemented into such an existing university environment by adding a plugin to the local digital learning environment which injects user-centric information to specific objects within students’ and teachers’ learning environment. The LeAP platform can be used in various learning and teaching scenarios in order to provide adaptive support for learners and teachers.

## Marketing, Advertising, and Consumer Behavior

### Advertising Over Time: Moving in the speed-of-life?

**Authors:** Leonie Gehrmann, [Florian Stahl](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/quantitativemarketing/researchgroup/florianstahl/)
**Cooperation Partners:** Hebrew University (Israel), Economist
**Description:** We analyze how print advertising content has adapted to changes in speed-of-life, best defined as the “rapidity and density of experiences, meanings, perceptions, and activities” (Werner et al. 1985), over time. The development of our image mining algorithm promises relevance for a variety of alternative use cases, since it is easily extendable to images beyond the print advertisements in our data set. Similarly, the analysis could be continued for other magazines, as well as social media postings, further refining our algorithm. Our findings also provide insights into the existing competition for consumer attention due to increasing advertising clutter.

### Variation in review writing behavior across time-of-the-day, week day, season, weather conditions, a city’s life-quality and other factors

**Authors:** [Andreas Bayerl](https://www.eur.nl/en/people/andreas-bayerl), [Florian Stahl](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/quantitativemarketing/researchgroup/florianstahl/), Jacob Goldenberg
**Cooperation Partners:** IDC Israel, Kununu.de
**Description:** We analyze a dataset of over 10 million online reviews from two different platforms collecting User-Generated Content to find differences in review writing behavior across time-of-the-day, week day, season, weather conditions, a city’s life quality and other factors. Online reviews are meant to be an unbiased source of information, but we show how they can be influenced by externalities. Methods employed include web scraping, Natural Language Processing (NLP) for data analysis, and Machine Learning to develop a model predicting review scores based on externalities.

### The Impact of the Offered Product Mix on the Acquisition, Usage and Retention of Information Goods

**Authors:** [Daniela Schmitt](https://www.novasbe.unl.pt/en/faculty-research/faculty/faculty-detail/id/1170/daniela-schmitt), Raghuram Iyengar, [Florian Stahl](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/quantitativemarketing/researchgroup/florianstahl/)
**Cooperation Partners:** Wharton School (Philadelphia)
**Description:** A primary challenge for information goods providers (e.g., news websites) is balancing free users and paid subscribers. We address what content users consume and how inter-session consumption dynamics impact subscription likelihood. Model-free evidence suggests that increased paid content correlates with higher acquisition and retention probability, moderated by consumption volume. However, we also find a segment of users who abandon the site upon seeing increased paid content, suggesting heterogeneous effects. We specify a hidden Markov model to capture underlying behavioral dynamics.

### A friendly turn: advertising bias in the news media

**Authors:** Florens Focke, [Alexandra Niessen-Ruenzi](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/niessen-ruenzi/), [Stefan Ruenzi](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/ruenzi/)
**Cooperation Partners:** Chair of International Finance & Chair of Corporate Governance
**Description:** This paper investigates whether newspapers report more favorably about advertising corporate clients than about other firms. Our identification strategy shows that advertising leads to more positive press coverage in both local and national newspapers. This bias is particularly evident in less negative reporting after bad news events, casting doubt on the press's independence from corporate pressure.

### Prediction of Consumer Behavior

**Authors:** Yasid Soufi, [Florian Kraus](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/kraus/)
**Cooperation Partners:** Chair of Sales & Services Marketing
**Description:** Gaining insights into predictors of a customer’s purchase behavior and personal traits enables a more adaptive sales process. Utilizing data on behavioral patterns, consumer characteristics, and self-reported traits, we aim to anticipate customer behavior in an app-based context using machine learning algorithms. Insights are valuable for both customer and provider perspectives, as optimized processes lead to higher customer satisfaction and operational efficiency.

### Marketing in Finance

**Authors:** Yasid Soufi, [Florian Kraus](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/kraus/)
**Cooperation Partners:** Chair of Sales & Services Marketing
**Description:** Skillful communication with financial market participants helps develop new financing sources and improve sentiment towards a company’s value potential. High-quality Investor Relations (IR) is a critical success factor. By crawling large amounts of textual data and deploying textual analysis methodologies, we explore how marketing should communicate within the framework of Investor Relations.

## Business Administration and Taxation

### The scale of sharing transaction via online platforms and issues for taxation in the digital economy

**Authors:** Christopher Ludwig, [Christoph Spengel](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/en/spengel/), [Rainer Bräutigam](https://www.mosbach.dhbw.de/dhbw-mosbach/who-is-who/prof-dr-rainer-braeutigam/)
**Cooperation Partners:** Chair of Business Administration and Taxation II
**Description:** Using publicly accessible Airbnb data from 20 German cities, we project the annual turnover of hosts and the potential tax revenue arising from this turnover. The analysis estimates the tax revenue from income and value-added tax liability to be enormous. Given the size of the tax revenues at stake, we develop recommendations on how to adjust existing tax systems to adapt to the digital economy and ensure compliance.

### The Complexity of Multinational Business Groups

**Authors:** [Marcel Olbert](https://www.zew.de/team/mol), [Christoph Spengel](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/en/spengel/)
**Cooperation Partners:** Chair of Business Administration and Taxation II
**Description:** By tracking organizational structure development over time, this project helps understand how multinational firms structure their value chains and business models globally. Using data on financial characteristics, legal status, and shareholder structures of over 250 million firms, we investigate the determinants of organizational structures and firms’ complexity. The study attempts to explain why multinational firms employ complex structures and ambiguous legal strategies, such as founding subsidiaries in tax havens.

### Digitalization of the Tax Function

**Authors:** [Christoph Spengel](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/en/spengel/), Alexander Maedche, Sven Scheu, Sarah Winter
**Cooperation Partners:** Institute of Information Systems and Marketing (IISM) at KIT, EnBW AG
**Description:** Tax departments face new regulatory requirements to provide increasing amounts of information to tax authorities. We collaborate with a major German energy supplier to develop concepts for the digital transformation of the tax function. The project focuses on optimizing tax department processes through digital means, enhancing existing tax information systems, and developing data-driven intelligent assistant functions using Process Mining, Business Intelligence, and Data Science.

### Can European Banks’ Country-by-Country Reports Reveal Profit Shifting? An Analysis of the Information Content of EU Banks’ Disclosures

**Authors:** Verena Dutt, [Katharina Nicolay](https://www.zew.de/team/kfi), Heiko Vay, [Johannes Voget](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/voget/), [Christoph Spengel](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/en/spengel/)
**Cooperation Partners:** Chair of Business Administration & Taxation II, Taxation & Finance
**Description:** This project is relevant to political discussions about mandatory public country-by-country (CbC) reporting for large multinational firms in the EU. Using hand-collected data from mandatory CbC-reports of European banks, we analyze the amount of profits, turnover, and employees in various jurisdictions. The goal is to identify if public CbC-reporting provides incremental information on the profit shifting behavior of multinational banks compared to commercial databases like Orbis.

## Human Resources and Organizational Processes

### Competence development in VET enculturation processes

**Authors:** [Viola Deutscher](https://www.uni-goettingen.de/de/prof.+dr.+viola+deutscher/685786.html)
**Cooperation Partners:** Esther Winther, University of Duisburg-Essen; Vocational schools/companies with VET programmes
**Description:** This DFG-funded project analyzes competence development in vocational education training (VET) and relevant success factors for commercial apprentices (n = 877). It models VET enculturation processes at group and individual levels, including company learning conditions. Using a longitudinal design, a validated competence assessment is used alongside an instrument for recording enculturation conditions at the start, middle, and end of initial vocational training, generating practical findings for companies to improve educational programs.

### Driving forward while looking back: Employee responses to error amidst when monitored by emergent technologies

**Authors:** Rajiv Sabherwal, [Hartmut Höhle](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/hoehle/), [Tobias Nisius](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/hoehle/), [Florian Pethig](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/hoehle/team/wissenschaftliche-mitarbeiter/alumni/florian-pethig/), [Fareed Zandkarimi](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/hoehle/)
**Cooperation Partners:** Aalberts Material Technology GmbH
**Description:** We examine the effect of reclamations on production staff behavior using process mining. Measures collected over three years are planned for analysis using a difference-in-difference (DID) design. Applying statistical methods on production-level data combined with process mining is a major contribution. The expected outcomes provide practitioners with new insights on making effective decisions before and after an error is reported.

### „Helpful or Harmful? Negative Behavior Toward Newcomers and Welfare in Online Communities

**Authors:** [Florian Pethig](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/hoehle/team/wissenschaftliche-mitarbeiter/alumni/florian-pethig/), Kai-Lung Hui, Andreas Lanz, [Hartmut Höhle](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/hoehle/)
**Cooperation Partners:** Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, HEC Paris
**Description:** Hostility toward newcomers is common in online communities. We study a "newcomer nudge"—an intervention that informs members when interacting with a newcomer post and encourages leniency. Using a natural experiment design, we analyze posts before and after the nudge. We observe a strong increase in upvotes and responses, and a decrease in negative sentiment. Furthermore, newcomers socialized with the nudge are significantly more likely to post again in the following 12 months.
