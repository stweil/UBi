# Research Projects in Digital Technologies and Competence Development

This document summarizes two distinct research projects related to the application of technology and analytics in professional development and business strategy.

## 1. Competence Development in VET Enculturation Processes

This DFG-funded research project analyzes the development of professional competencies within Vocational Education Training (VET).

- **Authors:** [Viola Deutscher](https://www.uni-goettingen.de/de/prof.+dr.+viola+deutscher/685786.html)
- **Cooperation Partners:** Esther Winther, University of Duisburg-Essen; Vocational schools/companies with VET programmes
- **Description:** The study focuses on identifying relevant success factors for competence development among commercial apprentices ($n=877$). The project models VET enculturation processes at both group and individual levels, including an analysis of company learning conditions. A validated competence assessment is used longitudinally—at the beginning, middle, and end of initial vocational training—to generate practical findings that help companies improve their educational programs.

## 2. Maximizing Customer Lifetime Value through Strategic Channel Management

This research explores how online retailers can increase Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) by strategically managing customer channels, specifically comparing mobile app usage versus website usage.

- **Authors:** Gokhan Gecer, [Florian Kraus](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/kraus/)
- **Cooperation Partners:** Chair of Sales & Services Marketing
- **Description:** Given that mobile devices have changed online purchasing habits, increasing the use of mobile apps boosts CLV. The study proposes that online retailers should incentivize customers to use the mobile channel, potentially by offering a permanent discount. While this strategy may decrease short-term income, it can increase CLV. Using a probability-based CLV model, the research determines that discounts should be offered to customers who are either very likely or very unlikely to increase their purchasing probability. Crucially, the strategy is recommended for increasing **customer retention**, rather than for new customer acquisition.
