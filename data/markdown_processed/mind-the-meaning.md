# Mind the Meaning: The Philosophy of Psychological Expressivism

## Core Concept

This research investigates self-ascriptions of psychological states—such as "I wish that the sun were shining," "I believe that it is raining," and "I am in pain"—and posits that these statements function as **expressions**, rather than mere descriptions, of our internal mental states. The primary goal is to develop a systematic theory of psychological expressivism.

## Significance and Scope

The implications of psychological expressivism extend far beyond the philosophy of language. The research explores significant consequences for several fields:

- **Epistemology:** Examining topics like the transparency of belief and self-knowledge.
- **Philosophy of Mind and Self:** Addressing concepts such as dualism and the dual usage of the pronoun "I."
- **History of Philosophy:** Drawing parallels with foundational works, including Descartes’ *Cogito* and Kant’s "I think."
