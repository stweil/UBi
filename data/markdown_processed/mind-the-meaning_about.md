# Mind the Meaning: Philosophical Research on Self-Ascriptions

This project investigates the way we talk about ourselves—our past, present, or future—and how we report our own physiological or psychological states.

## Core Focus: Psychological Expressivism

The research specifically examines **self-ascriptions of mental states** through utterances such as:

- “I wish that the sun were shining.”
- “I believe that it is raining.”
- “I am in pain.”

The central theoretical focus is **Psychological Expressivism**. This thesis posits that such utterances do not primarily function as assertions *about* the speaker's mental states, but rather they *express* those states themselves.

## Project Objectives

While the basic concept of psychological expressivism has a long history (e.g., in Wittgenstein’s later writings), the project aims to achieve two main goals:

1. **Systematic Exposition:** To fill the gap by providing a systematic exposition of the theory.
1. **General Theory:** To embed psychological expressivism within a general theory of expressive acts.

## Applications and Insights

The idea that mental states are expressed rather than merely described by avowals offers novel insights into several key philosophical debates involving first-person reference. The theory is applied to:

- **Self-Knowledge:** Analyzing the problem of self-knowledge.
- **Puzzle Cases:** Examining complex scenarios such as Evans’ Transparency and Wittgenstein’s two uses of “I.”
- **Classical Philosophy:** Investigating the possibilities and consequences of expressivist analyses of Kant’s “I think” and Descartes’ *Cogito*.
