# Colluding Against Environmental Regulation

This document summarizes the seminar presentation on market collusion in response to environmental regulations.

## Speaker Information

**Jing Li**
*MIT Sloan School of Management*

**Bio:** Jing Li holds the William Barton Rogers Career Development Chair of Energy Economics at the MIT Sloan School of Management. Her research interests focus on energy economics and industrial organization, particularly concerning the development and adoption of new technologies. Her work includes examining compatibility and investment in electric vehicle recharging networks in the US, and cost pass-through in the E85 retail market. She received double B.Sc. degrees in Mathematics and Economics from MIT (2011) and her Ph.D. in Economics from Harvard (2017).

## Seminar Details

- **Topic:** Collusion among firms in response to imperfectly monitored environmental regulation.
- **Date:** November 15, 2021
- **Format:** Live virtual event (Registration was closed).

## Abstract: Market Collusion and Environmental Policy

The seminar investigates how firms engage in collusion to improve market profits by "shading pollution" and jointly evading noncompliance penalties.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Case Study:** The analysis quantifies the welfare effects of collusion among three German automakers regarding the reduction of Diesel Exhaust Fluid (DEF) tanks—an emission control technology required for air pollution compliance.
1. **Structural Model:** A structural model of the European automobile industry (2007–2018) was developed. The study found that while smaller DEF tanks increase pollution damages, they simultaneously improve buyer and producer surplus by freeing up valuable trunk space and reducing production costs.
1. **Economic Impact:** Jointly choosing small DEF tanks was found to reduce the automakers' expected noncompliance penalties by at least 188–976 million euros. However, this collusion ultimately reduces overall social welfare by an estimated 0.78 to 4.44 billion euros.
1. **Policy Conclusion:** The presentation concludes that environmental policy design and antitrust measures play complementary and crucial roles in protecting society from anti-competitive collusion against regulations.
