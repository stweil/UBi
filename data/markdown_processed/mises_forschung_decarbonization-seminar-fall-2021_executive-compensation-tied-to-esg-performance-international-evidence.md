# Executive Compensation Tied to ESG Performance: International Evidence

## Speaker Profile: Professor Gaizka Ormazabal

Professor Gaizka Ormazabal holds a PhD in Business from Stanford University and a PhD in Construction Engineering from Universitat Politecnica de Catalunya. He earned his Bachelor’s Degree in Civil Engineering (Ingeniería de Caminos, Canals y Puertos) from Universitat Politécnica de Catalunya.

**Research Focus:**
Professor Ormazabal's research centers on executive compensation and corporate governance. His work investigates the choice and valuation implications of various corporate governance mechanisms. His current research analyzes trends in managerial compensation, corporate governance intermediaries, financial regulation, bank accounting, and the role of corporate governance in sustainable development.

**Publications & Affiliations:**
His research has been published in leading academic journals, including the *Journal of Accounting and Economics*, *Journal of Financial Economics*, *Journal of Law and Economics*, *The Accounting Review*, *Journal of Finance*, and *Journal of Accounting Research*. His work has also been featured in popular media outlets such as *The Wall Street Journal*, *The New York Times*, and *The Economist*, and has been cited in final rulings by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.

He serves as a Research Affiliate in the Financial Economics program of the Center for Economic Policy Research (CEPR) and is a research member in the European Corporate Governance Institute (ECGI). He holds the Grupo Santander Chair of Financial Institutions and Corporate Governance and acts as the academic director at the IESE Center for Corporate Governance.

## Seminar Abstract

This seminar examines the integration of Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) performance metrics into executive compensation contracts.

The analysis documents that a growing number of publicly traded companies globally are incorporating ESG metrics into the compensation schemes for their top executives. The study links the reliance on these metrics to:

- Firm fundamentals
- The geographic location of the firms
- The prevalence of institutional shareholders

Furthermore, the findings suggest that the adoption of ESG variables in managerial performance measures is followed by measurable improvements in ESG performance and meaningful changes in the compensation structure of executives.
