# Seminar: Firm Commitments to Decarbonization

This seminar provides an empirical analysis of voluntary commitments made by publicly traded companies to reduce their carbon emissions. The discussion explores what drives these commitments and what their actual effects are.

**Key Topics Covered:**

- Analysis of two major commitment movements: the Carbon Disclosure Project (CDP) and the Science-Based Target Initiative (SBTi).
- **Main Findings:**
  1. While committing companies subsequently reduce emissions, the overall effect of these initiatives on the total emissions of all publicly traded companies (including non-committers) has been small.
  1. Companies that commit, especially those making the most ambitious commitments, tend to already have lower carbon emissions.
  1. Firm commitments to reduce emissions are less common in countries where national government commitments are already in place.
- **Conclusion:** The movements to encourage corporate commitment have been successful in engaging willing participants but face greater resistance from the companies that need to reduce their emissions the most.

## Speaker Profile: Patrick Bolton

**Patrick Bolton** is the Barbara and David Zalaznick Professor of Business at Columbia Business School.

**Academic Background & Expertise:**

- **Education:** PhD from the London School of Economics (1986); BA in Economics from the University of Cambridge; BA in Political Science from the Institut d'Etudes Politiques de Paris.
- **Career Highlights:** Held positions at the University of California at Berkeley, Harvard University, C.N.R.S., London School of Economics, Institut d'Etudes Européennes, and Princeton University.
- **Research Focus:** His primary research interests lie in contract theory and contracting issues within corporate finance and industrial organization. He focuses on the allocation of control and decision rights when long-term contracts are incomplete.
- **Areas of Application:** His work is relevant to optimal debt structure, corporate governance, optimal ownership structure, vertical integration, and constitution design. In industrial organization, he focuses on antitrust economics and potential anticompetitive effects of contracting practices.
- **Publications:** Recently published *Contract Theory* (with Mathias Dewatripont) and co-edited *Credit Markets for the Poor* (with Howard Rosenthal).
