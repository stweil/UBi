# What Kinds of Distributed Generation Technologies Defer Network Expansions? Evidence from France

**Speaker:** Frank Wolak
**Affiliation:** Stanford University
**Date:** September 20, 2021

## Seminar Abstract: Impact of Distributed Generation on the Electricity Grid

This seminar analyzes the relationship between investments in distributed generation and the resulting hourly net injections into the distribution grid across over 2,000 substations in France, covering the period from 2005 to 2018.

**Key Findings:**

- **Solar PV:** A 1 MW increase in solar PV capacity showed no statistically significant impact on the highest percentiles of the annual distribution of hourly net injections.
- **Wind Power:** A 1 MW increase in wind capacity is predicted to reduce the 99th percentile of the annual distribution of hourly net injections by 0.037 MWh.
- **Other Technologies:** Investments in distributed small hydro, non-renewable thermal, or renewable thermal generation units predict an almost five times larger MWh reduction in the 99th percentile of the annual distribution of hourly net injections.
- **Ramp Rates:**
  - Distributed solar PV and wind capacity predict substantial absolute changes in both extremes of the annual distribution of hourly ramp rates of net injections.
  - For the remaining three distributed generation technologies, a 1 MW capacity increase does not predict a non-zero change in any percentile of the annual distribution of hourly ramp rates.

**Conclusion:**
The results suggest that, at least for the case of France, increases in distributed solar and wind capacity are more likely to lead to *increases*, rather than decreases, in necessary distribution network investments.

## Speaker Biography: Frank A. Wolak

Frank A. Wolak is the Holbrook Working Professor of Commodity Price Studies in the Department of Economics at Stanford University.

**Areas of Specialization:**

- Industrial Organization
- Econometric Theory

**Research Focus:**
His recent work examines methods for introducing competition into infrastructure industries, including telecommunications, electricity, water delivery, and postal services. He assesses the impact of these competition policies on consumer and producer welfare.

**Previous Roles:**

- From 1998 to 2011: Chair of the Market Surveillance Committee for the California Independent System Operator (CAISO) for the electricity supply industry.
- Research Associate of the National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER).
- Currently directs the Program on Energy and Sustainable Development (PESD) at the Freeman-Spogli Institute (FSI) for International Studies and co-directs the Natural Gas Initiative at Stanford University.
- Also served as a member of the Emissions Market Advisory Committee (EMAC) for California’s Market for Greenhouse Gas Emissions allowances (2012–2013).
