## Seminar Abstract

Decarbonization requires a completely transformed electricity grid. This seminar analyzes a long-run model that captures crucial aspects of the electricity industry, including:

- Time-varying electricity demand.
- Intermittency of renewable sources.
- Optimal use of storage technologies.
- Entry and exit of generation and storage capacity.

The analysis highlights that long-run effects can differ significantly from short-run intuition. Key findings include:

- A carbon tax can paradoxically increase electricity consumption.
- Cheaper storage might decrease renewable capacity by lowering prices when renewables are operating.
- Increased electricity demand (e.g., electrification) may reduce emissions if it stimulates sufficient renewable capacity entry.

Using hourly data from the U.S. market, the model is calibrated to evaluate various decarbonization policies. The research indicates that a carbon price of $150 or more essentially eliminates carbon emissions. Furthermore, while a renewable subsidy performs better than a nuclear subsidy for modest decarbonization goals, this ranking reverses for ambitious goals. Policies promoting transmission or storage are unlikely to yield significant benefits unless paired with renewable subsidies. Electrifying 100% of car miles traveled could increase electricity-sector carbon emissions by 23–27% if charging occurs at night, but could decrease emissions if charging occurs during the day.

The NBER working paper detailing this research can be found [here](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30082).

## Speaker Biography

Erin Mansur is an economist specializing in energy economics, environmental economics, and industrial organization. His research interests include:

- Labor market implications of hydrofracturing.
- Environmental effects of electric vehicles.
- How natural gas prices impact electricity markets.
- The effect of mergers on competition within electricity markets.

He teaches courses on energy economics and serves as a research associate at the National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER).
