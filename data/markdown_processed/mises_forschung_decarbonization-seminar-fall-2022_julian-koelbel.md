### Seminar Abstract

This seminar examines the novel phenomenon of sustainability-linked bonds (SLBs). The coupon of these bonds is linked to the issuer achieving a predetermined sustainability performance target.

The analysis estimates the yield differential between SLBs and non-sustainable counterfactuals by matching bonds from the same issuer. Key findings include:

- In most cases, investors pay for the improvement in sustainability, while issuers benefit from a sustainability premium.
- The sustainability premium is shown to be larger for bonds with a higher coupon step-up and for callable bonds.
- Some SLB issuers benefit from a 'free lunch,' as their financial savings exceed the potential penalty, and they retain a call option to reduce this penalty.

While the findings suggest that most SLBs incentivize sustainability improvements by offering a lower cost of capital, the presentation also notes that some companies may issue SLBs purely to signal commitment without realizing a true sustainability premium. This suggests that SLBs can potentially be a form of greenwashing if issued solely for financial optimization without genuine commitment to sustainability improvements.

*The corresponding paper can be found [here](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4007629).*

### Speaker Biography

Dr. Julian Kölbel is an assistant professor of sustainable finance at the Center for Financial Services Innovation at the University of St. Gallen. He is also a research affiliate at MIT Sloan, where he is a co-founder of the Aggregate Confusion Project.

His research focuses on:

- The real-world impact of sustainable investing.
- Analysis of Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) metrics.
- Investor preferences for sustainability.

Julian has studied at ETH Zurich and the University of Oxford, and completed a Post-Doc at MIT Sloan. Professionally, he has worked at Bank Vontobel and RepRisk AG, and currently serves on the investment committee at the Swiss pension fund Abendrot.
