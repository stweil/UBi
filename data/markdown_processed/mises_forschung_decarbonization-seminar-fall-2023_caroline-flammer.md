## Speaker Biography

Caroline Flammer is a Professor of International and Public Affairs and of Climate at Columbia University, holding joint appointments at the School of International and Public Affairs (SIPA) and the Climate School. She is also a Research Associate at the National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER) and a Research Member at the European Corporate Governance Institute (ECGI).

**Expertise:**
Caroline specializes in sustainable investing and impact investing, researching how sustainable finance can finance a more sustainable world. Her work examines how firms can integrate Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) considerations into their operations to enhance competitiveness while addressing global challenges such as climate change, inequality, and global health.

**Leadership Roles:**

- President of the Alliance for Research on Corporate Sustainability (ARCS).
- Chair of the Academic Advisory Committee of the United Nations-supported Principles for Responsible Investment (PRI).
- Associate Editor for *Management Science* and *The Strategic Management Journal*.

## Seminar Abstract: Financing Biodiversity

This seminar explores the emerging practice of using private capital to finance biodiversity conservation and restoration.

**Key Areas Covered:**

1. **Conceptual Framework:** The presentation outlines how biodiversity can be financed through:
   - Pure private capital.
   - Blended financing structures (where private capital is combined with public or philanthropic capital to de-risk investments).
1. **Empirical Findings:** Using deal-level data from a leading biodiversity finance institution, the research finds:
   - Projects with higher expected returns tend to be financed by pure private capital, though these projects are smaller in scale and expected impact.
   - Larger-scale projects with more ambitious biodiversity goals are more often financed by blended finance. While these projects have lower expected returns, their risk is also lower. This suggests that blending capital is a crucial tool for improving the risk-return tradeoff.
1. **Policy Implications:** An analysis of projects that did not reach the portfolio stage suggests that private capital alone is unlikely to substitute for effective public policies in addressing the biodiversity crisis, as projects must meet specific thresholds for both financial return and biodiversity impact to attract private funding.
