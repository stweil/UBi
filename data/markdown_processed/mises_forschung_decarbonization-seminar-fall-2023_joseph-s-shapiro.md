## Seminar Abstract

The seminar investigates how multinational production impacts climate change. Microdata analysis reveals that the emission rate of a foreign-owned plant increases proportionally with the emission rate of its home country, suggesting that firms tend to bring green technology with them when operating abroad. The presentation develops and quantifies a multi-country general equilibrium model encompassing multinational production, trade, and energy to assess how various policies encouraging multinational production could affect global carbon emissions and overall welfare.

## Speaker Biography: Joseph S. Shapiro

Joseph S. Shapiro is an Associate Professor at the University of California, Berkeley, specializing in Agricultural & Resource Economics and the Department of Economics.

**Research Roles:**

- Associate Editor of the *Journal of Political Economy*
- Co-Editor of the *Journal of Public Economics*
- Research Associate at the National Bureau of Economic Research
- Research Associate at the Energy Institute at Haas

**Research Focus:**
His research agenda addresses three core questions:

1. The interaction between globalization and the environment.
1. The effectiveness, efficiency, and equity impacts of environmental and energy policies over the last half-century, with a specific focus on water, air, and climate pollution.
1. The importance of investments made by individuals to protect themselves against air pollution and climate change.

**Education & Honors:**
Shapiro has received prestigious awards, including an Alfred P. Sloan Research Fellowship and the Kiel Institute Excellence in Global Affairs Award, and the Marshall Scholarship. He holds a Ph.D. in economics from MIT, Master's degrees from Oxford and LSE, and a B.A. from Stanford.
