## Seminar Abstract

Migration is one of the most consequential disruptions to human society anticipated under climate change. While empirical evidence of climate-induced migration often focuses on individual regions, making global assessments challenging, this seminar provides a quantification in a causal framework. By leveraging rich data on sub-national migration and detailed climate exposure analysis, the talk demonstrates that both global warming and exposure to climate extremes induce outward migration from poorer regions. Furthermore, explicit cross-border flows from poor to rich regions are identifiable. With climate change amplifying these effects, the research projects 210 million climate migrants from low-income regions by mid-century and over 4 billion migrants by 2100 under a high-emission scenario.

## Speaker Biography

**Dr. Leonie Wenz** is the deputy head of Complexity Science and a research group leader for Data-based analysis of climate decisions at the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research (PIK).

**Research Focus:**
Leonie conducts research on the societal and economic impacts of climate change using various quantitative methods. Her work specifically investigates how individuals and societies respond to weather extremes and a changing climatic environment, aiming to derive robust estimates of the cost of warming and inform sustainable development pathways.

**Publications & Recognition:**
Her research has been published in leading journals, including *Nature*, *Science*, *PNAS*, *Journal of Environmental Economics and Management*, and *Journal of Economic Dynamics and Control*. Her findings have informed major governmental bodies, such as the German parliament, the European Central Bank, and the US White House.

**Academic Background:**
A mathematician by training, Leonie earned her Ph.D. from the University of Potsdam and PIK, where her dissertation focused on the intersection of Climate Physics and Economics (awarded *summa cum laude* and the i.a. Leibniz dissertation award). She completed a Postdoc at UC Berkeley through the Ciriacy-Wantrup programme in Natural Resource Economics and Public Policy. Leonie is an elected Member of the German Young Academy (Junge Akademie) at the Berlin-Brandenburg Academy of Science and the National Academy of Sciences Leopoldina.
