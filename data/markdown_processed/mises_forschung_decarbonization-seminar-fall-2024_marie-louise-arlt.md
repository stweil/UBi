## Seminar Abstract

The transition from conventional cars to electric vehicles necessitates a robust public charging infrastructure. This presentation investigates the role of direct public investment, particularly from municipality-owned utilities, in developing such a charging grid.

**Key Research Points:**

1. **Two-Sided Model:** The speaker presents a model of EV adoption and charging station investment, incorporating public investment. The model suggests that while public investment may crowd out private investment in the short run, it can spur accelerated and larger private investor entry in the long run.
1. **Empirical Testing:** The research empirically tests these predictions using a novel panel dataset of municipality-level charging markets across Germany, including data on charging stations and EV registrations.
1. **Methodology:** To address identification challenges, the study utilizes matching and synthetic control methods, leveraging the variation in the share of publicly-owned investors across German municipalities.
1. **Findings:** The analysis finds that municipalities with publicly-owned local investors tend to have higher numbers of installed charging stations. Furthermore, the research explores whether public investment effectively incentivizes or, conversely, discourages private investment.

## Speaker Biography

**Marie-Louise Arlt** is an Assistant Professor at the University of Bayreuth and is affiliated with the [Bavarian Center for Battery Technology (BayBatt)](https://www.baybatt.uni-bayreuth.de/en/index.html).

Her research focuses on new technologies within the energy and mobility sectors, specifically how to leverage economic tools and institutions to enable their optimal integration and deployment for climate change mitigation. Her work operates at the intersection of economics, information systems, and engineering.

**Previous Experience:**

- Junior Research Group Leader at the [ifo Institute](https://www.ifo.de/en).
- Postdoctoral fellow at the Chair for [Comparative Economics](https://www.en.compecon.econ.uni-muenchen.de/index.html) at Ludwig Maximilian University in Munich (Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions programme).
- She graduated from Freiburg University in October 2020. During her PhD studies, she worked with [Stanford University](https://profiles.stanford.edu/ram-rajagopal), [SLAC](https://gismo.slac.stanford.edu/), and [Lawrence Berkeley Lab](https://gridintegration.lbl.gov/about-us).
