### Seminar Abstract

This paper analyzes the green economy using a novel measure of a firm’s green revenues. The research sheds light on the factors driving global public companies' expansion of business activities to support the green transition toward a low-carbon and more environmentally sustainable economy.

Key findings include:

- The green economy grew at an accelerated pace following the Paris Agreement.
- This accelerated growth is attributed to both regulatory initiatives and innovative US firms converting green patents into actual revenues from green products and services.
- A stronger presence of institutional investors prior to the Paris Agreement is associated with higher green revenues afterward.
- The study examines the stock returns of firms with high green revenues and finds only modest evidence of a "green alpha," which appears concentrated in US stocks in the post-Paris period.

### Speaker Biography

**Philipp Krüger** is a Professor of Responsible Finance at the University of Geneva (GSEM, GFRI) and holds a Senior Chair at the Swiss Finance Institute.

After studying Economics and Finance in Germany, the United States, and France, he earned a PhD in Economics from the Toulouse School of Economics in 2010.

His research interests lie in corporate, sustainable, and behavioral finance. His work has been published in all three major academic Finance journals (Journal of Finance, Journal of Financial Economics, Review of Financial Studies). Professor Krüger is a frequent speaker at leading global finance conferences.

His research has received or been nominated for awards from prestigious organizations, including the BSI Gamma Foundation, Chicago Quantitative Alliance, the Financial Management Association, and the European Financial Management Association. Notable awards include:

- **2015:** Moskowitz Prize for outstanding research in sustainable investing.
- **2017:** Best Quantitative Paper Prize at the Principles for Responsible Investment Academic Network Conference.
- **2018:** ICPM Climate Change Research Award for his work on how institutional investors consider climate risks in investment decisions.

He currently serves on the board of experts for Inrate AG, Zurich.
