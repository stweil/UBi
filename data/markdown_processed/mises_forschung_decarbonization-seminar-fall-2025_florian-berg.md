### Seminar Abstract

This session analyzes pricing dynamics within the Voluntary Carbon Market (VCM). The research utilizes a proprietary dataset detailing sales of emission reduction certificates (credits) from a leading VCM dealer. Key findings include:

- **Price Dispersion:** Extraordinary price variation, with carbon credits trading between a few cents and $100 per ton of CO2.
- **Pricing Drivers:** Prices are systematically related to the specific credit project, the buyer, and trade characteristics, rather than a single common value for carbon emissions.
- **Premium Factors:** Credits from technologies with positive non-carbon externalities, even if less reliable, command a premium compared to trusted industrial solutions.
- **Buyer Behavior:** Buyers in low-emission industries, wealthier countries, and large corporations pay a premium for carbon credits, while heavy polluters and firms with explicit sustainability commitments do not.
- **Market Mechanics:** The VCM pricing exhibits volume and client relationship discounts typical of over-the-counter markets.
- **Futures Impact:** A causal link is documented between the introduction of the VCM futures market and the resulting price premia for credits eligible for expiring futures settlement.

### Speaker Biography

Florian Berg is a research scientist at the MIT Sloan School of Management, where he co-founded and co-runs the Aggregate Confusion Project. His research interests span sustainability measurement, focusing specifically on ESG ratings, disclosure, investor impact, carbon accounting, and voluntary carbon markets.

He earned his PhD in financial economics from Paris-Dauphine University, where he also held a visiting research position at ETH Zurich. Professionally, Florian has worked as a quantitative researcher at Amundi Asset Management and as a quantitative strategist/trader at Alphadyne Asset Management. He has taught at Paris-Dauphine University, Sciences-Po Paris, and MIT Sloan. His published work includes op-eds for the *Financial Times* and the *Wall Street Journal*. Florian's research has received numerous accolades, including the Pagano and Zechner price. He is also a member of the Consultative Working Group of ESMA, the European securities regulator.
