# Seminar Talk: Cross-State Spillovers of Regulation Under National Pricing Strategies: Evidence from Electric Vehicles

## Seminar Abstract

When policies differ across regions but prices are not set independently across regions, the equivalency between demand- and supply-side policy instruments breaks down. Drawing on price discrimination theory, this seminar shows that regional demand-side tools, such as consumer subsidies, generate effective price variation across regions, while producer subsidies do not, creating different incentives for firms and ultimately different policy responses.

The research studies the interaction between firms' pricing strategies and the US state-level Zero-Emission Vehicle (ZEV) policy, which incentivizes automobile manufacturers to sell electric vehicles in California and nine other states. Focusing on 2012–2017, the presentation builds and estimates a structural model of the new passenger vehicle market, examining the impacts of the ZEV program in the regulated region and the spillovers to other states. The ZEV policy is compared to a counterfactual demand-side subsidy and tax that holds incentives per vehicle in ZEV states fixed. Under the assumption that Tesla sets national prices, the estimates imply that the demand-side policy would increase electric vehicle sales in the US by 13%.

## Speaker Biography

**Sarah Armitage** is an Assistant Professor at Boston University's Questrom School of Business. She received her Ph.D. from Harvard University, specializing in environmental economics, industrial organization, and public finance.

Her professional experience includes serving as an Economist Fellow at the Environmental Defense Fund (2022–2023 academic year). Prior to graduate school, she worked as a consultant at Industrial Economics, Inc., advising state and federal government clients on various environmental projects, and as a research assistant at MIT's Center for Energy and Environmental Policy Research. She also served as an Impact Fellow at Prime Coalition, overseeing Prime's inaugural impact audit of investments with gigaton-scale emissions reduction potential.

Academically, she holds an M.Phil. in Economic and Social History from the University of Cambridge (where she was a Gates Cambridge Scholar) and a B.A. in History from Yale University.
