# Are New Vehicle Emissions Standards Effective and Efficient?

**Speaker:** Arthur van Benthem
**Affiliation:** University of Pennsylvania
**Date:** May 3, 2021
**Format:** Live virtual event (Registration no longer available)

## Seminar Abstract

This paper assesses the effectiveness and efficiency of current air pollution regulations for vehicles. Theoretically, the optimal policy would involve a Pigouvian tax on emissions. However, since such a tax is technologically infeasible, most countries rely heavily on exhaust standards for new vehicles, which limit emissions per mile (e.g., carbon monoxide and nitrogen oxides).

The research demonstrates that:

- The emissions rate of new vehicles in the US has fallen by over 99% since exhaust standards began in 1967.
- Used vehicles have shown comparable declines, suggesting that exhaust standards are responsible for much of this reduction.
- Exhaust standards are not fully cost-effective because they fail to incentivize the scrapping of older vehicles, which still account for a large share of total emissions.

To study counterfactual policies, the authors develop analytical and quantitative models for both new and used vehicle fleets.

## Speaker Biography

**Arthur van Benthem** is an Associate Professor of Business Economics and Public Policy at Wharton. His research specializes in environmental and energy economics, focusing on the unintended consequences of environmental legislation and the economic efficiency of energy policies. His current research interests include markets for transportation, renewable energy, and oil & gas. He earned his Ph.D. in Economics from Stanford University in 2012, and holds degrees from Stanford and the University of Amsterdam.
