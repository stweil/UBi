# Deregulation, Market Power, and Prices: Evidence from the Electricity Sector

**Speaker:** Ignacia Mercadal
**Affiliation:** Columbia University
**Date:** Monday, April 19, 2021
**Time:** 04:00 – 05:15 PM (CEST)
**Format:** Live virtual event

## Seminar Abstract

When deciding whether to introduce market-based prices into a regulated market, a regulator faces a key tradeoff: profit incentives may reduce costs through more efficient resource allocation, but the presence of market power may lead to increased markups.

This seminar uses a detailed dataset on electricity transactions to investigate the impact of market-based deregulation within the U.S. electricity sector. The findings indicate that the increase in markups dominates any modest efficiency gains, ultimately leading to higher prices for consumers. The research concludes that deregulation does not guarantee lower consumer prices, suggesting that a consumer-oriented regulator might prefer regulating rates to be consumer-friendly rather than allowing prices to be subject to market power.

## Speaker Biography

**Ignacia Mercadal** is an assistant professor in the School of International and Public Affairs at Columbia University. Her research specializes in industrial organization and energy economics. She has previously served as a postdoctoral fellow at the MIT Center for Energy and Environmental Policy Research and the MIT Sloan School of Management. Mercadal holds a PhD in Economics from the University of Chicago, an MSc from Tilburg University, and an MA and a BA from Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile.
