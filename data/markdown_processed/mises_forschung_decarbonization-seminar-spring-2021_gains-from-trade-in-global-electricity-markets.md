# Gains from Trade in Global Electricity Markets

**Speaker:** Matti Liski
**Affiliation:** Aalto University of Helsinki
**Event Date:** May 17, 2021 (Live virtual event)

### Seminar Abstract

This paper begins with rudimentary price theory predictions regarding consumer welfare gains derived from new technologies that improve the efficiency of goods trade. Using micro-data from over 140 million bids across electricity markets in California, the Nordics, and Spain, the research reveals that the consumer surplus gain from efficiency improvements follows a markedly different pattern across these three markets. Consistent with theory, the results can be explained by structural differences in excess demand, which arise from institutional and technological determinants. The welfare mechanism discussed is important for policies concerning new technologies and for understanding the implications of technologies that introduce arbitrage between electricity markets.

### Speaker Biography

Matti Liski is a Professor of Economics at Aalto University Helsinki. He also serves as a research affiliate at several prestigious institutions, including the MIT Center for Energy and Environmental Policy Research, the University of Cambridge Energy Policy Research Group, and CESifo Munich. He has previously been a visiting professor at the Toulouse School of Economics. Matti is recognized as an award-winning teacher and a prolific researcher, with numerous articles published in renowned journals such as the *Journal of the European Economic Association*, *Economic Journal*, *Journal of Economic Theory*, *Journal of Public Economics*, and *Journal of Environmental Economics and Management*. His research focuses on microeconomics—both theoretical and empirical—with applications in industrial organization, public policy, energy markets, and climate change.
