# Accounting for Climate Change Seminar

This seminar discusses the growing pressure on corporations to reduce greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions and proposes advanced accounting methods for tracking these liabilities.

## Speaker Profile

**Karthik Ramanna**
*Professor of Business and Public Policy at the Blavatnik School of Government, University of Oxford.*

Professor Ramanna is an expert on business-government relations, sustainable capitalism, and corporate reporting and auditing. His research focuses on how organizations build trust with stakeholders and the role of business in designing responsible "rules of the game." He has consulted with major global organizations, including Fidelity, KPMG, McKinsey, and PwC.

## Seminar Abstract: Accounting for Climate Change

Corporations face increasing pressure from investors, advocacy groups, and politicians to reduce GHG emissions across their operations and supply chains. Currently, most large companies issue environmental, social, and governance (ESG) reports, but the authors critique these as "catchall reports that are often made up of inaccurate, unverifiable, and contradictory data."

**Proposed Solution: E-liability Accounting System**
The seminar proposes the **E-liability accounting system**. This method measures emissions using a combination of chemistry and engineering principles, applying cost accounting to assign emissions to individual outputs. The authors provide a detailed methodology for assigning E-liabilities across an entire value chain, using the complex example of a car-door manufacturer whose supply chain involves mining, shipping, steel production, and final delivery.
