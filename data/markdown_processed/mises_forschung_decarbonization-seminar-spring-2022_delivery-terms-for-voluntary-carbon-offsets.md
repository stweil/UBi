## Seminar Abstract: Delivery Terms for Voluntary Carbon Offsets

A carbon offset represents one unit of reduction in greenhouse gas emissions that can be used to compensate for emissions occurring elsewhere. Currently, nearly 40% of S&P 500 companies voluntarily purchase carbon offsets (rather than for regulatory compliance) to reduce their emissions. These offsets originate from various projects, including forestry, waste disposal, and renewable energy, with the market size exceeding $6 billion and facilitating 1.7 billion tons of emissions reduction.

Companies purchase voluntary carbon offsets under two primary delivery terms: **prompt delivery** and **forward delivery**.

1. **Prompt Delivery:** The seller first undertakes the investment, and the yield uncertainty is realized later. Based on this realization, the seller determines the price for the carbon offsets as a recourse action.
1. **Forward Delivery:** The buyer orders a specific quantity of offsets *before* the seller undertakes the investment. A key difference here is that, unlike prompt delivery, there is a **quantity risk** under forward delivery, as the seller may generate fewer offsets than the buyer's ordered quantity.

This paper analyzes and compares the economic and environmental implications of these two delivery terms. The findings indicate:

- A buyer prefers **prompt delivery** for projects with either a low or high investment cost, despite the seller's recourse pricing mechanism.
- A buyer prefers **forward delivery** for projects with an intermediate investment cost, despite the inherent quantity risk.
- Crucially, the study finds that a buyer's preference between the two delivery terms is not necessarily aligned with their environmental effectiveness, suggesting that environmental groups should exercise caution when promoting one delivery term over the other.

*This work is a joint effort with Vishal Agrawal (Georgetown University) and Gokce Esenduran (Purdue University).*
