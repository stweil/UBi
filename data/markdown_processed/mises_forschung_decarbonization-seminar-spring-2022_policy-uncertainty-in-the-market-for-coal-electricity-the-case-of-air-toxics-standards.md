# Policy Uncertainty in the Market for Coal Electricity: The Case of Air Toxics Standards

**Speaker:** Ashley Langer
**Affiliation:** University of Arizona
**Date:** March 21, 2022
**Event Type:** Live virtual seminar (Registration required via Zoom)

## Seminar Abstract

Policy uncertainty significantly impacts major irreversible decisions, including investment, technology adoption, and market exit. This is particularly relevant in U.S. environmental policy, where Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) rule-making faces uncertainty due to judicial challenges and executive turnover.

In this presentation, the speaker estimates a dynamic oligopoly model focusing on merchant coal generators facing uncertainty regarding the future enforcement of the Mercury and Air Toxics Standard (MATS). To estimate annual profits—a key input for the model—new approaches are developed for estimating ramping and operation and maintenance costs.

The analysis estimates generators' beliefs about the probability of future policy enforcement and simulates how this uncertainty affects:

- Generator exit decisions.
- Equilibrium costs.
- Overall pollution levels.

**Key Findings:** Generators subject to MATS exhibited substantial uncertainty regarding policy enforcement, with implied probabilities of 70% in 2013 and 36% in 2014. This uncertainty resulted in significant delays in both exit strategies and the adoption of abatement technologies, increasing generator compliance costs by an estimated **$1.6 billion**.

## Speaker Biography

**Ashley Langer** is an Associate Professor of Economics at the University of Arizona. Her research utilizes frontier economic methods to evaluate the impact of environmental and energy policies. Professor Langer's interest in environmental economics stems from the understanding that because individual choices have environmental repercussions, policies (such as subsidies, regulations, and standards) are crucial for improving environmental outcomes. Her research merges theoretical insights with econometric modeling to evaluate how alternative policy approaches change environmental outcomes by recovering the drivers of individual and firm behavior. Prior to joining the University of Arizona in 2012, she worked at the University of Michigan and the Brookings Institution, and earned degrees from the University of California-Berkeley and Northwestern University.
