### Seminar Abstract

The European Union designates 26% of its landmass as a protected area. Protected area policies aim to limit economic development in favor of biodiversity. This paper utilizes a high-resolution remote sensing dataset covering Europe and analyzes the staggered introduction of protected area policies between 1980 and 2020. The study investigates the selection of land for protection and the causal effect of protection on vegetation cover.

**Key Findings:**

- The levels and trends in vegetation cover, measured by an NDVI index, are statistically indistinguishable for both protected and unprotected land.
- The research concludes that land protected later does not show different greening trends compared to land protected earlier.
- The authors suggest that European conservation efforts lack ambition because policymakers tend to select land for protection that is not currently threatened by development.

### Speaker Biography

Mathias Reynaert is a Professor of Economics at the Toulouse School of Economics and a research affiliate at the Center for Economic Policy Research (CEPR). His primary fields of interest include empirical industrial organization and environmental economics. He specializes in evaluating European environmental policy.

**Awards & Education:**

- Awarded an ERC starting grant.
- Holds a Ph.D. from KU Leuven and the University of Antwerp.
