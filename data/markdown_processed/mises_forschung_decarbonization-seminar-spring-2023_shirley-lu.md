# Accountability of Corporate Emissions Reduction Targets

## Seminar Abstract

Companies are increasingly announcing emissions reduction targets, but it remains unclear whether firms are held accountable for these commitments. This paper examines emissions targets set before 2020 to investigate:

1. Which firms fail to meet their targets.
1. How firms disclose the outcomes of these targets.
1. Whether there are tangible consequences for failing to meet emissions targets.

Using data from the CDP, the research found that 85 firms failed to achieve their 2020 targets, representing 13% of all completed emissions targets reported for that year.

**Key Findings:**

- After a firm fails its 2020 emissions target, the study did not observe significant changes in media coverage, market response, environmental scores, or environment-related shareholder proposals.
- While setting emissions targets generates high media coverage, only four of the 85 failed firms were picked up by the media, suggesting a general lack of awareness regarding target outcomes.
- The research did find evidence of "reputation building," noting a positive market reaction to firms that *did* achieve their 2020 targets in subsequent announcements.

The findings underscore the critical importance of institutions providing accountability mechanisms for the progress of emissions targets, offering insights for future targets set for 2030, 2040, and 2050.

## Speaker Biography: Shirley Lu

Shirley Lu is an Assistant Professor in the Accounting and Management Unit. She teaches the Financial Reporting and Control course within the MBA required curriculum.

Professor Lu's research focuses on Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) disclosure, with a specific emphasis on climate change and gender diversity. Her work aims to:

- Evaluate firm CSR performance.
- Understand CSR measurement and rating methodologies.
- Investigate institutional frameworks that facilitate CSR disclosures.

Her research portfolio also includes examining the bonding mechanisms of green bonds and assessing the credibility of CSR disclosures and ESG scores.

**Education:**

- **PhD in Accounting** and **MBA** from the University of Chicago.
- **BS in Accounting and Finance** and **MS in Accounting** from New York University.
