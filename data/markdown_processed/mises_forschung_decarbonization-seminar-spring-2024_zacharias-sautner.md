## Speaker Biography

Zacharias Sautner is a Professor of Sustainable Finance at the Department of Banking and Finance of the University of Zurich (UZH) and holds a senior chair at the Swiss Finance Institute (SFI). His research focuses on ESG topics, including climate change and biodiversity, providing insights into how finance can drive a more sustainable future.

**Expertise Highlights:**

- His work has been published in leading international journals.
- He was ranked #23 globally among 12,000 business authors based on SSRN research paper downloads (January 2023).
- His research has been cited over 5,000 times according to Google Scholar.
- His expertise is referenced in major publications such as the *Wall Street Journal*, *New York Times*, and *Financial Times*, and in reports from institutions like the IMF, ECB, and European Commission.
- He serves as an Associate Editor for the *Review of Financial Studies*, *Journal of Banking & Finance*, *Financial Management*, and *Journal of Corporate Finance*.
- He is also a Regular Research Visitor at the ECB and advises institutional investors on ESG issues.
- Prior to moving to Switzerland, he was Professor of Finance at the Frankfurt School of Finance & Management, where he led the finance department for many years.

## Seminar Abstract: Quantifying Climate Lobbying

The seminar addresses the concern that ambitious climate policy is often obstructed, at least partially, by corporate lobbying activities.

**Key Research Areas:**

- Quantifying corporate anti- and pro-climate lobbying expenses.
- Identifying the largest corporate lobbyists and analyzing their underlying motives.
- Establishing the relationship between climate lobbying and corporate business models.
- Documenting whether and how climate lobbying is priced into financial markets.

**Key Findings:**

- Firms spend an average of $295,921 annually on anti-climate lobbying, compared to $164,991 on pro-climate lobbying.
- Firms attempting to camouflage their climate lobbying activities are noted.
- Large anti-climate lobbyists tend to have more carbon-intensive business models and face higher risks of future climate-related incidents.
- The study suggests that firms spending more on anti-climate lobbying may earn higher returns, potentially due to a risk premium.
- Stock price movements were observed: prices rose when the Waxman-Markey Cap-and-Trade Bill failed, and fell when the Inflation Reduction Act was announced.
