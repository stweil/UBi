## Seminar Abstract

This seminar explores the economic effects of biodiversity loss by developing an ecologically-founded model. The model captures how different species interact to deliver essential ecosystem services that complement other factors of economic production.

**Key Concepts Covered:**

- **Ecosystem Services:** Aggregate services (like pollination and water filtration) are produced by combining several non-substitutable functions, each provided by many substitutable species.
- **Economic Output:** Economic output is modeled as an increasing but highly concave function of species richness.
- **Species Value:** The marginal economic value of a species depends on three factors: (i) the number of similar species within its ecosystem function, (ii) the marginal importance of the affected function for overall ecosystem productivity, and (iii) the extent to which ecosystem services constrain economic output in a given country.
- **Policy Implications:** The framework derives expressions for the fragility of ecosystem service provision, helping policymakers assess risks and prioritize conservation efforts. The model is also embedded in a standard economic framework to study optimal land use when development raises output at the cost of biodiversity.
- **Empirical Findings:** The research finds that even when species loss does not substantially reduce current output, it lowers future growth opportunities and reduces resilience to subsequent species loss, particularly if past losses were asymmetric across functions. Empirically, news about biodiversity loss increases spreads on credit default swaps (CDS) more for countries with more depleted ecosystems.

## Speaker Biography

**Stefano Giglio** is the Frederic D. Wolfe Professor of Finance and Management at Yale School of Management.

His research interests are broad, spanning asset pricing, macroeconomics, and climate finance. He has a particular focus on hedging macroeconomic risks—such as crash risk, uncertainty risk, and climate risk—using various financial instruments.

**Academic History:**

- Before Yale, Professor Giglio was an Associate Professor of Finance at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business.
- He received his PhD in Economics from Harvard University.
- He is a Faculty Research Fellow at the National Bureau of Economic Research and a Research Affiliate at the Center for Economic Policy Research.
- He has served as an editor for the *Review of Financial Studies*.

**Awards:**
Professor Giglio has received several accolades, including the 2021 Carlo Alberto medal (awarded to an Italian economist under 40 for outstanding research contributions), the AQR Insight Award, the Fama-DFA Prize, and the Moskowitz prize for sustainable finance.
