## Seminar Abstract

This paper designs a novel accounting framework that reports an entity’s progress in reducing carbon emissions (or lack thereof). Similar to traditional financial accounting, it features a balance sheet where assets that reduce carbon are offset by liabilities for carbon emissions. The resulting net position reflects the entity's overall carbon standing and its progress toward milestones, such as achieving net-zero carbon goals.

The quasi-income statement reports periodic emissions that contribute to the balance sheet's net position. By recognizing assets on the balance sheet, the framework addresses the timing problem inherent in carbon reduction—allowing credit for investments made today whose effects are realized later.

This responsibility accounting framework offers attractive incentive and monitoring features:

- It provides a structure for *pro forma* (budgeting) of carbon reduction strategies, setting measurable benchmarks.
- It facilitates consolidation across different entities to report carbon metrics for specific groups, such as entire industries.
- Mirroring financial accounting, it supports "double materiality" assessments by comparing carbon metrics directly against financial metrics to evaluate trade-offs and overall sustainability.

## Speaker Biography

**Stephen Penman** is the George O. May Professor in the Graduate School of Business at Columbia University. He also serves as co-director of the Center for Excellence in Accounting and Security Analysis and director of the Masters Program in Accounting and Fundamental Analysis.

Professor Penman earned a first-class honors degree in Commerce from the University of Queensland, Australia, and holds M.B.A. and Ph.D. degrees from the University of Chicago. His research interests span the valuation of equity and the role of accounting information in security analysis, with several papers focusing on accounting policy issues.

He has published extensively in leading finance and accounting journals and has presented seminars on accounting and analysis to both academic and professional audiences. Stephen Penman is a founding editor of the *Review of Accounting Studies* and served as managing editor from 2002–2006. He was elected to the Accounting Hall of Fame in 2019 and the Australian Accounting Hall of Fame in 2020.
