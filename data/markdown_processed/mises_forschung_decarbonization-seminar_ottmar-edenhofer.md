## Seminar Abstract

Professor Ottmar Edenhofer will examine why large-scale carbon dioxide removal (CDR) is essential for meeting climate targets, establishing a third pillar of climate policy alongside abatement and adaptation.

**Key Discussion Points:**

- **Barriers to CDR:** The main obstacles to technology development and deployment are institutional, economic, and political, not purely technical.
- **Market Solutions:** Prof. Edenhofer will present market and governance solutions, including innovative “clean-up certificates” and a European Carbon Central Bank to manage net-negative emissions within carbon market frameworks.
- **Planetary Management:** By “cleaning up” the atmosphere, CDR can help reduce free-riding incentives in international climate co-operation. He identifies planetary carbon management as the central challenge of 21st-century climate policy.

**Core Argument:**
Climate targets require large-scale CO2 removal. Since projected CDR levels fall short, the focus must shift to governance gaps. Prof. Edenhofer argues that scaling CDR requires new market structures and durable institutions. He proposes **clean-up certificates**—rights to emit coupled with binding future removal obligations—to integrate removals into systems like the EU Emissions Trading System. He calls for a **European Carbon Central Bank** to issue and manage these certificates, thereby reframing climate policy around enabling net-negative emissions.

## Speaker Biography

**Ottmar Edenhofer** is Director and Chief Economist of the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research, and previously Director of the Mercator Research Institute on Global Commons and Climate Change (2012–2024). He is also a Professor for The Economics and Politics of Climate Change at the Technische Universität Berlin. Furthermore, he serves as Chair of the [European Scientific Advisory Board on Climate Change](https://climate-advisory-board.europa.eu/) (ESABCC).
