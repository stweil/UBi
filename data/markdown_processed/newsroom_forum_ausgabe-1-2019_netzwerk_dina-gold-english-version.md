# Stolen Legacy: A Fight for Historical Justice

This article details the research of Dina Gold, whose book *Stolen Legacy* examines the systematic expropriation of Jewish businesses during the Nazi era. Gold's investigation led to revelations concerning prominent figures, including Dr. Kurt Hamann, who, despite his honorary status at the University of Mannheim, was found to have been responsible for "Aryanizations" during the Nazi period. These findings prompted the University of Mannheim to review and rename the Dr. Kurt-Hamann Foundation.

## The Case of the Wolff Building

Dina Gold's research began with the history of the H. Wolff fur company, a major German fur merchant business.

- **Expropriation:** In 1937, Victoria Insurance (now part of ERGO Insurance) initiated a forced sale of the Wolff family's building at Krausenstraße 17/18 in East Berlin, passing it to the Reichsbahn.
- **The Struggle for Redress:** Gold traveled to Berlin after the fall of the Berlin Wall to claim compensation. The process was described as an "emotional roller coaster," involving years of research, legal action, and family support.
- **Outcome:** The family was eventually compensated in 1996, a rare success in German post-war history.
- **Tragedy Revealed:** The research also uncovered the fate of Gold's maternal great-uncle, who was found listed at the Sachsenhausen concentration camp in 1938 and later deported to Auschwitz.

## Institutional Accountability and Memory

The investigation extended to the role of institutions and individuals during the Nazi era.

### Questioning Honor Bearers

Gold learned about Dr. Kurt Hamann, the wartime CEO of Victoria Insurance, through the UK National Archives. While Hamann was not a member of the NSDAP, he was responsible for business practices that included insuring slave labor workshops at Auschwitz, Buchenwald, and Stutthof.

- **University Action:** Following an academic study commissioned by the University of Mannheim, the institution revoked Hamann's honorary senatorial status and renamed the associated foundation.
- **Advocacy for Remembrance:** Gold emphasized that while the current generation is not to blame, it has a responsibility to remember. She campaigned for:
  - A plaque at the building at Krausenstrasse (now part of the [Ministry of the Interior, Building and Homeland](https://www.bmi.bund.de/EN/service/service_node.html)) explaining its history.
  - A *Stolperstein* (stumbling stone) for her great-uncle Fritz in front of his former apartment on Dresdener Strasse 97 in Berlin.

### The Importance of Storytelling

Gold noted that while the first generation often focuses on rebuilding new lives, the second generation is fascinated by these stories. She stressed the necessity of continuing to tell these histories to ensure justice and remembrance.
