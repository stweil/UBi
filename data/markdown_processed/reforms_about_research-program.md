# Research Program: Political Reforms in Welfare States

The Collaborative Research Center aims to gain novel insights into the constraints on political reforms within welfare states. The interdisciplinary Center brings together over 80 senior and junior researchers from economics, political science, sociology, and informatics.

## Core Research Focus and Approach

The research projects address the political economy of reforms across several dimensions:

1. **Individual Attitudes and Aggregation:** Examining individual attitudes towards reforms and how these attitudes aggregate into organized interests (Project Group A).
1. **Context and Spatial Dimension:** Analyzing the context and spatial differences of reforms within welfare states (Project Group B).
1. **Political Process:** Investigating the political process of reform-making, with a specific focus on the role of political parties (Project Group C).

The common approach involves evaluating competing theoretical models from a political economy perspective. The Center promotes research that empirically evaluates the extent to which actors (citizens, interest groups, political parties, governments) rationally evaluate reforms based on available information, and conversely, where they fail to fully understand complex reform proposals.

## Methodological Rigour

The Center emphasizes a rigorous methodological approach to studying reform-making, aiming to overcome the terminological ambiguity surrounding the term "reform."

- **Bias Mitigation:** Projects avoid selection bias by analyzing both reform failures and successes.
- **Econometric Development:** Given the challenge of empirically evaluating competing views of reform-making, the Center promotes the development of novel econometric tools for analyzing complex decisions involving multiple issues and actors with changing positions over time.
- **Data Infrastructure:** The Data Center collects and manages vast amounts of quantitative data on reform-making in Germany and other welfare states.

## Research Groups

The Center is structured into three specialized research groups, supported by a central Data Center:

### Group A: Individual Attitudes and Multi-Level Aggregation of Reform Interests

This group focuses on the micro-foundations of reforms—the formation and aggregation of actors’ attitudes and interests.

- **Methodology:** Theoretical analysis primarily uses tools from game theory and mechanism design theory to study reform feasibility under incomplete information.
- **Empirical Work:** Theoretical guidance informs empirical research using new data on individual reform preferences, knowledge, expectations, and experiments confronting participants with various policy options.

### Group B: The Spatial Dimension of Reform Making in Welfare States

This area studies the spatial contexts of reforms, examining differences across countries, regions, and policy sectors.

- **Focus Areas:** Particular attention is paid to labor markets, social security, and the public sector.
- **Analysis:** Quantitative analysis considers the contextual implications of both reform successes and failures, with a special emphasis on the *ex-post* evaluation of economic policy reforms, including RCTs in Europe and developing countries.

### Group C: The Political Process of Reform Making

This group concentrates on the political dynamics surrounding reform.

- **Investigation:** Projects develop measures for the necessity of reform, the motivations, and the restrictions placed on political actors when initiating or adopting reforms.
- **Analysis:** Common empirical analysis uses state-of-the-art techniques to estimate the reform potential, particularly focusing on the roles of political parties.

### The Data Center

As the core infrastructure, the Data Center builds an internet panel survey to gather data on individual attitudes and preferences relevant to reform decision-making. This panel serves as the core engine for collecting individual microdata for the Center's projects and will also provide infrastructure for surveys targeting political elites. These data are used by researchers and eventually made available to the scientific community.
