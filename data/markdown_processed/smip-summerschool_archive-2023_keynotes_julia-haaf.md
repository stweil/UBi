# Hierarchical Modeling and Individual Differences in Cognitive Tasks

**Speaker:** Julia Haaf
**Date:** 30 June, 14:00
**Source:** [Summer School 2023 Keynotes](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/smip-summerschool/archive-2023/keynotes/julia-haaf/)

This presentation explores the utility of hierarchical models in analyzing repeated measures designs, particularly for understanding individual differences across various cognitive tasks.

### Core Concepts

Hierarchical models are presented as a powerful approach for data structures where multiple observations are nested within participants. They are noted for optimally accounting for data structure, thereby reducing estimation bias and improving predictive accuracy compared to simpler models. A key focus is how these models can explicitly separate noise variability from true variability across people to better understand individual differences.

### Research Focus Areas

The talk highlights recent collaborative projects investigating individual differences in cognitive tasks, structured around the following topics:

- **Bayesian Hierarchical Modeling:** An initial introduction to the methodology (Collaborator: Myrthe Veenman).
- **Speed-Accuracy Trade-off:** Discussion on how multivariate Bayesian hierarchical modeling can account for this trade-off (Collaborator: Dr. Suzanne Hoogeveen).
- **Attentional Control:** Examination of whether cognitive Bayesian hierarchical modeling can improve the reliability of individual difference measures for attentional control (Collaborator: Michelle Hoogeveen).
- **Developmental Skills:** Exploration of combining Bayesian hierarchical modeling with computational modeling to better understand the development of mathematics skills in elementary school (Collaborator: Şeyma Nur Ertekin).
