# Exploring the Impact of Design Choices on Careless Responding with Confirmatory Mixture Models

**Speaker:** Esther Ulitzsch
**Date:** 4 July 2025
**Time:** 14:00
**Source:** [University of Mannheim SMIP Summer School Keynote](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/smip-summerschool/keynotes/esther-ulitzsch/)

## Overview: The Challenge of Careless Responding (C/IER)

Careless and insufficient effort responding (C/IER) on self-report measures poses a significant threat to data quality, as the resulting responses may not accurately reflect the underlying constructs being measured.

This presentation introduces **Confirmatory Mixture Models (CMM)**, sophisticated statistical tools that translate theoretical considerations about respondent behavior into constrained component models. These models are designed to study the occurrence, determinants, and contextual correlates of C/IER.

## Methodological Applications

The presentation utilizes two distinct case studies based on experimental survey data to illustrate the power and flexibility of CMMs:

1. **Cross-Sectional Data Analysis:**

   - **Focus:** Comparing the incidence of C/IER between different scale types.
   - **Comparison:** Examining whether increasingly common **visual analogue scales** elicit more or less C/IER compared to traditional **Likert-type scales**.
   - **Method:** Employing models that account for the unique statistical properties of each scale type.

1. **Longitudinal Data Analysis (EMA):**

   - **Focus:** Investigating the impact of data collection frequency on C/IER.
   - **Data Source:** Screen time data collected via an experimental **ecological momentary assessment (EMA)**.
   - **Variables Tested:** The influence of **questionnaire length** and **sampling frequency** on C/IER occurrence in high-frequency longitudinal settings.

## Key Takeaways and Guidelines

The core objective is to demonstrate how CMMs can inform best practices for study design to mitigate C/IER and yield higher-quality data.

**Conclusion:** The presentation concludes with a critical warning regarding the risks of drawing erroneous conclusions if the underlying assumptions of the statistical models are violated.
