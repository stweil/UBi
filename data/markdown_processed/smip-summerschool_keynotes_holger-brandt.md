# Exploring Psychometric Complexities in Intensive Longitudinal Data

**Speaker:** Holger Brandt
**Date:** 30 July 2025
**Time:** 18:30
**Source:** [University of Mannheim Summer School Keynote](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/smip-summerschool/keynotes/holger-brandt/)

## Overview of the Talk

This presentation addresses the complex psychometric issues encountered when researchers collect intensive longitudinal data, particularly when the same items are presented repeatedly to participants.

### Key Research Questions Addressed:

- How frequently should questionnaires be administered?
- How can researchers detect participant inattention?
- What are the implications if an item's meaning changes for individuals who respond to it very often?

### Core Methodological Challenges:

The talk discusses psychometric questions accompanying the collection of intensive longitudinal data, such as those used in ecological momentary assessments (EMA). A major challenge highlighted is the difficulty of extrapolating psychometric characteristics derived from cross-sectional questionnaire evaluations to high-frequency, repeated-measure longitudinal studies (e.g., daily assessments).

### Specific Topics Covered:

- **Measurement Invariance:** Examination of heterogeneous measurement invariance patterns that may vary across individuals and/or over time.
- **Behavioral Issues:** Addressing the occurrence of careless and insufficient effort behavior (inattention).
- **Structural Changes:** Analyzing structural changes within the dimensions that questionnaires are intended to measure.

### Analytical Approach:

The speaker will provide insights into using **Dynamic Structural Equation Modeling (DSEM)**. This framework will be used to test for the presence of these data problems and incorporate solutions to help researchers draw valid conclusions.

### Methodological Considerations:

The discussion will conclude by outlining the challenges associated with these complex methods, including:

- Necessary sample sizes.
- Model fit assessment.
- Optimal prior tuning in Bayesian implementations.
