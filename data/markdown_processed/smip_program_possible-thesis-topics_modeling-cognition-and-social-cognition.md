# Modeling Cognition and Social Cognition

Cognition is a broad term encompassing all processes related to our knowledge of the world. Theories of learning and perception describe how knowledge is acquired, built up, and integrated with existing knowledge. Memory theories detail how this information is stored and retrieved, while theories of motor behavior, action, and language explain how this knowledge guides our behavior.

Since cognitive processes are not directly observable, statistical modeling is particularly useful for disentangling the components of cognition that contribute to behavior. Stochastic modeling of behavioral outcomes, as well as response time distributions, has driven significant progress in understanding cognitive functioning in recent decades. Examples include models for separating storage, maintenance, and retrieval in memory, or evidence accumulation models used to explain response times and accuracy in decision-making.

In line with the research focus of the SMiP group, we outline several proposals for developing new models of cognitive processes. These areas include recognition memory, recall from episodic memory, decision strategy choice, advice seeking for decisions, attention, and implicit association tasks. Furthermore, we propose applying existing MPT models, diffusion models, and (generalized) hierarchical models to research questions in cognitive aging, sleep effects on episodic memory, decision making, subliminal priming, task switching, evaluative conditioning, and moral judgment.

*Note: These project proposals are exemplary; further developments and original modeling ideas are welcome.*

## Possible Model Development Projects

These projects focus on developing novel models for specific cognitive processes:

- **Recognition Memory:** Integration of response times in threshold models of recognition memory (Advisors: Erdfelder, Klauer).
- **Social Cognition:** Multinomial processing-tree models of implicit measures of social cognition with response-latency modeling (Advisors: Klauer, Erdfelder).
- **Episodic Memory:** Separation of storage and retrieval in episodic memory (Advisors: Bröder, Erdfelder, Klauer).
- **Decision Making:** Identification and separation of decision-making processes (Advisors: Hilbig, Bröder, Erdfelder).
- **Decision Making:** Extending diffusion model analyses to more complex decision making (Advisors: Voss, Klauer).
- **Cognitive Conflict:** Parameter estimation and model testing of time-varying diffusion processes in cognitive conflict tasks (Advisors: Ulrich, Voss).
- **Advice Seeking:** Modeling information uptake in an advice-seeking paradigm (Advisors: Hütter, Voss).

## Possible Model Application Projects

These projects involve applying existing modeling frameworks to specific research questions:

- **Memory:** Item versus source forgetting rates (Advisors: Kuhlmann, Bröder, Klauer, Voss).
- **Sleep & Memory:** The sleep benefit in episodic memory: Does sleep improve maintenance in memory, retrieval from memory, or both? (Advisors: Erdfelder, Ulrich, Kuhlmann).
- **Attention & Memory:** Analysis of dynamic attentional processes in decision making and memory with generalized hierarchical models (Advisors: Meiser, Hilbig, Voss).
- **Priming:** Analysis of interpersonal, item-specific, and dynamic predictors of subliminal priming effects (Advisors: Kiesel, Klauer).
- **Task Choice:** Modeling of task choice behavior (Advisors: Kiesel, Bröder).
- **Learning:** Conditions for controllable and uncontrollable learning in evaluative conditioning (Advisors: Hütter, Klauer).
- **Moral Judgment:** Investigating the role of sufficiency and necessity in moral judgment (Advisors: Klauer, Hütter).
- **Decision Making:** Integrating sampling theories of risky choice with evidence accumulation (diffusion) models (Advisors: Hilbig, Voss, Bröder).
