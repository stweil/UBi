### 🔬 Potential PhD Thesis Topics & Research Projects

The following projects outline areas of planned research within SMiP. PhD Candidates are welcome to propose their own thesis focus within these broader themes.

**1. Metamemory Predictions of Different Memory Processes**

- **Focus:** Evaluating individuals’ ability to predict the likelihood of later memory recall (Judgment of Learning, JOL) or their confidence in a memory report (Confidence Judgments, CJs).
- **Challenge:** Current research often confounds memory measures by relating metamemory to empirical performance (e.g., recalled/not recalled), mixing memory processes with non-memory processes (like guessing).
- **Goal:** To explore approaches linking metamemory judgments with Multinomial Process Theory (MPT) parameter estimates to evaluate how well these judgments predict specific memory processes. (See Kuhlmann & Undorf, 2018, for an existing approach).

**2. Emotional Congruency Effects in Source Memory**

- **Definition:** Source memory involves remembering not just the information, but also its context (e.g., who said it).
- **Gap:** Previous studies have limited emotional influence to either the item itself or the source, rarely considering scenarios where both have emotional connotations.
- **Goal:** To systematically examine the effects of emotional congruence in source memory. This will utilize the two-high threshold multinomial model of source monitoring (2HTSM; Bayen, Murnane, & Erdfelder, 1996) to test scenarios where item and source emotions are congruent or discongruent.

**3. Longitudinal Cognitive Aging**

- **Context:** Cognitive decline with age is inevitable, but significant interindividual differences exist in the rate of decline, as well as differences between cohorts (generations) and countries.
- **Gap:** While education is a known moderator, the longitudinal effects of job history—including job control, learning opportunities, and job stressors—on cognitive change have not been systematically modeled.
- **Goal:** To apply multilevel modeling techniques to examine the temporal effects of education and job history as person-level predictors of cognitive change rates. Furthermore, the project aims to model education, job, and retirement policy differences as cohort-level and country-level predictors. (In cooperation with Sabine Sonnentag).
