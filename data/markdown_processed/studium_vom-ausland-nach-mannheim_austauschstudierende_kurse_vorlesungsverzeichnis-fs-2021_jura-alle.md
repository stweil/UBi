# Course Catalog: International and European Law (Spring Semester 2021)

This catalog summarizes advanced modules covering comparative, European, and international legal topics.

## Comparative Law I

This course provides an accessible introduction to the nature, functions, and aims of Comparative Law for students from diverse legal backgrounds. Given the difficulty of mastering an entire legal system, the course adopts a selective approach, focusing particularly on **European Legal Traditions**, as their similarities and differences drive European harmonization. The module concentrates on private law and business law aspects of comparative law.

**Key Components Covered:**

- **Comparative Law Methodology:** Aims, tools, and methods of comparative legal research.
- **Legal Systems:** Principles of equality across legal orders and the grouping of legal families by historical origin or structure.
- **Private Law Concepts:** Basic concepts of contract law (party autonomy, formation, performance).
- **Business Law Concepts:** Core elements of business law, including division of work, liability, tort law, and insurance.
- **Modern Legal Issues:** Analysis of legal transplants, hybrids, and the development of European private law.
- **Sources of Law:** Examination of different layers of legal sources, including Uniform Sales Law, UNIDROIT, and PECL.
- **Harmonization Debates:** Discussions on excessive harmonization, the function of soft law, and the concept of the weaker party in contract law.

## E-Commerce and Digital Law

This course offers a thorough introduction to the legal problems arising from the use of the internet, with a focus on business-related activities. It analyzes major legal issues across five economically relevant sections:

- **E-Commerce & Private Law:** Covering contract formation, standard terms and conditions, consumer protection, and issues related to internet auctions and mobile commerce.
- **Advertising for E-Commerce:** Rules on unfair competition, required imprint information, spamming bans, and liability under telemedia law (including for hyperlinks and user-generated content).
- **Copyright Law and Online Shops:** Focus on copyright restrictions for content presentation (including file sharing and streaming) and content/website protection.
- **Domains:** Explanation of the domain name system, its implications for trademark and unfair competition law, and protection possibilities.
- **Cross-Border E-Commerce:** Addressing international aspects of online trade.

## EU Competition Law (Antitrust)

This module provides an introduction to the main areas of EU competition law, illustrated with practical examples reflecting professional experience. Since EU competition rules are directly applicable, violations can lead to unenforceable contracts and substantial fines.

**Core Elements Treated:**

- Horizontal and vertical restraints of competition.
- Market definition and associated techniques.
- The role of market power under Articles 101 and 102 TFEU.
- Basic outlines of European merger control.
- **Key Areas:** The fight against cartels, abuse of dominance, investigating and sanctioning cartels, merger control, and state aid control.

## Market Freedoms of the European Union

This course systematically surveys the market freedoms—the free movement of goods, persons, services, and capital—which are cornerstones of the European Union's internal market. These freedoms grant direct, enforceable rights to private persons and legal persons.

**Focus Areas:**

- General concept and development of market freedoms.
- Analysis of leading case law from the European Court of Justice (ECJ).
- The function of market freedoms in fostering integration.
- Compliance and further development of these freedoms.

## EU Tax Law

This course examines the increasing impact of European Union law on the taxation of both private individuals and companies operating in Europe. While the EU lacks an original tax authority, its law significantly influences national tax laws.

**Structure:**

- **Introduction:** Overview of EU Tax Law aims and measures.
- **Indirect Taxes:** Focus on positive harmonization through European directives.
- **Direct Taxes:** Examination of secondary law harmonizing direct taxes (e.g., the Parent-Subsidiary Directive).
- **Fundamental Freedoms:** Discussion of the importance of fundamental freedoms concerning taxation in Europe, with a special focus on ECJ case law.

## International Dispute Resolution: Litigation vs. Arbitration

This module compares cross-border litigation before state courts with international arbitration. Parties often favor litigation in their home country due to familiarity, while arbitration is often chosen for perceived efficiency, neutrality, and high legal quality.

**Course Structure:**

- **Cross-Border Litigation (State Courts):**
  - Impact of the forum on the dispute (*lex fori* vs. *lex causae*).
  - International Jurisdiction (e.g., Brussels I Regulation).
  - Provisional measures and evidence access.
  - Recognition and enforcement of foreign judgments.
- **Arbitration Law:**
  - Advantages and disadvantages of arbitration.
  - Drafting arbitration agreements.
  - Introduction to common arbitration rules (ICC, LCIA, Swiss Rules, DIS).
  - Conduct of proceedings and enforcing awards (New York Convention).
