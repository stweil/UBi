# University Wide Electives Catalog

This catalog summarizes elective courses available to exchange students across various disciplines, including Linguistics, Computer Science, Business Administration, and Law.

## 📚 Linguistics Electives

**University Wide Electives – Bachelor (German Focus)**
This course provides foundational knowledge in linguistics, focusing on the German language. It covers central categories and descriptive models across various analysis levels, including Pragmatics, Semantics, Syntax, Morphology, Phonology, Graphhematics, Text, and Discourse Linguistics. Practical application is emphasized through tutorials.

**University Wide Electives – Bachelor (General Linguistics)**

- Know the basics of synchronic linguistics.
- Practice linguistic analyses in the areas of sounds, words, and sentences.
- Explore areas of interest within linguistics.
- Learn to work with English textbooks.
- Develop skills in accumulating and aggregating information from different sources.

## 💻 Computer Science & Mathematics Electives

### **Mathematics & Logic (Master Level)**

This module covers advanced foundational topics:

- Foundations of Propositional Logic (Deduction, Proofs).
- Sets, Relations, and Functions.
- Fundamentals of Combinatorics (Counting finite sets, Countability).
- Introduction to Graph Theory.
- Algebraic Structures (Semigroups, Groups, Homomorphisms, Factor Structures).
- Basic Computational Models / Finite Automata.

### **Computer Science & Algorithms**

This intensive module focuses on theoretical and practical programming skills:

- **Algorithm Design:** Step-by-step refinement, Modularity, Object-Orientation (Class Hierarchies, Inheritance), Recursion.
- **Programming Language:** The object-oriented language Java.
- **Data Structures:** Simple structures (Linked List, Binary Tree, B-Tree).
- **Modeling:** UML modeling (Class Diagrams, Activity Diagrams, State Diagrams).
- **Theory:** Introduction to Algorithm Theory: Computability, Complexity (O-notation), Testing, and Verification of Algorithms and Programs.

## 💼 Business & Information Systems Electives

### **Business Process Modeling & IS**

- Model complex business processes using popular modeling techniques.
- Discuss the requirements, characteristics, and effects of integrated information systems in industrial companies, including complex process interdependencies.
- Complete basic tasks from different functional areas within a wide-spread integrated information system.

**Functional Areas Covered:**

- Research and Development
- Marketing and Sales
- Procurement and Warehousing
- Production
- Shipping and Customer Service
- Finance, Accounting, HR
- Planning and Control Systems

### **Human Resource Management (HR)**

This module covers the entire employee lifecycle within an organizational context:

- Job Analysis & Job Design
- HR Planning & Recruiting
- Selection
- Training
- Performance Management
- Employee Development
- Separation & Retention
- Compensation & Incentives (including International HRM)
- High-Performance Organizations

## ⚖️ Law Electives

**International Criminal Law**
This module provides an overview of key texts and concepts in international criminal law:

- Cassese, Antonio, ‘International Criminal Law’ (Oxford, Oxford University Press, 2008)
- Cryer, Robert, ‘An Introduction to International Criminal Law and Procedure’ (Cambridge, Cambridge University Press, 2010)
- Schabas, William A., ‘The International Criminal Court: A Commentary on the Rome Statute’ (Oxford, Oxford University Press, 2010)
- Werle, Gerhard, ‘Principles of International Criminal Law’ (The Hague, Asser, 2005)

## ℹ️ Important Information for Exchange Students

- **Registration:** Exchange students from all departments are welcome to register for these courses using **Portal2**.
- **Timing:** It is recommended to register before the lecture period starts to ensure timely access to reading materials.
- **Tutorials:** Optional tutorials are also available and require separate registration via Portal2.
- **Prerequisites:** Specific prerequisites vary by course (e.g., Basic knowledge in Political Science is helpful for certain modules).
