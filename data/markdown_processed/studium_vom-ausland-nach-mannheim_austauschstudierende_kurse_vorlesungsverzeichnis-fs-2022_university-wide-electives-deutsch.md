# University Wide Electives Course Catalog

This catalog provides descriptions for various elective courses available to exchange students, covering topics from law and linguistics to IT and history.

## 📚 Course Descriptions

### Linguistics

- **Focus:** Introduction to synchronic linguistics.
- **Skills Developed:** Practicing linguistic analyses across sounds, words, and sentences; identifying personal interests within linguistics; working with English textbooks; accumulating and aggregating information from diverse sources.

### German Private Law (Deutsches Privatrecht)

- **Overview:** An introduction to German private law, particularly for foreign law students.
- **Content:** Begins with an overview of German legal sources, focusing on the Civil Code (BGB) and its historical development. Key areas covered include:
  - Tort Law (Deliktsrecht)
  - Law of Obligations (Schuldrecht)
  - Property Law (Sachenrecht)
  - Short introductions to Inheritance and Family Law, Company Law, and Civil Procedural Law.
- **Goal:** To ensure students are familiar with the structures of German civil law and capable of handling smaller legal cases.

### Combinatorics and Graph Theory

- **Topics Covered:**
  - Foundations of propositional logic (deduction, proof).
  - Sets, relations, and functions.
  - Fundamentals of combinatorics (counting finite sets, countability).
  - Introduction to Graph Theory.
  - Algebraic structures (semigroups, groups, homomorphisms, factor structures).
  - Basic computational models/Finite Automata.

### Memory Politics: Evolution of Ukrainian History Education

- **Approach:** An interdisciplinary course blending historical analysis, cultural exploration, and critical reflection.
- **Focus:** Examining the evolution of history education in Ukraine since its independence in 1991.
- **Learning Objectives:** Understanding the impact of memory politics on historical narratives, gaining insights into Ukraine's historical transitions, and exploring the cultural dimensions influencing historical portrayals. Students engage in discussions with experts and develop research skills.

### Information Systems and Business Process Modeling

- **Core Skills:** Modeling complex business processes using popular techniques and discussing the requirements, characteristics, and effects of integrated information systems in industrial companies.
- **Functional Areas Covered:**
  - Business Process Modelling
  - Application Systems in:
    - Research and Development
    - Marketing and Sales
    - Procurement and Warehousing
    - Production
    - Shipping and Customer Service
    - Finance, Accounting, HR
  - Planning and Control Systems

### Human Resource Management (HR)

- **Scope:** Comprehensive coverage of the employee lifecycle and organizational development.
- **Topics:**
  - Job Analysis & Job Design
  - HR Planning & Recruiting
  - Selection
  - Training
  - Performance Management
  - Employee Development
  - Separation & Retention
  - Compensation and Incentives (including International HRM)
  - High-Performance Organizations

### Computer Science & Algorithms (Programming Focus)

- **Key Areas:**
  - **Algorithm Design:** Step-by-step refinement, modularity, object-orientation (class hierarchies, inheritance), and recursion.
  - **Programming Language:** Introduction to the object-oriented language Java.
  - **Data Structures:** Simple structures (linked list, binary tree, B-tree).
  - **Modeling:** Using UML (Class Diagrams, Activity Diagrams, State Diagrams).
  - **Theory:** Introduction to algorithm theory: computability, complexity (O-notation), and testing/verification of algorithms and programs.

### International Criminal Law Readings

- **Recommended Texts:**
  - Cassese, Antonio, *International Criminal Law* (Oxford, Oxford University Press, 2008)
  - Cryer, Robert, *An Introduction to International Criminal Law and Procedure* (Cambridge, Cambridge University Press, 2010)
  - Schabas, William A., *The International Criminal Court: A Commentary on the Rome Statute* (Oxford, Oxford University Press, 2010)
  - Werle, Gerhard, *Principles of International Criminal Law* (The Hague, Asser, 2005)

## ℹ️ General Information for Exchange Students

- **Registration:** Exchange students from all departments are welcome to register for these courses using **Portal2**.
- **Timing:** It is recommended to register before the lecture period starts to ensure access to reading materials.
- **Prerequisites & Level:**
  - Some courses are designated for 1st or 2nd-year students.
  - Basic knowledge in Political Science may be helpful for certain modules.
  - No prerequisites are required for all students.
- **Optional Tutorials:** Optional tutorials are also available and require separate registration via Portal2.
