# Course Catalog for Exchange Students (Business School)

This catalog outlines available courses for exchange students. Please note the guidelines below regarding course selection based on your academic level.

## 🎓 Course Selection Guidelines

**For Bachelor's Students:**
You are restricted to choosing courses only from the Bachelor's level curriculum. Master's level courses are generally not open to undergraduate students, unless you are in your 4th year of an undergraduate program at the time of arrival in Mannheim.
*For specific questions, please contact the Business School Exchange Team at [incoming.business@uni-mannheim.de](mailto:incoming.business@uni-mannheim.de).*

**For Master's/MBA Students:**
You may select Business courses from all available Master's level courses. If your home university permits, you are also welcome to take selected Bachelor's level courses.

## 📚 Available Course Areas

### Area Finance

This area covers advanced topics in financial theory, impact measurement, and market ethics.

**Key Topics Covered:**

- Mechanics of traditional finance and the comparison between shareholder vs. stakeholder theory.
- Differentiating between ESG, socially responsible investing, and impact investing.
- Applying frameworks for measuring and evaluating social impact (e.g., logic models, SROI).
- Critically assessing the moral boundaries of financial markets.
- Reflecting on how personal values and societal norms influence financial practice.

**Detailed Modules:**

- **The Status Quo in Finance:** Shareholder vs. stakeholder value; opportunity cost of capital; NPV as a decision tool; questioning whom companies serve.
- **Impact Investing:** Global trends, distinctions from ESG, classic approaches (microfinance, benefit corporations, UNPRI), and pitfalls in mission preservation.
- **Measuring Impact:** Logic models, levels of evidence, randomized control trials, SROI, and IRIS+ framework.
- **Markets and Morals:** Debates on commodification (e.g., organ markets, price gouging, corporate-owned life insurance); how markets can crowd out morals.
- **Psychology in Finance:** The role of psychology and human needs in shaping investment priorities.

### Area Information Systems

This area focuses on modeling and implementing integrated information systems across various business functions.

**Key Skills Developed:**

- Modeling complex business processes using popular modeling techniques.
- Discussing the requirements, characteristics, and effects of integrated information systems in industrial companies, including complex process interdependencies.
- Completing basic tasks from different functional areas within a wide-spread integrated information system.

**Functional Areas Covered:**

- Research and Development
- Marketing and Sales
- Procurement and Warehousing
- Production
- Shipping and Customer Service
- Finance, Accounting, HR
- Planning and Control Systems

### Area Management

This area explores sustainability, corporate strategy, and human resource management.

**Core Themes:**

- **Sustainability & Strategy:** Investigating how business can react to the ecological crisis, defining sustainable and regenerative business models, and the role of business in shaping an eco-friendlier future.
- **Human Resource Management:** Topics include Job Analysis & Job Design, HR Planning & Recruiting, Selection, Training, Performance Management, Employee Development, Compensation, Incentives + International HRM, and High-Performance Organizations.
- **Innovation & Foresight:** Modules cover innovation imperatives, design thinking, scenario planning (Manoa Framework), and technology deep dives (AI, IoT).

**Recommended Readings & Resources:**

- Bansal, P., & Clelland, I. (2004). Talking trash: Legitimacy, impression management, and unsystematic risk in the context of the natural environment. *Academy of Management Journal, 47*(1), 93–103.
- Hart, S.L. and Milstein, M.B. (2003). Creating sustainable value. *Academy of Management Executive, 17*(2).
- Orsato, R. (2006). Competitive Environmental Strategies: When does it pay to be green? *California Management Review, 48*(2), 127–143.
- Porter, M. E., & Kramer, M. R. (2011). Creating shared value. *Harvard Business Review, 9*(1/2), 62–77.
- Volschenk, J. (2018). The value-implications of coopetition. In *The Routledge companion to coopetition strategy*.
- The SBTi Corporate Net Zero Standard (03–2024).

### Area Marketing

This area focuses on developing and evaluating comprehensive marketing strategies.

**Learning Objectives:**

- Understand the fundamental principles involved in developing and managing marketing strategies.
- Explain the critical importance of marketing strategy for superior business performance.
- Critically evaluate a marketing strategy-related argument (e.g., strategy follows structure, or vice versa).
- Solve complex marketing strategy problems, such as determining the most valuable customer segments.
- Evaluate empirical and theoretical marketing strategy research from various sources.

**Key Texts:**

- Kim, Chan W. and Renee Mauborgne (2017). *Blue Ocean Shift: Beyond Competing*.
- Kim, Chan W. and Renee Mauborgne (2005). *Blue Ocean Strategy*.
- Palmatier, Robert W. and Shrihari Shridha (2017). *Marketing Strategy: Based on First Principles and Marketing Analytics*.
- Rao, Vithala R. and Joel H. Steckel (1998). *Analysis for Strategic Marketing*.
- Whitwell, Gregory, Bryan A. Lukas, and Peter Doyle (2003). *Marketing Management: A Strategic, Value-based Approach*.
