# Business School Course Catalog for Exchange Students (Master's Level)

This guide outlines course selection options for exchange students at the University of Mannheim Business School.

## 🎓 Course Selection Guidelines

**For Bachelor's Students:**
You are restricted to choosing courses only from the bachelor's level catalog. Master's level courses are generally not open to undergraduate students, unless you are in your 4th year of an undergraduate program when you arrive in Mannheim.
*For specific questions, please contact the Business School Exchange Team via email: [incoming.business@uni-mannheim.de](mailto:incoming.business@uni-mannheim.de).*

**For Master's/MBA Students:**
You are eligible to select Business courses from all available master's level courses. If your home university permits, you may also enroll in selected bachelor's level courses.

### Area Management

This area features advanced research and organizational theory topics:

- **Academic Readings:**

  - Battilana, J., & Lee, M. (2014). Advancing Research on Hybrid Organizing – Insights from the Study of Social Enterprises. *The Academy of Management Annals*, 8(1), 397–441. [DOI Link](https://doi.org/10.1080/19416520.2014.893615)
  - Battilana, J., Besharov, M., & Mitzinneck, B. (2017). On Hybrids and Hybrid Organizing: A Review and Roadmap for Future Research. In *The SAGE handbook of organizational institutionalism* (2nd ed., pp. 128–162). SAGE.
  - Thornton, P. H., Ocasio, W., & Lounsbury, M. (2012). Defining the Interinstitutional System. In *The Institutional Logics Perspective: A New Approach to Culture, Structure and Process* (pp. 50–75). [DOI Link](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199601936.003.0003)
  - Besharov, M. L., & Smith, W. K. (2014). Multiple Institutional Logics in Organizations: Explaining Their Varied Nature and Implications. *Academy of Management Review*, 39(3), 364–381. [DOI Link](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2011.0431)

- **Course Structure/Activities:**

  - Setting: Group presentation (~3 students per group), with individual grading.
  - Presentation of a scientific paper (maximum 12 minutes), followed by questions/discussion (~20–30 minutes).
  - Application of knowledge from seminar to new settings.
  - **Session Focus:**
    - Session 1: Kick-off, foundations of hybrid organizing (including sector model).
    - Session 2: Introduction to hybrid organizing in the context of institutional theory.
    - Session 4: Focus: Hybrid organizing in the nonprofit and public sector.
    - Session 5: Focus: Hybrid organizing in the for-profit sector.
    - Session 6: Focus: Hybrid organizing in social enterprises and beyond.

### Area Operations Management

This course follows a structured, multi-block format:

- **Block 1:** Introduction to the topic and overview of airline functions, followed by self-organized group formation and the start of case work. Students can submit follow-up questions via email for discussion in Block 2.
- **Block 2:** Recap of key concepts, discussion of submitted questions, and continued group work with lecturer guidance.
- **Block 3:** Final group presentations and a plenary discussion of findings.
