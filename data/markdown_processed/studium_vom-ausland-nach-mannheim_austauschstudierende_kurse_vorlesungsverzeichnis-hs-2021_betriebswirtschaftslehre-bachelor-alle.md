# Course Catalog for Exchange Students - Business School

This catalog outlines the available courses for exchange students at the Business School. For general information regarding exchange studies, please visit the [University Mannheim Exchange Student Portal](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/studium/vom-ausland-nach-mannheim/austauschstudierende/kurse/vorlesungsverzeichnis-hs-2021/betriebswirtschaftslehre-bachelor-alle/).

## Eligibility Guidelines

The selection of suitable courses depends on your current academic level:

- **Bachelor's Students:** You must select courses exclusively from the bachelor's level curriculum. Master's level courses are generally not open to undergraduate students, unless you are in your 4th year of an undergraduate program when you arrive in Mannheim.
- **Master's/MBA Students:** You are eligible to choose Business courses from all master's level courses. If your home university permits, you may also take selected bachelor's level courses.

**For specific questions regarding eligibility, please contact the Business School Exchange Team at** [incoming.business@uni-mannheim.de](mailto:incoming.business@uni-mannheim.de).

### Area Finance

This area focuses on advanced financial concepts, including:

- Demonstrating a thorough understanding of international financial securities and core theories of pricing currency derivatives (forwards, futures, options, swaps).
- Applying pricing models of currency derivatives and financial risk management to international finance decision-making.
- Critically evaluating concepts such as arbitrage, speculation, hedging, and asset replication in various international financial transactions.
- **Specific Topics Covered:**
  - Spot exchange markets, arbitrage, and least cost dealing.
  - Forward contracts in perfect and imperfect markets.
  - Currency futures: hedging and speculation methods; risk management using futures.
  - Currency options: basic market mechanism and put-call parity.
  - Pricing currency options: using binomial, Black-Scholes, and log-normal models.
  - Swap contracts.
  - Credit derivatives, financial innovation, and securitization.
