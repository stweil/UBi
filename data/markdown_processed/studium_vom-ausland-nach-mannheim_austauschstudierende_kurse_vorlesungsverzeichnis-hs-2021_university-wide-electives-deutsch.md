# University Wide Electives Catalog

This catalog provides descriptions for various elective courses available to exchange students, covering topics from law and linguistics to sociology and modeling.

## 🏛️ Law and Legal Studies

### German Private Law (Zivilrecht)

This course offers an introduction to German private law, particularly for foreign law students.

- **Content Focus:** Begins with an overview of the legal sources of German law, discussing the special position and historical development of the Civil Code (BGB).
- **Core Areas:** Systematic descriptions and case solutions focusing on:
  - Tort Law (Deliktsrecht)
  - Law of Obligations (Schuldrecht)
  - Property Law (Sachenrecht)
- **Additional Topics:** Short introductions to Inheritance and Family Law, Company Law, and Civil Procedural Law.
- **Goal:** To ensure students are familiar with the structures of German civil law and capable of handling smaller legal cases.

### German Constitutional Law (Grundgesetz)

This course provides a general introduction to German Constitutional Law, covering the constitution, its background, contents, and methods for working with constitutional legal texts.

- **Background:** The German Basic Law (*Grundgesetz*) forms the formal constitutional document of the Federal Republic of Germany, rooted in the allied occupation after WWII. It is a foundation for the German democratic system and federal state.
- **Key Focus:** The structure of the *Grundgesetz*, with a particular focus on the first chapter containing fundamental rights (Art. 1 – 19).
- **Contents:**
  - The German legal system
  - Constitutional Law
  - The *Grundgesetz* and the European Union
  - Fundamental Rights
  - Fundamental Constitutional Principles
  - The Federal State, Constitutional organs, Legislative procedure, Public administration, and The judicial system.
- **Learning Target:** Students will understand the basic principles and most important provisions of the *Grundgesetz*, solve simple cases regarding fundamental rights, and compare German structures with other legal systems.
- **Examination:** Oral Exam
- **Lecturer:** Katharina Longin

## 🗣️ Linguistics

### Linguistics Elective

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to linguistic analysis.

- **Skills Acquired:**
  - Learning the basics of synchronic linguistics.
  - Familiarity with descriptive and analytic tools used in linguistics.
  - Practicing linguistic analyses across sounds, words, and sentences.
  - Developing skills to accumulate and aggregate information from diverse sources.
  - Working with English textbooks.

## 🌍 Sociology and Social Structure

### Social Structure in International Comparison

This lecture provides a systematic overview of the key characteristics of the social structure of the Federal Republic of Germany and Europe in comparison to other European countries.

- **Focus Areas:** Changes and trends in:
  - Population, household, and family structures.
  - Social inequality and social mobility.
  - Education, employment, and profession.
  - Income distribution and development of prosperity.
- **Key Readings/References:** Includes academic works comparing social structures (e.g., Hradil, Mau & Verwiebe, Statistisches Bundesamt).

## 💻 Technical and Methodological Skills

### Modeling and Process Design

This module focuses on the formal and practical aspects of system modeling.

- **Core Knowledge:**
  - Understanding the fundamental principles and formal foundations for modeling application domains and processes.
  - Knowledge of current modeling languages and tools.
- **Skills Developed:**
  - Describing domains and processes of simple to medium complexity using standard languages and tools.
  - Understanding complex interdependencies, teamwork, and communicating modeling decisions.
- **Topics Covered:** Modeling principles, practical languages (UML, BPMN), formal foundations (Logic, Petri Nets), and modeling tools.

## 🌐 Interdisciplinary Knowledge Requirements

### Political Science & European Integration

Students are expected to develop expertise in:

- Understanding and selecting political science studies concerning European integration and the European Union.
- Possessing excellent knowledge of the research state regarding main approaches to European integration and the EU.
- Gaining theoretical and methodological knowledge for studying European integration and the EU.
- Applying skills to related or future problems of European integration.

### International Relations & Comparative Politics

Students should have foundational knowledge in:

- Basic literature in international relations and comparative politics (lectures).
- Methodological knowledge of research design and statistics (courses in political science or related disciplines).
- A high interest in cutting-edge political science research published in leading international scholarly journals.
