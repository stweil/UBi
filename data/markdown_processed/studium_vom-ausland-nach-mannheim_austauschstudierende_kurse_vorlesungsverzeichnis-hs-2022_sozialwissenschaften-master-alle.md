# Course Catalog for Exchange Students in Social Sciences

This guide outlines the course requirements and guidelines for incoming exchange students in Political Science, Sociology, and Psychology at the University of Mannheim.

## 🎓 Departmental Course Guidelines

### Political Science (Master)

Courses are generally restricted to incoming exchange students majoring in Political Science or those from the School of Social Sciences (Sociology, Psychology).

- **Course Selection:** Nominated exchange students will be contacted by their departmental exchange coordinator via email regarding their course choice (Fall/Winter semester: end of May/early June; Spring/Summer semester: end of November/early December).
- **Students from Other Departments:** Participation is only possible if:
  - Places are available for other students.
  - The student possesses basic knowledge in Political Science and Statistics.
  - The departmental exchange coordinator explicitly approves the participation.
- **Inquiries:** For further questions, please contact: [int-pol@uni-mannheim.de](mailto:int-pol@uni-mannheim.de).

### Sociology (Bachelor)

Courses are generally restricted to incoming exchange students majoring in Sociology or those from the School of Social Sciences (Political Science, Psychology).

- **Course Selection:** Nominated exchange students will be contacted by their departmental exchange coordinator via email regarding their course choice (Fall semester: end of November/early December; Spring semester: end of May/early June).
- **Students from Other Departments:** Participation is only possible if:
  - Places are available for other students.
  - The student possesses basic knowledge in Sociology and Statistics.
  - The departmental exchange coordinator explicitly approves the participation.
- **Inquiries:** For further questions, please contact: [International.sowi@uni-mannheim.de](mailto:International.sowi@uni-mannheim.de).

### Psychology (Bachelor)

Courses are generally restricted to incoming exchange students majoring in Psychology.

- **Course Selection:** Nominated exchange students will be contacted by their departmental exchange coordinator via email regarding their course choice (Fall semester: end of November/early December; Spring semester: end of May/early June).
- **Students from Other Departments:** Unless otherwise indicated, Psychology courses are restricted to students nominated for Psychology.
- **Inquiries:** For further questions, please contact: [International.sowi@uni-mannheim.de](mailto:International.sowi@uni-mannheim.de).

## 📚 Academic Focus Areas & Prerequisites

### Sociology

**Key Skills/Knowledge Areas:**

- Ability to design basic surveys.
- Ability to evaluate the quality and design of existing surveys.
- Improving skills in presenting and writing research reports.

**Recommended Literature:**

- Cameron, A., Colin, and Trivedi, P. K. (2005). *Microeconometrics: Methods and Applications*. Cambridge University Press, Cambridge, UK.
- Green, William H. (2008). *Econometric Analysis* (sixth edition). Prentice Hall, New Jersey, USA.
- Wooldridge, Jeffrey M. (2001). *Econometric Analysis of Cross Section and Panel Data*. The MIT Press, Cambridge (MA), USA.
- Light, R., & Moody, J. (Eds.). (2020). *The Oxford handbook of social networks*. Oxford University Press.
- Carrington, P. J., & Scott, J. (2011). *The SAGE handbook of social network analysis*. The SAGE Handbook of Social Network Analysis, 1–640.
- Gërxhani, K., de Graaf, N. D., & Raub, W. (Eds.). (2022). *Handbook of sociological science: Contributions to rigorous sociology*. Edward Elgar Publishing.
- Lazega, E., Snijders, T. A., & Wittek, R. P. (Eds.). (2022). *A Research Agenda for Social Networks and Social Resilience*.

### Psychology

**Key Skills/Knowledge Areas:**

- Standard and advanced linear models (including multiple regression with continuous and categorical predictors, product terms, regularization methods, and nonlinear regression).
- Generalized linear models (including logistic regression, Poisson models, and log-linear models).
- Supervised and unsupervised classification methods (including discriminant analysis, clustering methods, regression trees, and mixture models).

**Recommended Literature:**

- Dunn, P. K., & Smyth, G. K. (2018). *Generalized linear models with examples in R*. New York: Springer.
- Hastie, T., Tibshirani, R., & Friedman, J. (2009). *The elements of statistical learning. Data mining, inference, and prediction* (2nd edition). New York: Springer.
- James, G., Witten, D., Hastie, T., & Tibshirani, R. (2013). *An introduction to statistical learning with applications in R*. New York: Springer.
- Debelak, R., Strobl, C., & Zeigenfuse, M. (2022). *An introduction to the Rasch model with Examples in R*. Boca Raton, FL: CRC Press.
- Eid, M., & Schmidt, K. (2014). *Testtheorie und Testkonstruktion*. Göttingen: Hogrefe.
- Embretson, S. E., & Reise, S. P. (2000). *Item response theory for psychologists*. Mahwah, NJ: Lawrence Erlbaum.
- Raykov, T., & Marcoulides, G. A. (2011). *Introduction to psychometric theory*. New York: Routledge.

**Advanced Topics (Item Response Theory & Psychometrics):**

- **Item Response Theory (IRT):** Covers probabilistic test models for binary and ordinal responses, and extensions to mixture and multidimensional IRT models.
  - *Literature:* Böckenholt, U., & Meiser, T. (2017). Response style analysis with threshold and multi-process IRT models: A review and tutorial. *British Journal of Mathematical and Statistical Psychology*, 70, 159–181.
  - *Literature:* De Boeck, P., & Wilson, M. (2004). *Explanatory item response models*. New York: Springer.
  - *Literature:* Chalmers, R. P. (2012). mirt: A multidimensional item response theory package for the R environment. *Journal of Statistical Software*, 48(6), 1–29.
  - *Literature:* Embretson, S. E., & Reise, S. P. (2000). *Item response theory for psychologists*. Mahwah, NJ: Lawrence Erlbaum.
  - *Literature:* Rizopoulos, D. (2006). ltm: An R package for latent variable modeling and item response analysis. *Journal of Statistical Software*, 17(5), 1–25.

**Bayesian Statistics:**

- Overview and history of Bayesian statistics.
- Key differences between Bayesian and frequentist approaches.
- Core concepts: prior, likelihood, posterior, and Bayes factor.
- *Practical Modules:* Includes walkthroughs for Bayesian t-tests, ANOVA, correlation, and regression analyses.
- *Advanced Topics:* Model comparison (Bayes factors, model averaging), sensitivity analysis, and exploring hierarchical models in JASP.

### Communication & Methodology Seminars

- **Applied Psychology/Technology:**
  - Development of action-oriented psychological technologies for solving practical problems.
  - Stakeholder-oriented communication (conveying one's own ideas to practitioners).
- **Research Writing (In-Mind Style):**
  - Students write a popular science article based on the seminar topic, adhering to the formal guidelines of *In-Mind* ([https://de.in-mind.org/content/beitrage-einreichen](https://de.in-mind.org/content/beitrage-einreichen)).
  - The article should detail and discuss 1–2 empirical studies from psychology, placing them in the broader context of psychological research.
  - Submissions can be group or individual work.
- **Communication Skills Seminar:**
  - The seminar dedicates one session to popular science communication.
  - Students present a popular science contribution (approx. 30 minutes) in each session, focusing on specific aspects of communicating popular science.
  - The final assessment requires writing the popular science article.
  - A guideline for popular science communication is developed throughout the seminar, leading to an assessment rubric.
